⚡ 1. What is Alternating Current?

Alternating current, or AC, is an electric current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time.

AC is widely used for the transmission and distribution of electrical energy.

🔋 2. Direct Current and Alternating Current

Direct current (DC) flows in one direction and generally has a constant direction.

Alternating current (AC) periodically changes its direction and magnitude.

  • Battery is a common source of DC.
  • Domestic electrical supply is commonly AC.

〰️ 3. AC Waveform

Alternating voltage and current are commonly represented by sinusoidal waves.

V = V₀ sin(ωt)

Where V₀ is the maximum voltage, ω is angular frequency and t is time.

🔄 4. Time Period and Frequency

The time period (T) is the time required for one complete cycle of an alternating current.

Frequency (f) is the number of complete cycles per second.

f = 1 / T

The SI unit of frequency is hertz (Hz).

🔄 5. Angular Frequency

Angular frequency represents how rapidly the phase of an alternating quantity changes.

ω = 2πf

Its SI unit is radians per second (rad/s).

📊 6. RMS Value

The root mean square, or RMS, value of an alternating current is the effective value that produces the same heating effect as an equivalent DC current.

Vrms = V₀ / √2
Irms = I₀ / √2

Here V₀ and I₀ are the maximum values of voltage and current.

⚙️ 7. AC in Resistor

In a purely resistive AC circuit, voltage and current are in phase.

V = IR

The resistor opposes the flow of current through its resistance.

🌀 8. AC in Inductor

An inductor opposes changes in current by producing an induced emf.

In a purely inductive circuit, current lags behind voltage by 90°.

🔋 9. AC in Capacitor

A capacitor stores electrical energy in an electric field.

In a purely capacitive circuit, current leads voltage by 90°.

🏠 10. Domestic AC Supply

Alternating current is used in homes because it can be generated and transmitted efficiently over long distances.

Transformers can increase or decrease AC voltage according to the requirements of electrical power systems.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. Alternating current periodically changes its:

A) Mass only

B) Direction and magnitude

C) Temperature only

D) Resistance only

Answer: B) Direction and magnitude

2. The SI unit of frequency is:

A) Volt

B) Ampere

C) Hertz

D) Ohm

Answer: C) Hertz

3. The relationship between frequency and time period is:

A) f = T

B) f = 1/T

C) f = T²

D) f = 2T

Answer: B) f = 1/T

4. In a purely resistive AC circuit, voltage and current are:

A) 90° apart

B) In phase

C) 180° apart

D) Unrelated

Answer: B) In phase

5. In a purely inductive circuit, current:

A) Leads voltage by 90°

B) Lags voltage by 90°

C) Is always zero

D) Is equal to voltage

Answer: B) Lags voltage by 90°

⚡ Quick Revision

  • AC periodically changes direction and magnitude.
  • Frequency: f = 1/T.
  • Angular frequency: ω = 2πf.
  • RMS voltage: Vrms = V₀/√2.
  • RMS current: Irms = I₀/√2.
  • In a resistor, voltage and current are in phase.
  • In an inductor, current lags voltage by 90°.
  • In a capacitor, current leads voltage by 90°.
  • AC is widely used for electrical power transmission.
