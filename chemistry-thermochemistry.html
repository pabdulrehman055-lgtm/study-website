Unit 8: Thermochemistry and Energetics of Chemical Reaction

Definition: Thermochemistry is the study of heat changes and energy changes associated with chemical reactions and physical processes.

Chemical reactions involve energy changes. Energy may be released to the surroundings or absorbed from the surroundings.

MDCAT Focus: Focus on exothermic and endothermic reactions, system and surroundings, heat, work, internal energy, enthalpy, first law of thermodynamics and Hess's law.

1. Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics is the study of energy transformations and the relationship between heat and work.

In chemistry, thermodynamics helps us understand how energy is transferred during chemical reactions.

Thermochemistry mainly focuses on the heat changes associated with chemical reactions.

2. System, Surroundings and Boundary

System

The part of the universe that we are studying is called the system.

Surroundings

Everything outside the system is called the surroundings.

Boundary

The boundary separates the system from its surroundings.

Example:
If a reaction is taking place inside a beaker, the reaction mixture can be considered the system, while the beaker and surrounding environment are part of the surroundings.

3. State of a System

The condition of a system can be described by properties such as temperature, pressure, volume and composition.

A state function is a property whose value depends only on the current state of the system, not on the path used to reach that state.

Examples of state functions include:

  • Temperature
  • Pressure
  • Volume
  • Internal energy
  • Enthalpy

4. Heat

Heat is energy transferred between a system and its surroundings because of a temperature difference.

Heat naturally flows from a hotter region to a colder region.

Heat is energy in transfer. It is not considered a property stored in a system in the same way as internal energy.

5. Heat Capacity

Heat capacity is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a substance or system by a specified amount.
q = mcΔT

q = heat absorbed or released
m = mass
c = specific heat capacity
ΔT = change in temperature
Example:
If the mass of a substance is 10 g, its specific heat capacity is 2 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ and its temperature increases by 5°C:

q = 10 × 2 × 5
q = 100 J

6. Exothermic Reactions

An exothermic reaction releases heat energy to the surroundings.

Because heat is released, the surroundings may become warmer.

Exothermic reaction:

Reactants → Products + Heat

The enthalpy change for an exothermic reaction is generally negative.

Examples:
  • Combustion of fuels
  • Many neutralization reactions
  • Respiration

7. Endothermic Reactions

An endothermic reaction absorbs heat energy from the surroundings.

The surroundings may become cooler as energy is absorbed by the system.

Endothermic reaction:

Reactants + Heat → Products

The enthalpy change for an endothermic reaction is generally positive.

Example:
Thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate requires heat:

CaCO3 → CaO + CO2

8. Exothermic vs Endothermic

Exothermic Endothermic
Releases heat Absorbs heat
Surroundings may become warmer Surroundings may become cooler
ΔH is generally negative ΔH is generally positive
Heat appears on product side Heat appears on reactant side

9. Internal Energy

Internal energy (U) is the total energy contained within a system due to the motions and interactions of its particles.

A change in internal energy is represented by:

ΔU = Ufinal − Uinitial

Internal energy is a state function.

10. Work

Energy can be transferred between a system and surroundings in the form of heat or work.

In chemistry, work may occur when a gas expands or is compressed against an external pressure.

Expansion work:

w = −PΔV

Here P is external pressure and ΔV is the change in volume.

Under the common chemistry sign convention, expansion generally gives negative work for the system, while compression gives positive work.

11. First Law of Thermodynamics

The first law of thermodynamics is based on the law of conservation of energy.

Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred or transformed.

ΔU = q + w

Where:

  • ΔU = change in internal energy
  • q = heat transferred to the system
  • w = work done on the system
Example:
If a system absorbs 500 J of heat and 100 J of work is done on the system:

ΔU = q + w
ΔU = 500 + 100
ΔU = 600 J

12. Enthalpy

Enthalpy (H) is a thermodynamic quantity related to the heat content of a system at constant pressure.

The change in enthalpy is represented by ΔH.

ΔH = Hproducts − Hreactants

At constant pressure, the enthalpy change is related to the heat absorbed or released by the system.

13. Enthalpy Change

Enthalpy change tells us about the energy difference between reactants and products.

Exothermic: ΔH < 0
Endothermic: ΔH > 0

A negative ΔH means energy is released, while a positive ΔH means energy is absorbed.

14. Hess's Law

Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change for a reaction is the same whether the reaction occurs in one step or several steps.

This is possible because enthalpy is a state function.

Overall ΔH = Sum of ΔH values of individual steps
Simple Example:

Reaction 1: A → B    ΔH₁
Reaction 2: B → C    ΔH₂

Overall: A → C
ΔH = ΔH₁ + ΔH₂

15. Using Hess's Law

When using Hess's law, remember these rules:

  • If a reaction is reversed, the sign of ΔH changes.
  • If a reaction is multiplied by a number, ΔH is also multiplied by that number.
  • When reactions are added, their enthalpy changes are added.
Exam Trick: Reversing an equation changes the sign of ΔH. Multiplying an equation also multiplies its ΔH.

16. Common Enthalpy Changes

Type Meaning
Enthalpy of combustion Heat change when one mole of a substance undergoes complete combustion under specified conditions.
Enthalpy of formation Enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states.
Enthalpy of neutralization Heat change associated with neutralization of an acid and a base.

17. Energy Profile

An energy profile shows the energy changes that occur during a chemical reaction.

Exothermic Reaction

Products have lower energy than reactants, so energy is released.

Endothermic Reaction

Products have higher energy than reactants, so energy is absorbed.

Activation energy represents the energy barrier that must be overcome for the reaction to proceed.

18. Quick Revision

  • Thermochemistry deals with heat changes in chemical reactions.
  • The system is the part being studied.
  • Everything outside the system is the surroundings.
  • Heat is energy transferred because of temperature difference.
  • Exothermic reactions release heat.
  • Endothermic reactions absorb heat.
  • Exothermic reactions generally have negative ΔH.
  • Endothermic reactions generally have positive ΔH.
  • Internal energy is represented by U.
  • First law: ΔU = q + w.
  • Enthalpy is represented by H.
  • At constant pressure, ΔH is related to heat transfer.
  • Hess's law allows enthalpy changes of multiple steps to be added.
  • Reversing a reaction changes the sign of ΔH.
  • Multiplying a reaction also multiplies its ΔH.

19. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Thermochemistry mainly deals with:

A. Atomic numbers
B. Heat changes in chemical reactions
C. Nuclear reactions
D. Electron configuration

Answer: B. Heat changes in chemical reactions

2. A reaction that releases heat is called:

A. Endothermic
B. Exothermic
C. Reversible
D. Neutral

Answer: B. Exothermic

3. In an endothermic reaction, heat is:

A. Released
B. Absorbed
C. Destroyed
D. Unchanged

Answer: B. Absorbed

4. The enthalpy change of an exothermic reaction is generally:

A. Positive
B. Negative
C. Zero always
D. Infinite

Answer: B. Negative

5. The enthalpy change of an endothermic reaction is generally:

A. Negative
B. Positive
C. Always zero
D. Always infinite

Answer: B. Positive

6. The first law of thermodynamics is based on:

A. Conservation of mass only
B. Conservation of energy
C. Boyle's law
D. Charles's law

Answer: B. Conservation of energy

7. The correct equation for the first law is:

A. ΔU = q + w
B. ΔU = q − w
C. ΔU = qw
D. ΔU = q/w

Answer: A. ΔU = q + w

8. Which of the following is a state function?

A. Enthalpy
B. Path taken
C. Distance travelled
D. Process duration

Answer: A. Enthalpy

9. Hess's law is possible because enthalpy is:

A. A catalyst
B. A state function
C. A reactant
D. A gas

Answer: B. A state function

10. When a thermochemical equation is reversed, ΔH:

A. Remains unchanged
B. Changes sign
C. Becomes zero
D. Doubles automatically

Answer: B. Changes sign

11. If a thermochemical equation is multiplied by 2, its ΔH is:

A. Divided by 2
B. Multiplied by 2
C. Unchanged
D. Made zero

Answer: B. Multiplied by 2

12. The formula q = mcΔT is related to:

A. Heat transfer
B. Atomic number
C. Reaction order
D. Gas pressure only

Answer: A. Heat transfer

13. In an exothermic reaction, the products generally have:

A. Higher energy than reactants
B. Lower energy than reactants
C. Exactly the same energy always
D. No energy

Answer: B. Lower energy than reactants

14. The part of the universe being studied is called:

A. Surroundings
B. System
C. Boundary
D. Environment

Answer: B. System

15. Which law states that total enthalpy change is independent of the path?

A. Boyle's law
B. Hess's law
C. Charles's law
D. Graham's law

Answer: B. Hess's law

20. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember: Exothermic = heat released = ΔH negative.
  • Remember: Endothermic = heat absorbed = ΔH positive.
  • Learn the first law equation: ΔU = q + w.
  • Understand the difference between system and surroundings.
  • Memorize the heat equation: q = mcΔT.
  • Hess's law is based on enthalpy being a state function.
  • If a reaction is reversed, change the sign of ΔH.
  • If an equation is multiplied, multiply ΔH by the same number.
  • Practice numerical questions involving q = mcΔT.
  • Understand energy diagrams instead of memorizing them blindly.
