Unit 19: Macromolecules

Definition: Macromolecules are very large molecules formed from smaller molecular units. Important biological macromolecules include proteins, carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic acids.

In this MDCAT unit, special attention is given to proteins, their structure and functions, nutritional importance, and enzymes as biological catalysts.

1. Proteins

Proteins are complex biological macromolecules made mainly from amino acids joined together by peptide bonds.

Amino acids → Peptide chains → Proteins

Proteins contain mainly carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen. Some proteins may also contain sulfur and other elements.

MDCAT Point: Amino acids are the basic building blocks of proteins.

2. Amino Acids

An amino acid generally contains an amino group (–NH₂), a carboxyl group (–COOH), a hydrogen atom and a variable side chain attached to a central carbon atom.

General structure: NH₂–CH(R)–COOH

The variable group R determines the properties of an amino acid.

Example:
Glycine is the simplest amino acid and has R = H.

3. Peptide Bond

Amino acids join together through a condensation reaction. The bond formed between the amino group of one amino acid and the carboxyl group of another amino acid is called a peptide bond.

–COOH + –NH₂ → –CO–NH– + H₂O

The characteristic peptide linkage is:

–CO–NH–
Remember: Peptide bond formation involves the removal of water.

4. Classification of Proteins

Proteins can be classified according to their shape and composition.

Fibrous Proteins

Fibrous proteins generally have long, thread-like structures and often provide structural support.

Examples: Keratin and collagen.

Globular Proteins

Globular proteins have compact, roughly spherical structures and perform many functional roles.

Examples: Enzymes and many transport proteins.
Type General Characteristic Example
Fibrous Long and structural Keratin, Collagen
Globular Compact and functional Many enzymes

5. Structure of Proteins

Protein structure can be described at different levels.

Primary Structure

The primary structure is the specific sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.

Secondary Structure

Secondary structure includes arrangements such as α-helix and β-pleated sheet.

Tertiary Structure

Tertiary structure is the overall three-dimensional folding of a single polypeptide chain.

Quaternary Structure

Quaternary structure occurs when two or more polypeptide chains associate to form a functional protein.

Level Main Idea
Primary Amino acid sequence
Secondary α-helix and β-sheet
Tertiary 3D folding of one polypeptide
Quaternary Association of multiple polypeptide chains

6. Functions of Proteins

Proteins perform many important functions in living organisms.

  • Structural: provide support and strength.
  • Enzymatic: catalyze biochemical reactions.
  • Transport: help transport substances.
  • Defensive: antibodies help defend the body.
  • Hormonal: some hormones are proteins or peptides.
  • Contractile: some proteins are involved in movement.
Exam Tip: Do not associate proteins only with muscles. Proteins have structural, enzymatic, transport, defensive and regulatory roles.

7. Nutritional Importance of Proteins

Proteins are essential nutrients required for growth, development and repair of body tissues.

  • Growth and maintenance of tissues.
  • Repair of damaged tissues.
  • Production of enzymes.
  • Formation of antibodies and other body proteins.
  • Supply of amino acids required by the body.
Important: Proteins are made from amino acids, and amino acids are required for the synthesis of many important molecules in the body.

8. Essential and Non-Essential Amino Acids

Amino acids can be described according to whether the human body can synthesize them in sufficient amounts.

Essential Amino Acids

These must be obtained from the diet because the body cannot synthesize enough of them.

Non-Essential Amino Acids

These can generally be synthesized by the body.

MDCAT Point: Essential amino acids must be supplied through the diet.

9. Enzymes as Biocatalysts

Definition: Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of biochemical reactions without being permanently consumed.

Most enzymes are proteins. They have specific structures that allow them to interact with particular substrates.

Enzyme + Substrate ⇌ Enzyme–Substrate Complex → Products

Active Site

The region of an enzyme where the substrate binds and the reaction occurs is called the active site.

Substrate Specificity

An enzyme generally acts on a particular substrate or a group of closely related substrates.

10. Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

Temperature

Increasing temperature can increase enzyme activity up to an optimum point. Very high temperatures can denature many enzymes.

pH

Each enzyme generally has an optimum pH at which its activity is highest.

Substrate Concentration

Increasing substrate concentration can increase reaction rate until the available active sites become occupied.

Remember: Extreme temperature or unsuitable pH can change protein structure and reduce enzyme activity.

11. Denaturation of Proteins

Denaturation is the alteration of the natural three-dimensional structure of a protein, often causing loss of its biological activity.

Heat, extreme pH and certain chemicals can cause denaturation.

Example: Heating egg white causes its proteins to denature and change their physical appearance.

Quick Revision

  • Proteins are biological macromolecules.
  • Amino acids are the building blocks of proteins.
  • General amino acid structure = NH₂–CH(R)–COOH.
  • Peptide bond = –CO–NH–.
  • Protein structures include primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary.
  • Fibrous proteins are generally structural.
  • Globular proteins are generally compact and functional.
  • Proteins are important for growth and tissue repair.
  • Essential amino acids must be obtained from the diet.
  • Enzymes are biological catalysts.
  • The substrate binds at the enzyme's active site.
  • Temperature and pH affect enzyme activity.
  • Extreme conditions can denature proteins.

15 MDCAT MCQs

1. The basic building blocks of proteins are:

A) Fatty acids

B) Amino acids

C) Glucose

D) Nucleotides

Answer: B) Amino acids
2. The functional group present in all amino acids is:

A) Only –OH

B) Amino and carboxyl groups

C) Only –CHO

D) Only –CO–

Answer: B) Amino and carboxyl groups
3. The general structure of an α-amino acid is:

A) R–OH

B) NH₂–CH(R)–COOH

C) R–CHO

D) R–COOR

Answer: B) NH₂–CH(R)–COOH
4. The bond joining amino acids in proteins is called:

A) Ionic bond

B) Hydrogen bond

C) Peptide bond

D) Metallic bond

Answer: C) Peptide bond
5. The peptide linkage is:

A) –O–O–

B) –CO–NH–

C) –CO–O–

D) –NH–NH–

Answer: B) –CO–NH–
6. Which is an example of a fibrous protein?

A) Keratin

B) Glucose

C) Cholesterol

D) DNA

Answer: A) Keratin
7. The primary structure of a protein refers to:

A) Its 3D shape

B) Its amino acid sequence

C) Its molecular mass only

D) Its solubility

Answer: B) Its amino acid sequence
8. α-helix is associated with which level of protein structure?

A) Primary

B) Secondary

C) Tertiary

D) Quaternary

Answer: B) Secondary
9. Enzymes are biological:

A) Fuels

B) Catalysts

C) Solvents

D) Salts

Answer: B) Catalysts
10. The substrate binds to an enzyme at its:

A) Nucleus

B) Active site

C) Cell wall

D) Peptide bond

Answer: B) Active site
11. Which factor can strongly affect enzyme activity?

A) Temperature

B) pH

C) Substrate concentration

D) All of these

Answer: D) All of these
12. Essential amino acids are amino acids that:

A) Are never used by the body

B) Must generally be obtained from the diet

C) Are only found in plants

D) Cannot form proteins

Answer: B) Must generally be obtained from the diet
13. Very high temperature can reduce enzyme activity because it may:

A) Increase water formation

B) Denature the enzyme

C) Produce glucose

D) Form amino acids

Answer: B) Denature the enzyme
14. Which protein structure describes the association of multiple polypeptide chains?

A) Primary

B) Secondary

C) Tertiary

D) Quaternary

Answer: D) Quaternary
15. Which of the following is an important function of proteins?

A) Tissue growth and repair

B) Only water storage

C) Only oxygen production

D) Only DNA replication

Answer: A) Tissue growth and repair

Exam Tips 🎯

  • Remember that amino acids are protein building blocks.
  • Memorize the peptide bond: –CO–NH–.
  • Know the four levels of protein structure.
  • Remember the difference between fibrous and globular proteins.
  • Know the nutritional importance of proteins.
  • Remember that enzymes are biological catalysts.
  • Learn the meaning of active site and substrate specificity.
  • Temperature and pH are important factors affecting enzyme activity.
  • Extreme conditions can cause protein denaturation.
