Biology Chapter 1: Cell Biology

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.

MDCAT Important: Cell theory is one of the most important concepts in Cell Biology.

1. Cell Theory

  • All living organisms are composed of cells.
  • The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

The cell theory was developed through the work of scientists including Matthias Schleiden, Theodor Schwann and Rudolf Virchow.

2. Types of Cells

Prokaryotic Cells

  • Usually smaller and simpler cells.
  • Do not have a true membrane-bound nucleus.
  • DNA is present in the nucleoid region.
  • Membrane-bound organelles are absent.
  • Bacteria are examples of prokaryotes.

Eukaryotic Cells

  • Generally larger and more complex.
  • Have a true nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane.
  • Contain membrane-bound organelles.
  • Plants, animals, fungi and protists have eukaryotic cells.
Feature Prokaryotic Eukaryotic
True nucleus Absent Present
Membrane-bound organelles Absent Present
DNA location Nucleoid Nucleus
Examples Bacteria Plants and animals

3. Cell Membrane

The cell membrane surrounds the cell and separates the internal environment from the external environment.

  • It is mainly composed of phospholipids and proteins.
  • It is selectively permeable.
  • It controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  • It helps maintain the internal environment of the cell.
Remember: The cell membrane is selectively permeable, meaning it allows certain substances to pass more easily than others.

4. Nucleus

The nucleus is a major control center of the eukaryotic cell.

  • Contains genetic material (DNA).
  • Controls many cellular activities.
  • Surrounded by a nuclear envelope.
  • Contains the nucleolus.

Nucleolus

The nucleolus is involved in the production of ribosomal components.

5. Mitochondria

Mitochondria are organelles involved in cellular respiration and ATP production.

  • Have a double membrane.
  • The inner membrane forms folds called cristae.
  • The internal fluid-filled region is called the matrix.
  • Contain their own DNA and ribosomes.
  • ATP is produced during cellular respiration.
MDCAT Tip: Mitochondria are often called the "powerhouses" of the cell because they are major sites of ATP production.

6. Ribosomes

  • Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis.
  • They are not surrounded by a membrane.
  • They may be free in the cytoplasm.
  • They may also be attached to rough endoplasmic reticulum.

7. Endoplasmic Reticulum

Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER)

Rough ER has ribosomes attached to its surface and is involved mainly in the synthesis and transport of proteins.

Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER)

Smooth ER lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and other cellular functions.

8. Golgi Apparatus

The Golgi apparatus consists of flattened membrane-bound sacs.

  • Modifies proteins and lipids.
  • Sorts and packages cellular products.
  • Helps form secretory vesicles.
  • Participates in the formation of lysosomes.

9. Lysosomes

Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that can break down various materials inside cells.

  • Contain hydrolytic digestive enzymes.
  • Help digest damaged cellular components.
  • Can break down certain foreign materials.
Remember: Lysosomes are associated with intracellular digestion.

10. Vacuoles

Vacuoles are membrane-bound compartments used for storage and other functions.

  • Plant cells usually have a large central vacuole.
  • They can store water, ions, nutrients and waste materials.
  • The central vacuole contributes to maintaining plant cell pressure.

11. Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are found in plants and other photosynthetic organisms. They are the main sites of photosynthesis in plant cells.

  • Have a double membrane.
  • Contain chlorophyll and other pigments.
  • Contain internal structures called thylakoids.
  • Stacks of thylakoids are called grana.
  • The surrounding fluid is called stroma.
  • Contain their own DNA and ribosomes.

12. Cell Wall

Plant cells have a cell wall outside the cell membrane.

  • Provides support and protection.
  • Helps maintain cell shape.
  • Plant cell walls are mainly composed of cellulose.

13. Cytoplasm

Cytoplasm includes the cytosol and the structures suspended within it, excluding the nucleus in a typical eukaryotic cell.

Many metabolic reactions occur in the cytoplasm.

14. Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell wall Present Absent
Chloroplast Present in photosynthetic plant cells Absent
Large central vacuole Usually present Usually absent

16. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Which organelle is the major site of ATP production?

A) Ribosome

B) Golgi apparatus

C) Mitochondrion

D) Lysosome

Answer: C) Mitochondrion

2. Which structure controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell?

A) Cell wall

B) Cell membrane

C) Nucleolus

D) Ribosome

Answer: B) Cell membrane

3. Ribosomes are primarily responsible for:

A) Photosynthesis

B) Protein synthesis

C) ATP storage

D) DNA digestion

Answer: B) Protein synthesis

4. Which structure is characteristic of plant cells?

A) Cell wall

B) Ribosome

C) Cell membrane

D) Cytoplasm

Answer: A) Cell wall

5. The site of photosynthesis in plant cells is the:

A) Lysosome

B) Chloroplast

C) Golgi apparatus

D) Nucleus

Answer: B) Chloroplast

6. Which type of cell lacks a true nucleus?

A) Plant cell

B) Animal cell

C) Prokaryotic cell

D) Fungal cell

Answer: C) Prokaryotic cell

7. Which organelle modifies and packages proteins?

A) Golgi apparatus

B) Ribosome

C) Nucleus

D) Chloroplast

Answer: A) Golgi apparatus

8. The folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane are called:

A) Grana

B) Cristae

C) Thylakoids

D) Cisternae

Answer: B) Cristae

9. The main component of a plant cell wall is:

A) Glycogen

B) Cellulose

C) Cholesterol

D) Protein

Answer: B) Cellulose

10. Which organelle contains digestive enzymes?

A) Lysosome

B) Ribosome

C) Chloroplast

D) Nucleolus

Answer: A) Lysosome

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tip

Learn the function of every major organelle. MDCAT questions often test the relationship between an organelle and its function.

Most important: Nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, ER, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, chloroplasts, vacuoles, cell membrane and cell wall.
