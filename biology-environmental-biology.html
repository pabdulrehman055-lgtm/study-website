Chapter 19: Environmental Biology

Learn about ecosystems, populations, communities, food chains, energy flow, nutrient cycles, pollution and environmental balance.

1. What is Environmental Biology?

Environmental biology is the study of relationships between living organisms and their physical and biological surroundings.

It helps us understand how organisms interact with one another and with the environment.

Important: The environment includes both living and non-living components.

2. Biotic and Abiotic Factors

Biotic Factors

Biotic factors are the living components of an ecosystem.

  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Fungi
  • Bacteria
  • Other microorganisms

Abiotic Factors

Abiotic factors are the non-living components of the environment.

  • Temperature
  • Light
  • Water
  • Air
  • Soil
  • Minerals
  • pH

3. Levels of Ecological Organization

Ecological organization can be studied at different levels:

  • Organism
  • Population
  • Community
  • Ecosystem
  • Biome
  • Biosphere
Remember: Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere

4. Population

A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in a particular area at a particular time.

Population Density

Population density refers to the number of individuals of a species present per unit area or volume.

5. Community

A community consists of populations of different species living and interacting in the same area.

For example, plants, insects, birds and microorganisms living together in a forest form part of a biological community.

6. Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of living organisms interacting with each other and with the non-living components of their environment.

Main Components

  • Producers
  • Consumers
  • Decomposers
  • Abiotic components

7. Producers

Producers are organisms that make their own food, usually through photosynthesis.

Green plants and algae are important producers in many ecosystems.

MDCAT Point: Producers form the first trophic level in most food chains.

8. Consumers

Consumers obtain energy by feeding on other organisms.

Primary Consumers

Primary consumers usually feed directly on producers. Herbivores are common examples.

Secondary Consumers

Secondary consumers generally feed on primary consumers.

Tertiary Consumers

Tertiary consumers may feed on secondary consumers and can occupy higher trophic levels.

9. Decomposers

Decomposers break down dead organic matter and release nutrients back into the environment.

Bacteria and fungi are important decomposers.

Remember: Decomposers help recycle nutrients in ecosystems.

10. Food Chain

A food chain shows the transfer of food and energy from one organism to another.

Example:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk

The arrows indicate the direction of energy transfer.

11. Food Web

A food web consists of interconnected food chains within an ecosystem.

Food webs are generally more complex and provide multiple feeding relationships between organisms.

12. Trophic Levels

Each step in a food chain is called a trophic level.

  • First trophic level → Producers
  • Second trophic level → Primary consumers
  • Third trophic level → Secondary consumers
  • Fourth trophic level → Tertiary consumers

13. Energy Flow

Energy enters most ecosystems through sunlight. Producers convert light energy into chemical energy through photosynthesis.

Energy then passes from producers to consumers and eventually to decomposers.

Important: Energy flow through an ecosystem is one-way, while nutrients can be recycled.

14. Ecological Pyramids

Ecological pyramids represent relationships between trophic levels.

Pyramid of Numbers

Shows the number of organisms at different trophic levels.

Pyramid of Biomass

Shows the total biomass present at each trophic level.

Pyramid of Energy

Shows the amount of energy available at each trophic level.

15. Nutrient Cycles

Matter is continuously recycled between organisms and the environment.

Water Cycle

Water moves through processes such as evaporation, condensation, precipitation and runoff.

Carbon Cycle

Carbon moves between the atmosphere, organisms, soil and oceans. Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, while respiration returns carbon dioxide.

Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen is essential for proteins and nucleic acids. Microorganisms play important roles in converting nitrogen into forms that organisms can use.

16. Population Growth

Population size can change because of:

  • Births
  • Deaths
  • Immigration
  • Emigration

Carrying Capacity

Carrying capacity is the maximum population size that an environment can support for a particular period under given conditions.

17. Pollution

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or forms of energy into the environment.

Air Pollution

Air pollution may result from vehicle emissions, industrial gases and burning of fuels.

Water Pollution

Water pollution can result from sewage, industrial waste, agricultural chemicals and other contaminants.

Soil Pollution

Soil may be polluted by pesticides, industrial waste and improper disposal of chemicals.

18. Global Warming

Global warming refers to the long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature.

Increased concentrations of greenhouse gases can enhance the greenhouse effect and contribute to global warming.

Important Greenhouse Gases

  • Carbon dioxide
  • Methane
  • Nitrous oxide
  • Water vapour

19. Conservation of Biodiversity

Biodiversity conservation aims to protect species, genetic diversity and ecosystems.

Methods

  • Protection of natural habitats
  • National parks and protected areas
  • Control of deforestation
  • Protection of endangered species
  • Sustainable use of natural resources

20. Quick Revision

  • Biotic → Living components.
  • Abiotic → Non-living components.
  • Population → Same species in an area.
  • Community → Different populations interacting.
  • Ecosystem → Biotic + abiotic interactions.
  • Producers → Make their own food.
  • Consumers → Obtain food from other organisms.
  • Decomposers → Break down dead matter.
  • Food chain → Linear feeding relationship.
  • Food web → Interconnected food chains.
  • Energy → Flows in one direction.
  • Nutrients → Recycled through ecosystems.
  • Carrying capacity → Maximum sustainable population size.

21. MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Which is an abiotic factor?

A) Bacteria

B) Plants

C) Temperature

D) Animals

Answer: C) Temperature

2. A group of individuals of the same species living in an area is called:

A) Community

B) Population

C) Ecosystem

D) Biome

Answer: B) Population

3. Which organisms are usually producers?

A) Green plants

B) Carnivores

C) Fungi

D) Humans

Answer: A) Green plants

4. Which organisms are important decomposers?

A) Birds and mammals

B) Bacteria and fungi

C) Fish and reptiles

D) Plants only

Answer: B) Bacteria and fungi

5. The first trophic level consists mainly of:

A) Producers

B) Herbivores

C) Carnivores

D) Decomposers

Answer: A) Producers

6. Which represents a food chain?

A) Grass → Grasshopper → Frog

B) Frog → Grass → Hawk

C) Hawk → Grass → Frog

D) Grasshopper → Grass → Frog

Answer: A) Grass → Grasshopper → Frog

7. Energy flow in an ecosystem is generally:

A) Circular

B) One-way

C) Random

D) Reversible

Answer: B) One-way

8. Which process removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere?

A) Photosynthesis

B) Respiration

C) Combustion

D) Decomposition

Answer: A) Photosynthesis

9. Carrying capacity refers to:

A) Number of species in the world

B) Maximum population an environment can support

C) Number of predators only

D) Total amount of sunlight

Answer: B) Maximum population an environment can support

10. Which gas is a major greenhouse gas?

A) Carbon dioxide

B) Helium

C) Neon

D) Argon

Answer: A) Carbon dioxide

11. A food web consists of:

A) One organism

B) Interconnected food chains

C) Only producers

D) Only decomposers

Answer: B) Interconnected food chains

12. Primary consumers usually feed on:

A) Producers

B) Secondary consumers

C) Decomposers

D) Tertiary consumers

Answer: A) Producers

13. Which cycle involves evaporation and precipitation?

A) Carbon cycle

B) Nitrogen cycle

C) Water cycle

D) Phosphorus cycle

Answer: C) Water cycle

14. Which is an example of water pollution?

A) Sewage entering a river

B) Plant photosynthesis

C) Bird migration

D) Seed germination

Answer: A) Sewage entering a river

15. The main purpose of biodiversity conservation is to:

A) Destroy habitats

B) Protect species and ecosystems

C) Increase pollution

D) Remove producers

Answer: B) Protect species and ecosystems

22. Exam Tips 📝

  • Memorize the sequence of ecological organization.
  • Understand the difference between biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Know the roles of producers, consumers and decomposers.
  • Practice food-chain and trophic-level questions.
  • Remember that energy flow is one-way.
  • Understand the basic carbon, nitrogen and water cycles.
  • Revise pollution and conservation concepts.
Quick Trick: Producer = makes food, Consumer = eats, Decomposer = breaks down, Food chain = energy transfer, Ecosystem = living + non-living interactions.
