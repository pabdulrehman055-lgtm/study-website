1. Dawn of Modern Physics

Modern physics developed when scientists discovered that some phenomena could not be explained completely by classical physics.

Important ideas include the quantum nature of energy, photons, the photoelectric effect, relativity and matter waves.

2. Quantum Theory

According to Planck's quantum theory, energy is emitted or absorbed in discrete packets called quanta.

The energy of a quantum is directly related to the frequency of radiation.

E = hf

Here, E is energy, h is Planck's constant and f is frequency.

3. Photon

A photon is a quantum of electromagnetic radiation.

The energy of a photon increases when the frequency of radiation increases.

E = hf

The momentum of a photon is related to its energy and wavelength.

p = h / λ

4. Photoelectric Effect

The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of suitable frequency falls on the surface.

The emitted electrons are called photoelectrons.

The minimum frequency required to produce photoelectric emission is called the threshold frequency.

5. Work Function

The minimum energy required to remove an electron from the surface of a metal is called its work function.

φ = hf₀

Here, φ is the work function and f₀ is the threshold frequency.

6. Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

Einstein explained the photoelectric effect by treating light as a stream of photons.

hf = φ + K.E.max

Therefore, the energy of an incoming photon is used partly to overcome the work function and the remaining energy appears as the kinetic energy of the emitted electron.

7. Special Theory of Relativity

Einstein's theory of special relativity describes the behavior of space and time for observers moving relative to one another.

A famous result of relativity is the equivalence of mass and energy.

E = mc²

Here, E is energy, m is mass and c is the speed of light in vacuum.

8. Matter Waves

Louis de Broglie proposed that moving particles can have wave-like properties.

The wavelength associated with a moving particle is called the de Broglie wavelength.

λ = h / p

Here, λ is wavelength, h is Planck's constant and p is momentum.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. According to quantum theory, energy is exchanged in:

A) Continuous waves only

B) Discrete packets

C) Empty space

D) Magnetic fields only

Answer: B) Discrete packets

2. The energy of a photon is:

A) E = ma

B) E = hf

C) E = IR

D) E = Pt

Answer: B) E = hf

3. The minimum frequency required for photoelectric emission is called:

A) Critical frequency

B) Threshold frequency

C) Angular frequency

D) Resonant frequency

Answer: B) Threshold frequency

4. Einstein's mass-energy relation is:

A) E = hf

B) E = mc²

C) F = ma

D) V = IR

Answer: B) E = mc²

5. The de Broglie wavelength is given by:

A) λ = p/h

B) λ = h/p

C) λ = hf

D) λ = mc²

Answer: B) λ = h/p

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Modern physics explains phenomena beyond classical physics.
  • Energy is exchanged in discrete packets called quanta.
  • Photon energy: E = hf.
  • Photoelectric effect produces photoelectrons.
  • Threshold frequency is the minimum frequency for emission.
  • Work function is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron.
  • Einstein's equation: E = mc².
  • Moving particles can show wave-like behavior.
  • de Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p.
