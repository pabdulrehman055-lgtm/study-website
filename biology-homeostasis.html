💧 Homeostasis

Definition: Homeostasis is the maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment in the body despite changes in the external or internal conditions.
MDCAT Focus: Learn the urinary system, kidney, nephron, urine formation, filtration, reabsorption, secretion, osmoregulation, nitrogenous excretion and thermoregulation.

1. Why is Homeostasis Important?

Cells need suitable conditions to carry out their metabolic reactions. Large changes in temperature, water balance, salt concentration or waste levels can interfere with normal cellular functions.

Examples of Conditions Regulated

  • Body temperature
  • Water balance
  • Ion and salt balance
  • Removal of metabolic wastes
  • Blood glucose concentration
Homeostasis does not mean that conditions never change. It means the body regulates them within suitable limits.

2. Urinary System

The urinary system removes nitrogenous wastes and helps regulate water and ion balance.

Main Parts

  • Two kidneys
  • Two ureters
  • Urinary bladder
  • Urethra
Pathway of urine:
Kidney → Ureter → Urinary bladder → Urethra → Outside

3. Kidneys

The kidneys are major organs involved in excretion and maintaining the body's internal environment.

Main Functions

  • Remove nitrogenous wastes from the blood.
  • Regulate water balance.
  • Help regulate ion concentrations.
  • Help maintain acid-base balance.
  • Produce urine.

4. Nephron

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. Each kidney contains a very large number of nephrons.

Main Parts of a Nephron

  • Glomerulus
  • Bowman's capsule
  • Proximal convoluted tubule
  • Loop of Henle
  • Distal convoluted tubule
  • Collecting duct
Exam Point: Nephron = functional unit of the kidney.

5. Glomerulus and Bowman's Capsule

The glomerulus is a network of capillaries located inside Bowman's capsule.

Blood enters the glomerulus under relatively high pressure. Small molecules are filtered from the blood into Bowman's capsule.

Usually Filtered

  • Water
  • Glucose
  • Amino acids
  • Mineral ions
  • Urea

Normally Not Filtered in Significant Amounts

  • Blood cells
  • Large plasma proteins

6. Ultrafiltration

Ultrafiltration is the pressure-driven filtration of small molecules from the blood in the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule.

Blood → Glomerulus → Filtrate → Bowman's Capsule
Large blood cells and most large plasma proteins remain in the bloodstream because they cannot normally pass through the filtration barrier.

7. Selective Reabsorption

After filtration, useful substances are taken back from the kidney tubule into the blood. This is called selective reabsorption.

Substances Reabsorbed

  • Most water
  • Glucose
  • Amino acids
  • Required ions

Reabsorption prevents valuable substances from being lost unnecessarily in urine.

8. Tubular Secretion

Tubular secretion is the movement of certain substances from the blood into the kidney tubule.

It helps the body remove additional wastes and contributes to regulation of ion and acid-base balance.

Three key processes:
Filtration → Reabsorption → Secretion

9. Urine Formation

Urine is formed through the combined processes of filtration, selective reabsorption and secretion.

Process What Happens?
Filtration Small substances move from blood into the nephron.
Reabsorption Useful substances move back into blood.
Secretion Additional substances move from blood into the tubule.

10. Nitrogenous Excretion

The breakdown of proteins and nucleic acids produces nitrogenous wastes.

Important Nitrogenous Wastes

  • Ammonia
  • Urea
  • Uric acid

Urea

In humans, the liver converts toxic ammonia into less toxic urea. Urea is transported in the blood to the kidneys and is removed mainly in urine.

Remember: Humans are primarily ureotelic, meaning urea is the major nitrogenous waste excreted.

11. Osmoregulation

Osmoregulation is the regulation of water and dissolved solute concentrations in the body.

The kidneys play a major role by adjusting how much water and ions are reabsorbed.

When the Body Has Less Water

  • More water is conserved.
  • Urine becomes more concentrated.
  • Urine volume decreases.

When the Body Has Excess Water

  • More water is lost in urine.
  • Urine becomes more dilute.
  • Urine volume increases.

12. Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)

ADH helps regulate the amount of water reabsorbed by the kidneys.

  • More ADH → greater water reabsorption.
  • Less ADH → less water reabsorption.
Easy Rule:
ADH ↑ → Water reabsorption ↑ → Urine more concentrated

13. Glomerular and Peritubular Capillaries

Glomerular Capillaries

These capillaries form the glomerulus and are involved in filtration.

Peritubular Capillaries

These capillaries surround the kidney tubules and are important in reabsorption and secretion.

Glomerulus → filtration
Peritubular capillaries → exchange with kidney tubules

14. Kidney Stones

Kidney stones are hard deposits that can form in the urinary system from substances present in urine.

Possible Effects

  • Severe pain
  • Difficulty in urine flow
  • Blood in urine
  • Urinary tract blockage

The exact composition of kidney stones can vary. Some contain calcium-based crystals.

15. Kidney Failure

Kidney failure occurs when the kidneys cannot perform their functions adequately.

Possible Consequences

  • Accumulation of wastes in blood.
  • Disturbance of water balance.
  • Disturbance of electrolyte balance.
  • Problems with acid-base regulation.

Treatment

Depending on the situation, treatment may include dialysis, medicines and, for some patients, kidney transplantation.

16. Thermoregulation 🌡️

Thermoregulation is the maintenance of body temperature within a suitable range.

When Body Temperature Increases

  • Sweating increases.
  • Blood vessels near the skin can dilate.
  • Heat loss from the body increases.

When Body Temperature Decreases

  • Skeletal muscles may contract rapidly, producing shivering.
  • Skin blood vessels can constrict.
  • Heat loss decreases.
Key idea: Thermoregulation helps maintain a relatively stable internal temperature.

17. Negative Feedback

Many homeostatic mechanisms operate through negative feedback. A change in a condition triggers responses that oppose the original change.

Example: If body temperature rises, sweating increases heat loss and helps bring temperature back toward its normal range.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Homeostasis maintains a relatively stable internal environment.
  • Kidneys are important organs of excretion and osmoregulation.
  • Nephron is the functional unit of the kidney.
  • Glomerulus is the site of filtration.
  • Bowman's capsule receives the filtrate.
  • Useful substances are selectively reabsorbed.
  • Tubular secretion adds certain substances to the filtrate.
  • Humans mainly excrete nitrogen as urea.
  • ADH increases water reabsorption.
  • Less body water usually leads to more concentrated urine.
  • Glomerular capillaries are involved in filtration.
  • Peritubular capillaries are important in reabsorption and secretion.
  • Kidney stones can obstruct urinary flow.
  • Kidney failure can cause wastes to accumulate in blood.
  • Thermoregulation helps control body temperature.

📝 MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. The functional unit of the kidney is:

A) Alveolus
B) Nephron
C) Neuron
D) Villus

Answer: B) Nephron

2. Filtration of blood occurs mainly in the:

A) Collecting duct
B) Glomerulus
C) Ureter
D) Urinary bladder

Answer: B) Glomerulus

3. The glomerulus is located inside the:

A) Ureter
B) Bowman's capsule
C) Urinary bladder
D) Urethra

Answer: B) Bowman's capsule

4. Which process returns useful substances from the filtrate to the blood?

A) Filtration
B) Selective reabsorption
C) Digestion
D) Ventilation

Answer: B) Selective reabsorption

5. The major nitrogenous waste excreted by humans is:

A) Urea
B) Oxygen
C) Glucose
D) Starch

Answer: A) Urea

6. Which hormone increases water reabsorption by the kidneys?

A) Insulin
B) ADH
C) Thyroxine
D) Adrenaline

Answer: B) ADH

7. When ADH levels increase, urine generally becomes:

A) More dilute
B) More concentrated
C) Completely absent
D) Rich in glucose

Answer: B) More concentrated

8. Which structure carries urine from kidney to urinary bladder?

A) Urethra
B) Ureter
C) Trachea
D) Esophagus

Answer: B) Ureter

9. Which vessels surround the kidney tubules and participate in exchange?

A) Peritubular capillaries
B) Pulmonary arteries
C) Coronary arteries only
D) Aorta

Answer: A) Peritubular capillaries

10. Osmoregulation is mainly concerned with regulation of:

A) Water and dissolved solutes
B) Bone length
C) Hair color
D) Vision only

Answer: A) Water and dissolved solutes

11. Which organ converts ammonia into urea?

A) Kidney
B) Liver
C) Heart
D) Lung

Answer: B) Liver

12. Which response helps cool the body when temperature rises?

A) Shivering
B) Sweating
C) Vasoconstriction only
D) Reduced heat loss

Answer: B) Sweating

13. Which process involves movement of substances from blood into the kidney tubule?

A) Tubular secretion
B) Inhalation
C) Digestion
D) Absorption in villi

Answer: A) Tubular secretion

14. A kidney stone may cause:

A) Airway blockage
B) Urinary tract blockage
C) Increased photosynthesis
D) Faster digestion

Answer: B) Urinary tract blockage

15. Homeostasis is best described as:

A) Complete absence of change in the body
B) Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment
C) Digestion of all food
D) Production of oxygen

Answer: B) Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the pathway of urine.
  • Remember: nephron = functional unit of kidney.
  • Know filtration, reabsorption and secretion separately.
  • Remember the role of the glomerulus.
  • Know the role of ADH in water balance.
  • Remember that humans mainly excrete urea.
  • Understand osmoregulation rather than only memorizing its definition.
  • Learn the basic causes and effects of kidney stones and kidney failure.
  • Know how sweating and shivering help thermoregulation.
