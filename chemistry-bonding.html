Unit 10: Chemical Bonding

Definition: Chemical bonding is the interaction that holds atoms or ions together in a chemical substance.

Atoms form bonds to achieve more stable electronic arrangements. Chemical bonding determines many physical and chemical properties of substances.

MDCAT Focus: Focus on VSEPR theory, sigma and pi bonds, hybridization, molecular shapes, polarity, dipole moment, ionic character of covalent bonds and bond energy.

1. Why Do Atoms Form Bonds?

Atoms generally form chemical bonds because the bonded arrangement can be more stable than the separate atoms.

The electrons involved in bonding are mainly the valence electrons.

Key Point: Chemical bonding involves interactions between atoms and their valence electrons.

2. Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share one or more pairs of electrons.
Example:

H₂ contains one shared pair of electrons between two hydrogen atoms.

H — H

Covalent bonds are commonly formed between non-metal atoms.

3. Ionic Character of Covalent Bonds

A covalent bond may have some ionic character when the bonding electrons are shared unequally because the bonded atoms have different electronegativities.

Greater electronegativity difference generally produces greater ionic character in a covalent bond.

A perfectly equal sharing of electrons is associated with a nonpolar covalent bond, while unequal sharing produces a polar covalent bond.

4. VSEPR Theory

VSEPR stands for Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion.

VSEPR theory states that electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible to minimize repulsion.

Important: Electron-pair repulsions determine the geometry or shape of many molecules.

5. Types of Electron-Pair Repulsion

Electron pairs may be bonding pairs or lone pairs.

Lone pairs generally repel more strongly than bonding pairs because they are localized closer to the central atom.

Repulsion Relative Strength
Lone pair – Lone pair Strongest
Lone pair – Bond pair Intermediate
Bond pair – Bond pair Weakest
Remember: LP–LP > LP–BP > BP–BP

6. Common Molecular Shapes

Molecule / Ion Shape
BeCl₂ Linear
BF₃ Trigonal planar
CH₄ Tetrahedral
NH₃ Trigonal pyramidal
H₂O Bent / V-shaped
MDCAT Tip: Lone pairs can change the observed molecular shape by increasing electron-pair repulsion.

7. Linear Shape

In a linear arrangement, the bond angle is approximately 180°.

Example:

BeCl₂

Cl — Be — Cl

Bond angle ≈ 180°

8. Trigonal Planar Shape

In a trigonal planar arrangement, three bonding regions are arranged around the central atom in one plane.

Typical bond angle ≈ 120°
Example:
BF₃ is trigonal planar.

9. Tetrahedral Shape

A tetrahedral arrangement has four bonding regions around the central atom.

Typical bond angle ≈ 109.5°
Example:
CH₄ has a tetrahedral molecular shape.

10. Trigonal Pyramidal Shape

NH₃ has three N–H bonds and one lone pair on nitrogen.

The lone pair causes stronger repulsion and reduces the H–N–H bond angle from the ideal tetrahedral angle.

Example:
NH₃ → trigonal pyramidal
Bond angle ≈ 107°

11. Bent / V-Shaped Molecule

H₂O has two bonding pairs and two lone pairs around oxygen.

The lone pairs repel strongly, producing a bent molecular shape.

Example:
H₂O → bent / V-shaped
Bond angle ≈ 104.5°

12. Sigma (σ) Bond

A sigma (σ) bond is formed by head-on overlap of orbitals along the internuclear axis.

Sigma bonds have electron density concentrated along the line joining the nuclei.

Key Point: Every single covalent bond is a sigma bond.

13. Pi (π) Bond

A pi (π) bond is formed by sideways overlap of parallel orbitals, usually p orbitals.

Pi bonds are found in multiple bonds along with a sigma bond.

Bond Composition
Single bond 1 σ
Double bond 1 σ + 1 π
Triple bond 1 σ + 2 π
Very Important: A double bond contains one sigma and one pi bond. A triple bond contains one sigma and two pi bonds.

14. Sigma vs Pi Bond

Sigma (σ) Pi (π)
Head-on overlap Sideways overlap
Electron density along internuclear axis Electron density above/below the internuclear axis
Present in all single bonds Present in multiple bonds
Generally stronger than a pi bond Generally weaker than a sigma bond

15. Hybridization

Hybridization is the mixing of atomic orbitals of comparable energy to form new hybrid orbitals suitable for bonding.

Hybridization helps explain molecular geometry and bonding in molecules.

Hybridization Common Geometry Example
sp Linear BeCl₂
sp² Trigonal planar BF₃
sp³ Tetrahedral electron geometry CH₄

16. sp Hybridization

In sp hybridization, one s orbital mixes with one p orbital to form two sp hybrid orbitals.

sp → 2 hybrid orbitals → linear arrangement
Example:
BeCl₂ is commonly described using sp hybridization around Be.

17. sp² Hybridization

In sp² hybridization, one s orbital mixes with two p orbitals to form three sp² hybrid orbitals.

sp² → 3 hybrid orbitals → trigonal planar arrangement
Example:
BF₃ is commonly described using sp² hybridization around B.

18. sp³ Hybridization

In sp³ hybridization, one s orbital mixes with three p orbitals to form four sp³ hybrid orbitals.

sp³ → 4 hybrid orbitals → tetrahedral electron geometry
Examples:
CH₄ → tetrahedral
NH₃ → trigonal pyramidal
H₂O → bent

19. Polarity of Bonds

A polar covalent bond occurs when bonding electrons are shared unequally between atoms.

The unequal sharing results from differences in electronegativity.

Example:
In H–Cl, chlorine attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen, producing a polar bond.

20. Nonpolar Covalent Bond

A nonpolar covalent bond involves approximately equal sharing of bonding electrons.
Example:
H₂ contains a nonpolar covalent H–H bond because both atoms have the same electronegativity.

21. Dipole Moment

Dipole moment is a measure of the separation of positive and negative charge in a bond or molecule.
μ = q × r

μ = dipole moment
q = magnitude of separated charge
r = distance between the charges

Molecular dipole moment depends on both bond polarity and molecular geometry.

22. Bond Polarity vs Molecular Polarity

A molecule may contain polar bonds but still have zero net dipole moment if the bond dipoles cancel because of molecular symmetry.

Example:
CO₂ contains polar C=O bonds, but because the molecule is linear and symmetrical, the bond dipoles cancel and the molecule has no net dipole moment.
H₂O has polar bonds and a bent shape, so its bond dipoles do not cancel completely. Therefore H₂O is polar.

23. Bond Energy

Bond energy is the energy required to break a particular chemical bond in the gaseous state, usually expressed as an average value for a bond type.

Stronger bonds generally require more energy to break.

Key Point: Greater bond energy generally indicates a stronger bond.

24. Bond Length and Bond Strength

Bond length is the average distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms.

In general, shorter bonds tend to be stronger than longer bonds between comparable atoms.

Bond General Relative Strength
Single Lowest
Double Higher
Triple Highest
For comparable bonds between the same atoms: Triple > Double > Single in bond strength, while bond length generally follows the opposite trend.

26. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. VSEPR stands for:

A. Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion
B. Variable Shell Electron Proton Reaction
C. Valence State Energy Pair Rotation
D. Variable State Electron Pair Ratio

Answer: A. Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion

2. According to VSEPR theory, electron pairs:

A. Attract each other strongly
B. Repel each other
C. Have no effect on shape
D. Always form ions

Answer: B. Repel each other

3. Which has the strongest electron-pair repulsion?

A. Bond pair–bond pair
B. Lone pair–bond pair
C. Lone pair–lone pair
D. Proton–electron

Answer: C. Lone pair–lone pair

4. The shape of BeCl₂ is:

A. Bent
B. Linear
C. Tetrahedral
D. Trigonal pyramidal

Answer: B. Linear

5. BF₃ has a:

A. Linear shape
B. Trigonal planar shape
C. Bent shape
D. Tetrahedral shape

Answer: B. Trigonal planar shape

6. CH₄ has a:

A. Linear shape
B. Trigonal planar shape
C. Tetrahedral shape
D. Bent shape

Answer: C. Tetrahedral shape

7. NH₃ has a:

A. Linear shape
B. Trigonal pyramidal shape
C. Trigonal planar shape
D. Square planar shape

Answer: B. Trigonal pyramidal shape

8. H₂O has a:

A. Linear shape
B. Bent shape
C. Trigonal planar shape
D. Tetrahedral molecular shape

Answer: B. Bent shape

9. A sigma bond is formed by:

A. Sideways overlap
B. Head-on overlap
C. No overlap
D. Nuclear fusion

Answer: B. Head-on overlap

10. A double bond contains:

A. 2 sigma bonds
B. 2 pi bonds
C. 1 sigma and 1 pi bond
D. 1 pi bond only

Answer: C. 1 sigma and 1 pi bond

11. A triple bond contains:

A. 3 sigma bonds
B. 1 sigma and 2 pi bonds
C. 2 sigma and 1 pi bond
D. 3 pi bonds

Answer: B. 1 sigma and 2 pi bonds

12. The hybridization commonly associated with linear geometry is:

A. sp
B. sp²
C. sp³
D. sp³d²

Answer: A. sp

13. The hybridization commonly associated with trigonal planar geometry is:

A. sp
B. sp²
C. sp³
D. d²sp³

Answer: B. sp²

14. Dipole moment is related to:

A. Charge separation
B. Atomic mass only
C. Number of neutrons only
D. Boiling point only

Answer: A. Charge separation

15. Which statement about bond energy is correct?

A. Stronger bonds generally require more energy to break
B. Stronger bonds always have lower energy
C. Bond energy is unrelated to bond strength
D. Bond energy applies only to ions

Answer: A. Stronger bonds generally require more energy to break

27. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember the VSEPR concept: electron pairs repel each other.
  • Learn the common shapes of BeCl₂, BF₃, CH₄, NH₃ and H₂O.
  • Remember LP–LP > LP–BP > BP–BP.
  • Single bond = 1 sigma.
  • Double bond = 1 sigma + 1 pi.
  • Triple bond = 1 sigma + 2 pi.
  • sp = linear.
  • sp² = trigonal planar.
  • sp³ = tetrahedral electron geometry.
  • Polar bonds result from unequal sharing of electrons.
  • Molecular shape determines whether bond dipoles cancel.
  • Learn the difference between bond polarity and molecular polarity.
  • Remember that stronger bonds generally have higher bond energy.
