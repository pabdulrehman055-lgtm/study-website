1. Particle Physics

Particle physics is the branch of physics that studies the fundamental particles that make up matter and the forces through which they interact.

Very small particles are studied using high-energy experiments and particle accelerators.

2. Fundamental Particles

Matter is made from very small particles. Some particles are considered fundamental, meaning they are not known to be made from smaller particles.

Important fundamental particles include quarks and leptons.

3. Quarks

Quarks are fundamental particles that combine to form particles such as protons and neutrons.

The six types, or flavors, of quarks are:

  • Up
  • Down
  • Charm
  • Strange
  • Top
  • Bottom

4. Protons and Neutrons

Protons and neutrons are called nucleons because they are found inside atomic nuclei.

A proton consists of two up quarks and one down quark.

Proton = uud

A neutron consists of one up quark and two down quarks.

Neutron = udd

5. Leptons

Leptons are another group of fundamental particles.

The best-known lepton is the electron. Other leptons include muons, taus and their associated neutrinos.

Unlike quarks, leptons do not participate in the strong nuclear interaction.

6. Antiparticles

Many particles have corresponding antiparticles.

An antiparticle has the same mass as its corresponding particle but opposite electric charge when the particle carries charge.

For example, the antiparticle of the electron is the positron.

7. Fundamental Forces

There are four fundamental interactions in nature:

  • Gravitational force
  • Electromagnetic force
  • Strong nuclear force
  • Weak nuclear force

8. Particle Accelerators

A particle accelerator is a machine that uses electric and magnetic fields to accelerate charged particles to very high speeds.

Scientists use particle accelerators to study collisions and investigate the structure of matter.

9. Standard Model

The Standard Model is a theory describing many of the known fundamental particles and their interactions.

It includes quarks, leptons and force-carrying particles.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. Which of the following is a fundamental particle?

A) Electron

B) Water molecule

C) Atom

D) Cell

Answer: A) Electron

2. A proton consists of:

A) uud

B) udd

C) uuu

D) ddd

Answer: A) uud

3. A neutron consists of:

A) uud

B) udd

C) uuu

D) ddd

Answer: B) udd

4. The antiparticle of an electron is called:

A) Proton

B) Neutron

C) Positron

D) Photon

Answer: C) Positron

5. How many fundamental interactions are recognized in nature?

A) Two

B) Three

C) Four

D) Five

Answer: C) Four
