Chapter Overview

Support and movement are important functions of the human body. The skeleton provides support and protection, while muscles and joints allow the body to move.

MDCAT Focus: Learn the functions of bones, types of joints, skeletal divisions, muscles, tendons, ligaments and basic muscle contraction.

1. Functions of the Skeleton

The human skeleton performs several important functions:

  • Provides support to the body
  • Maintains body shape
  • Protects internal organs
  • Allows movement with the help of muscles
  • Stores minerals such as calcium and phosphorus
  • Produces blood cells in bone marrow
Exam Tip: Bone marrow is an important site of blood cell formation.

2. Divisions of the Human Skeleton

Axial Skeleton

The axial skeleton forms the central axis of the body.

  • Skull
  • Vertebral column
  • Ribs
  • Sternum

Appendicular Skeleton

The appendicular skeleton consists mainly of the limbs and structures that attach limbs to the axial skeleton.

  • Upper limbs
  • Lower limbs
  • Pectoral girdle
  • Pelvic girdle
Remember: Axial = central axis   |   Appendicular = limbs and girdles

3. Bones

Bones are hard connective tissues that form the skeleton. They contain living cells and a mineral-rich matrix.

Functions of Bones

  • Support
  • Protection
  • Movement
  • Mineral storage
  • Blood cell production

Bone Marrow

Bone marrow is found inside many bones. Red bone marrow is associated with the production of blood cells.

4. Joints

A joint is a place where two or more bones meet. Joints help connect bones and may allow movement.

Major Types of Joints

  • Fixed joints: Allow little or no movement.
  • Cartilaginous joints: Allow limited movement.
  • Synovial joints: Allow considerable movement.

5. Synovial Joints

Synovial joints are freely movable joints found in many parts of the body.

Examples

  • Shoulder joint
  • Hip joint
  • Elbow joint
  • Knee joint

Common Types

  • Ball-and-socket: Shoulder and hip
  • Hinge: Elbow and knee
  • Pivot: Allows rotational movement
MDCAT Tip: Shoulder and hip are classic examples of ball-and-socket joints.

6. Cartilage

Cartilage is a strong but flexible connective tissue. It is present in several parts of the body.

  • Provides support
  • Reduces friction at joints
  • Absorbs shock
  • Provides flexible support

7. Ligaments

Ligaments connect one bone to another bone.

Easy Memory Trick:
Ligament → Bone to Bone

Ligaments help stabilize joints and prevent excessive movement.

8. Tendons

Tendons connect muscles to bones.

Easy Memory Trick:
Tendon → Muscle to Bone

When a muscle contracts, the tendon transmits the force to the bone and helps produce movement.

9. Muscles

Muscles are specialized tissues that can contract and generate force.

Three Main Types of Muscle

  • Skeletal muscle: Usually attached to bones and involved in voluntary movement.
  • Smooth muscle: Found in organs such as the intestine and blood vessels.
  • Cardiac muscle: Found in the heart.

10. Skeletal Muscle

Skeletal muscles are generally attached to bones through tendons. Their contraction produces movement of body parts.

  • Usually voluntary
  • Attached to bones
  • Shows striations under the microscope
  • Helps maintain posture
  • Produces body movement

11. Muscle Contraction

Muscle contraction involves interaction between protein filaments inside muscle cells.

Important Proteins

  • Actin
  • Myosin

During contraction, myosin interacts with actin, causing the muscle fibers to generate force.

Important: ATP provides energy required for the molecular processes involved in muscle contraction.

12. Antagonistic Muscles

Some muscles work in pairs where one muscle produces an action opposite to the other.

A common example is the pair of muscles associated with movement of the arm.

  • One muscle contracts
  • The opposing muscle relaxes
  • The movement can then occur in the required direction

13. Movement at Joints

Different joints permit different types and ranges of movement.

  • Flexion: Decreases the angle between bones.
  • Extension: Increases the angle between bones.
  • Rotation: Movement around an axis.
  • Abduction: Movement away from the body's midline.
  • Adduction: Movement toward the body's midline.

14. Bone Health

Healthy bones require proper nutrition and physical activity.

  • Calcium is important for bone structure.
  • Phosphorus contributes to bone mineralization.
  • Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium.
  • Regular physical activity supports bone health.
Remember: Calcium + phosphorus + vitamin D are important for normal bone health.

15. Quick Revision

  • Skeleton provides support and protection.
  • Axial skeleton forms the central axis.
  • Appendicular skeleton includes limbs and girdles.
  • Joints are places where bones meet.
  • Synovial joints are freely movable.
  • Ligament connects bone to bone.
  • Tendon connects muscle to bone.
  • Actin and myosin are important muscle proteins.
  • ATP provides energy for muscle contraction.
  • Skeletal muscles are generally voluntary.
  • Cardiac muscle is found in the heart.
  • Smooth muscle occurs in many internal organs.

16. MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Which structure connects muscle to bone?

A) Ligament

B) Tendon

C) Cartilage

D) Joint

Answer: B) Tendon

2. Which structure connects bone to bone?

A) Tendon

B) Ligament

C) Muscle

D) Cartilage

Answer: B) Ligament

3. Which is an example of a ball-and-socket joint?

A) Elbow

B) Knee

C) Shoulder

D) Finger

Answer: C) Shoulder

4. Which muscle is found in the heart?

A) Smooth muscle

B) Skeletal muscle

C) Cardiac muscle

D) Connective muscle

Answer: C) Cardiac muscle

5. Which proteins are mainly involved in muscle contraction?

A) Keratin and collagen

B) Actin and myosin

C) Insulin and glucagon

D) DNA and RNA

Answer: B) Actin and myosin

6. Which type of skeleton includes the skull and vertebral column?

A) Appendicular

B) Axial

C) Peripheral

D) Muscular

Answer: B) Axial

7. Which muscle type is generally voluntary?

A) Skeletal

B) Smooth

C) Cardiac

D) None

Answer: A) Skeletal

8. Which mineral is important for bone formation?

A) Calcium

B) Sodium only

C) Chlorine only

D) Iodine only

Answer: A) Calcium

9. Which type of joint is present at the elbow?

A) Ball-and-socket

B) Hinge

C) Fixed

D) Pivot only

Answer: B) Hinge

10. What molecule provides energy for muscle contraction?

A) DNA

B) ATP

C) RNA

D) Oxygen only

Answer: B) ATP

11. Which tissue reduces friction at many joints?

A) Cartilage

B) Nervous tissue

C) Blood

D) Epidermis

Answer: A) Cartilage

12. Which muscle type is present in the walls of many internal organs?

A) Skeletal

B) Smooth

C) Cardiac

D) Bone

Answer: B) Smooth

13. The appendicular skeleton mainly includes:

A) Skull only

B) Ribs only

C) Limbs and girdles

D) Vertebral column only

Answer: C) Limbs and girdles

14. Which vitamin helps the body absorb calcium?

A) Vitamin D

B) Vitamin C

C) Vitamin B12

D) Vitamin K only

Answer: A) Vitamin D

15. Which of the following decreases the angle between bones?

A) Extension

B) Flexion

C) Rotation

D) Abduction

Answer: B) Flexion

17. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember: Tendon = muscle to bone.
  • Remember: Ligament = bone to bone.
  • Shoulder and hip are ball-and-socket joints.
  • Elbow and knee are examples of hinge joints.
  • Actin and myosin are important for muscle contraction.
  • ATP supplies energy for muscle contraction.
  • Axial skeleton = central axis.
  • Appendicular skeleton = limbs and girdles.
High-Yield MCQ: If the question asks which structure connects a muscle with a bone, choose TENDON.
