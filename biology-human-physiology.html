1. What is Human Physiology?

Human physiology is the study of how the human body and its organs function.

Different organ systems work together to maintain life and keep the internal environment within suitable limits.

Homeostasis: Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment despite changes in external or internal conditions.

2. Digestive System

The digestive system breaks food into smaller molecules that can be absorbed and used by the body.

Main Parts

  • Mouth
  • Pharynx
  • Esophagus
  • Stomach
  • Small intestine
  • Large intestine
  • Rectum
  • Anus

Accessory Organs

  • Liver
  • Gallbladder
  • Pancreas
MDCAT Tip: Most chemical digestion and nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine.

3. Digestion in the Mouth

Digestion begins in the mouth. Teeth mechanically break food into smaller pieces while saliva moistens the food.

Salivary Amylase

Salivary amylase begins the digestion of starch into smaller carbohydrate molecules.

Remember: Amylase → starch digestion.

4. Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ that stores food and mixes it with gastric juice.

Important Components

  • Hydrochloric acid (HCl): provides an acidic environment.
  • Pepsin: begins protein digestion.
  • Mucus: helps protect the stomach lining.
MDCAT Point: Pepsin works best in an acidic environment.

5. Small Intestine and Absorption

The small intestine is the major site of chemical digestion and absorption of nutrients.

Villi

The inner surface contains numerous finger-like projections called villi.

Villi greatly increase the surface area available for absorption.

Key Point: Villi → larger surface area → efficient nutrient absorption.

6. Liver, Gallbladder and Pancreas

Liver

The liver produces bile. Bile helps in the digestion of fats by emulsifying large fat droplets.

Gallbladder

The gallbladder stores and concentrates bile.

Pancreas

The pancreas produces digestive enzymes and releases pancreatic juice into the small intestine.

Remember: Liver makes bile; gallbladder stores bile.

7. Respiratory System

The respiratory system supplies oxygen to the body and removes carbon dioxide.

Main Respiratory Structures

  • Nose and nasal cavity
  • Pharynx
  • Larynx
  • Trachea
  • Bronchi
  • Bronchioles
  • Alveoli
  • Lungs

8. Alveoli and Gas Exchange

Alveoli are tiny air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange occurs.

Oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood, while carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveoli.

Why are alveoli efficient? They have a large surface area, thin walls and a rich blood supply.

9. Breathing

Inhalation

During inhalation, the diaphragm contracts and moves downward. The volume of the thoracic cavity increases and air enters the lungs.

Exhalation

During normal exhalation, the diaphragm relaxes and moves upward. The volume of the thoracic cavity decreases and air leaves the lungs.

Inhalation Exhalation
Diaphragm contracts Diaphragm relaxes
Thoracic volume increases Thoracic volume decreases
Air enters lungs Air leaves lungs

10. Circulatory System

The circulatory system transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones and waste products throughout the body.

Main Components

  • Heart
  • Blood
  • Blood vessels

Blood Vessels

Vessel Function
Arteries Carry blood away from the heart.
Veins Carry blood toward the heart.
Capillaries Allow exchange of substances between blood and tissues.
Important: Arteries are defined by carrying blood away from the heart, not simply by whether the blood is oxygenated.

11. Human Heart

The human heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood through the circulatory system.

Four Chambers

  • Right atrium
  • Right ventricle
  • Left atrium
  • Left ventricle

Important Valves

  • Tricuspid valve: between right atrium and right ventricle.
  • Bicuspid/Mitral valve: between left atrium and left ventricle.

The right side generally handles deoxygenated blood, while the left side generally handles oxygenated blood.

Key Point: The left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood into systemic circulation.

12. Blood

Blood is a connective tissue that transports substances throughout the body.

Component Function
Red blood cells Transport oxygen using haemoglobin.
White blood cells Help defend the body against pathogens.
Platelets Help in blood clotting.
Plasma Liquid part of blood that carries dissolved substances.

13. Excretory System

Excretion is the removal of metabolic waste products from the body.

Main Organs of the Urinary System

  • Kidneys
  • Ureters
  • Urinary bladder
  • Urethra

The kidneys filter blood and help regulate water, ions and other components of the internal environment.

14. Nephron

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney.

Main Parts

  • Glomerulus
  • Bowman's capsule
  • Proximal convoluted tubule
  • Loop of Henle
  • Distal convoluted tubule
  • Collecting duct

Major Processes

  1. Filtration
  2. Reabsorption
  3. Secretion
MDCAT Point: Filtration begins at the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule.

15. Nervous System

The nervous system controls and coordinates many activities of the body.

Main Divisions

  • Central Nervous System (CNS) — brain and spinal cord.
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS) — nerves outside the CNS.

Neuron

A neuron is the basic functional cell of the nervous system.

16. Brain

Part Important Function
Cerebrum Associated with conscious activities, thinking, memory and sensory processing.
Cerebellum Helps coordinate movement and maintain balance.
Medulla oblongata Controls important involuntary functions such as breathing and heart activity.
Hypothalamus Helps regulate homeostasis and connects nervous and endocrine functions.

17. Reflex Action

A reflex action is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus.

Many reflexes are coordinated through the spinal cord, allowing a rapid response before conscious processing is complete.

Example: Quickly pulling your hand away from a hot object.

18. Endocrine System

The endocrine system consists of glands that release hormones into the blood.

Hormone Main Source Main Role
Insulin Pancreas Helps lower blood glucose concentration.
Glucagon Pancreas Helps raise blood glucose concentration.
Thyroxine Thyroid gland Helps regulate metabolic activity.
Adrenaline Adrenal glands Prepares the body for rapid action during stress.
Growth hormone Pituitary gland Promotes growth and development.

19. Homeostasis

Homeostasis keeps internal conditions within suitable ranges.

Examples

  • Body temperature regulation
  • Blood glucose regulation
  • Water balance
  • Ion balance
Negative Feedback: A change in a condition triggers responses that tend to oppose the original change and restore the condition toward its normal range.

21. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The study of functions of the human body is called:

A) Anatomy

B) Physiology

C) Genetics

D) Ecology

Answer: B) Physiology

2. Which structure increases the surface area for absorption in the small intestine?

A) Alveoli

B) Villi

C) Nephrons

D) Bronchi

Answer: B) Villi

3. Which enzyme begins protein digestion in the stomach?

A) Amylase

B) Lipase

C) Pepsin

D) Trypsin

Answer: C) Pepsin

4. Gas exchange occurs mainly in the:

A) Trachea

B) Bronchi

C) Alveoli

D) Larynx

Answer: C) Alveoli

5. Which blood vessel carries blood away from the heart?

A) Vein

B) Artery

C) Capillary

D) Venule

Answer: B) Artery

6. The functional unit of the kidney is:

A) Neuron

B) Nephron

C) Alveolus

D) Villus

Answer: B) Nephron

7. The central nervous system consists of:

A) Brain and spinal cord

B) Heart and brain

C) Brain and kidneys

D) Nerves and muscles only

Answer: A) Brain and spinal cord

8. Which part of the brain helps coordinate movement and balance?

A) Cerebrum

B) Cerebellum

C) Medulla

D) Hypothalamus

Answer: B) Cerebellum

9. Which hormone generally lowers blood glucose concentration?

A) Glucagon

B) Insulin

C) Adrenaline

D) Thyroxine

Answer: B) Insulin

10. Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment is called:

A) Digestion

B) Respiration

C) Homeostasis

D) Excretion

Answer: C) Homeostasis

11. Which chamber pumps oxygenated blood into systemic circulation?

A) Right atrium

B) Right ventricle

C) Left atrium

D) Left ventricle

Answer: D) Left ventricle

12. Which blood component is mainly involved in clotting?

A) Red blood cells

B) White blood cells

C) Platelets

D) Plasma proteins only

Answer: C) Platelets

13. During inhalation, the diaphragm:

A) Relaxes and moves upward

B) Contracts and moves downward

C) Does not move

D) Contracts and moves upward

Answer: B) Contracts and moves downward

14. Which hormone helps increase blood glucose concentration?

A) Insulin

B) Glucagon

C) Thyroxine

D) Growth hormone

Answer: B) Glucagon

15. Which structure is the first major site of filtration in a nephron?

A) Collecting duct

B) Loop of Henle

C) Glomerulus

D) Distal convoluted tubule

Answer: C) Glomerulus

22. MDCAT Exam Tip

Focus especially on digestion, gas exchange, heart chambers, blood vessels, nephron, nervous system, endocrine hormones and homeostasis.

Learn functions carefully and compare similar concepts such as arteries vs veins, inhalation vs exhalation and insulin vs glucagon.
