❤️ Circulation

Definition: Circulation is the movement of blood through the heart and blood vessels to transport oxygen, nutrients, hormones and other substances throughout the body.

The human circulatory system is mainly made up of the heart, blood and blood vessels.

MDCAT Focus: Understand the structure of the heart, cardiac cycle, arteries, veins, capillaries and the lymphatic system.

1. Blood 🩸

Blood is a connective tissue that circulates throughout the body. It transports gases, nutrients, hormones and wastes.

Main Components of Blood

Component Main Function
Plasma Liquid part of blood; transports dissolved substances.
Red Blood Cells (RBCs) Transport oxygen mainly using hemoglobin.
White Blood Cells (WBCs) Help defend the body against pathogens.
Platelets Help in blood clotting.

2. Red Blood Cells (RBCs)

  • RBCs are also called erythrocytes.
  • They transport oxygen from lungs to tissues.
  • They contain the pigment hemoglobin.
  • Hemoglobin binds with oxygen.
  • Mature human RBCs normally lack a nucleus.
  • Their biconcave shape provides a large surface area for gas exchange.
Remember: Hemoglobin is the oxygen-carrying protein present inside red blood cells.

3. White Blood Cells (WBCs)

White blood cells, also called leukocytes, are important for protection against infections.

  • They are involved in immune defense.
  • They can move out of blood vessels into tissues.
  • Some WBCs engulf pathogens.
  • Lymphocytes are involved in specific immune responses.

4. Platelets

Platelets are small cell fragments involved in blood clotting.

When a blood vessel is damaged, platelets help form a temporary plug and participate in the clotting process.

Exam Point: Platelets → Blood clotting.

5. Blood Groups

The ABO blood group system is based mainly on the presence or absence of A and B antigens on red blood cells.

Blood Group RBC Antigens Plasma Antibodies
A A Anti-B
B B Anti-A
AB A and B Neither anti-A nor anti-B
O Neither A nor B Anti-A and Anti-B

The Rh system is another important blood group system. A person may be Rh-positive or Rh-negative.

6. Human Heart ❤️

The human heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

Four Chambers of the Heart

  • Right atrium — receives deoxygenated blood from the body.
  • Right ventricle — pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs.
  • Left atrium — receives oxygenated blood from the lungs.
  • Left ventricle — pumps oxygenated blood to the body.
Very Important: The left ventricle has a thick muscular wall because it must pump blood to the entire body.

7. Major Blood Vessels of the Heart

  • Venae cavae: Bring deoxygenated blood from the body to the right atrium.
  • Pulmonary artery: Carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs.
  • Pulmonary veins: Carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium.
  • Aorta: Carries oxygenated blood from the left ventricle to the body.
Special Exceptions:
Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.
Pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.

8. Heart Valves

Valves ensure that blood flows in the correct direction and prevent backward flow.

  • Tricuspid valve: Between right atrium and right ventricle.
  • Mitral/Bicuspid valve: Between left atrium and left ventricle.
  • Pulmonary valve: Between right ventricle and pulmonary artery.
  • Aortic valve: Between left ventricle and aorta.

9. Cardiac Cycle 💓

The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events occurring during one complete heartbeat.

Main Phases

  1. Atrial systole: Atria contract and push blood into the ventricles.
  2. Ventricular systole: Ventricles contract and pump blood out of the heart.
  3. Diastole: Heart chambers relax and fill with blood.
Remember:
Systole = contraction
Diastole = relaxation

10. Pulmonary and Systemic Circulation

Pulmonary Circulation

Pulmonary circulation carries blood between the heart and lungs.

Right ventricle → Pulmonary artery → Lungs → Pulmonary veins → Left atrium

Systemic Circulation

Systemic circulation carries blood between the heart and the rest of the body.

Left ventricle → Aorta → Body tissues → Venae cavae → Right atrium

11. Blood Vessels

Blood Vessel Direction Main Feature
Arteries Away from heart Usually thick, muscular and elastic walls.
Veins Toward heart Usually thinner walls; many contain valves.
Capillaries Connect arterioles and venules Very thin walls for exchange of substances.
Important: Arteries are defined by carrying blood away from the heart, not simply by carrying oxygenated blood.

12. Capillaries

Capillaries are tiny blood vessels with very thin walls. They allow exchange between blood and body tissues.

  • Oxygen can move from blood into tissues.
  • Carbon dioxide can move from tissues into blood.
  • Nutrients can leave the blood.
  • Metabolic wastes can enter the blood.

13. Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is the pressure exerted by circulating blood against the walls of blood vessels, especially arteries.

  • Systolic pressure: Pressure during ventricular contraction.
  • Diastolic pressure: Pressure during ventricular relaxation.
Blood pressure is commonly written as systolic pressure over diastolic pressure, for example 120/80 mmHg.

14. Lymphatic System

The lymphatic system is an important part of fluid balance and immune defense.

Main Components

  • Lymph
  • Lymphatic vessels
  • Lymph nodes
  • Thymus
  • Spleen
  • Tonsils

Functions

  • Returns excess tissue fluid to the bloodstream.
  • Helps transport lymph.
  • Lymph nodes filter lymph and support immune responses.
  • Helps defend the body against pathogens.

15. Blood Flow — Easy Revision

Body → Right Heart → Lungs → Left Heart → Body

Complete pathway:

Body → Venae Cavae → Right Atrium → Tricuspid Valve → Right Ventricle → Pulmonary Valve → Pulmonary Artery → Lungs → Pulmonary Veins → Left Atrium → Mitral Valve → Left Ventricle → Aortic Valve → Aorta → Body

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Heart has four chambers.
  • Right side mainly handles deoxygenated blood.
  • Left side mainly handles oxygenated blood.
  • Right ventricle pumps blood to lungs.
  • Left ventricle pumps blood to the body.
  • Aorta carries blood from left ventricle to the body.
  • Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to lungs.
  • Pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood to left atrium.
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart.
  • Veins carry blood toward the heart.
  • Capillaries are the main exchange vessels.
  • RBCs transport oxygen using hemoglobin.
  • WBCs help in defense.
  • Platelets help in clotting.
  • Lymphatic system helps fluid balance and immunity.

📝 MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Which chamber pumps oxygenated blood to the whole body?

A) Right atrium
B) Right ventricle
C) Left atrium
D) Left ventricle

Answer: D) Left ventricle

2. Which blood vessel carries deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs?

A) Aorta
B) Pulmonary artery
C) Pulmonary vein
D) Vena cava

Answer: B) Pulmonary artery

3. Which protein carries oxygen in red blood cells?

A) Insulin
B) Keratin
C) Hemoglobin
D) Collagen

Answer: C) Hemoglobin

4. Which blood cells are mainly involved in body defense?

A) RBCs
B) WBCs
C) Platelets
D) Erythrocytes

Answer: B) WBCs

5. Which blood component helps in clotting?

A) Plasma
B) RBCs
C) Platelets
D) Hemoglobin

Answer: C) Platelets

6. Which vessel carries oxygenated blood from lungs to the heart?

A) Pulmonary artery
B) Pulmonary vein
C) Vena cava
D) Aorta

Answer: B) Pulmonary vein

7. Which valve is present between the right atrium and right ventricle?

A) Aortic valve
B) Mitral valve
C) Tricuspid valve
D) Pulmonary valve

Answer: C) Tricuspid valve

8. What does systole refer to?

A) Relaxation
B) Contraction
C) Blood clotting
D) Gas exchange

Answer: B) Contraction

9. Which blood vessels have very thin walls for exchange?

A) Arteries
B) Veins
C) Capillaries
D) Aorta

Answer: C) Capillaries

10. The aorta receives blood from the:

A) Right atrium
B) Right ventricle
C) Left atrium
D) Left ventricle

Answer: D) Left ventricle

11. Which side of the heart receives blood from the body?

A) Right side
B) Left side
C) Both only from lungs
D) Neither side

Answer: A) Right side

12. Which structure prevents backward flow of blood?

A) Alveoli
B) Valves
C) Nephrons
D) Neurons

Answer: B) Valves

13. The main function of capillaries is:

A) Pumping blood
B) Blood clotting
C) Exchange of substances
D) Producing hormones

Answer: C) Exchange of substances

14. Which system returns excess tissue fluid to the bloodstream?

A) Digestive system
B) Lymphatic system
C) Skeletal system
D) Respiratory system

Answer: B) Lymphatic system

15. Which chamber has the thickest muscular wall?

A) Right atrium
B) Left atrium
C) Right ventricle
D) Left ventricle

Answer: D) Left ventricle

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the complete blood-flow pathway.
  • Remember the difference between arteries and veins.
  • Do not forget the pulmonary artery exception.
  • Know the four chambers of the heart.
  • Learn the names and positions of the major valves.
  • Understand systole and diastole.
  • Remember the functions of RBCs, WBCs and platelets.
  • Understand the basic role of the lymphatic system.
