📚 What are Biological Molecules?

Biological molecules are chemical substances present in living organisms. They are essential for the structure, growth, energy requirements and biological activities of cells.

Major biological molecules: Carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.

Water is also extremely important in living organisms because many biological reactions take place in an aqueous environment.

💧 1. Water

Water is one of the most important substances in living organisms. Its special properties are mainly related to its polarity and hydrogen bonding.

Polarity of Water

A water molecule has an unequal distribution of electrical charge. Oxygen carries a partial negative charge while hydrogen atoms carry partial positive charges.

Important Properties of Water

  • Water is a good solvent for many ionic and polar substances.
  • Water participates in hydrolysis reactions.
  • Water has a high specific heat capacity.
  • Water shows cohesion between its molecules.
  • Water can act as a reagent in biological reactions.
  • Water has an important role in maintaining biological systems.
MDCAT Point: Water's polarity makes it an effective solvent for many ionic and polar substances.

🍞 2. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are organic compounds mainly composed of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. They are important sources of energy and also have structural roles.

Monosaccharides

Monosaccharides are the simplest carbohydrates and cannot be hydrolysed into smaller carbohydrate units.

  • Glucose
  • Fructose
Important: Glucose is an important respiratory substrate.

Oligosaccharides

Oligosaccharides contain a small number of monosaccharide units joined together.

  • Sucrose: Commonly known as cane sugar.
  • Lactose: Milk sugar.

Polysaccharides

Polysaccharides are large carbohydrate molecules made from many monosaccharide units.

  • Starch: Important storage carbohydrate in plants.
  • Glycogen: Storage carbohydrate in animals.
  • Cellulose: Important structural carbohydrate in plants.
Carbohydrate Type Important Point
Glucose Monosaccharide Important respiratory substrate
Sucrose Oligosaccharide Cane sugar
Lactose Oligosaccharide Milk sugar
Starch Polysaccharide Plant storage carbohydrate
Glycogen Polysaccharide Animal storage carbohydrate
Cellulose Polysaccharide Plant structural carbohydrate

🥩 3. Proteins

Proteins are large biological molecules made from amino acids. They perform many structural and functional roles in living organisms.

Amino Acids

Amino acids are the basic building blocks of proteins. They contain an amino group and a carboxyl group.

Peptide Bond

Amino acids can join together by peptide bonds to form polypeptide chains.

Remember: Amino acids → Peptide bonds → Polypeptide → Protein

Levels of Protein Structure

  • Primary structure: Sequence of amino acids.
  • Secondary structure: Local folding of the polypeptide chain.
  • Tertiary structure: Three-dimensional folding of a polypeptide.
  • Quaternary structure: Association of multiple polypeptide chains.

Functions of Proteins

  • Enzymatic activity
  • Structural support
  • Transport of substances
  • Defence
  • Cell signalling and receptors
  • Regulation of biological processes

🥑 4. Lipids

Lipids are a diverse group of biological molecules that are generally insoluble or poorly soluble in water.

Triglycerides

Triglycerides are lipids formed from glycerol and fatty acids. They are important for energy storage.

Phospholipids

Phospholipids are important components of biological membranes. They contain a phosphate-containing group along with lipid components.

Functions of Lipids

  • Long-term energy storage
  • Formation of biological membranes
  • Insulation
  • Protection of organs
  • Participation in biological signalling
MDCAT Point: Phospholipids are major structural components of cell membranes.

🧬 5. RNA

RNA stands for ribonucleic acid. It is a nucleic acid made from nucleotide units and has important roles in gene expression and protein synthesis.

Important Types of RNA

  • mRNA: Carries genetic information for protein synthesis.
  • tRNA: Helps bring amino acids during protein synthesis.
  • rRNA: Forms an important part of ribosomes.
Remember: RNA is involved in the expression of genetic information.

🔗 6. Conjugated Molecules

Conjugated biological molecules contain two or more different types of chemical components.

Glycolipids

Glycolipids contain carbohydrate components associated with lipids. They are important in cell membranes and cell recognition.

Glycoproteins

Glycoproteins are proteins with carbohydrate groups attached. They have important roles in cell recognition, receptors and other biological functions.

Molecule Components
Glycolipid Carbohydrate + Lipid
Glycoprotein Carbohydrate + Protein

🧬 7. DNA

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It is the major molecule responsible for storing hereditary genetic information.

DNA Structure

DNA consists of two polynucleotide strands arranged in a double-helix structure.

  • DNA contains nucleotide units.
  • The two strands form a double helix.
  • The structure of DNA was described by Watson and Crick.
  • DNA stores genetic information.
MDCAT Point: DNA double-helix structure is an important concept for Biology MCQs.

🧬 8. Gene

A gene is a specific sequence of DNA nucleotides that carries information for a functional product, such as a polypeptide.

DNA → Gene → Genetic Information → Functional Product
Remember: A gene is a sequence of DNA nucleotides coding for a polypeptide.

📊 DNA vs RNA

Feature DNA RNA
Name Deoxyribonucleic acid Ribonucleic acid
Structure Usually double-stranded Usually single-stranded
Main role Stores genetic information Important in gene expression and protein synthesis

📝 MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Which of the following is a monosaccharide?

A. Starch
B. Glycogen
C. Glucose
D. Cellulose

Answer: C. Glucose
2. Which carbohydrate is an animal storage carbohydrate?

A. Cellulose
B. Glycogen
C. Starch
D. Sucrose

Answer: B. Glycogen
3. Which carbohydrate is an important structural component of plants?

A. Glycogen
B. Glucose
C. Cellulose
D. Sucrose

Answer: C. Cellulose
4. What are the building blocks of proteins?

A. Nucleotides
B. Amino acids
C. Fatty acids
D. Glucose

Answer: B. Amino acids
5. Which bond joins amino acids in a polypeptide?

A. Hydrogen bond
B. Peptide bond
C. Ionic bond
D. Glycosidic bond

Answer: B. Peptide bond
6. Which lipid is an important component of biological membranes?

A. Phospholipid
B. Glucose
C. Glycogen
D. DNA

Answer: A. Phospholipid
7. Which molecule stores genetic information?

A. Lipid
B. Carbohydrate
C. DNA
D. Water

Answer: C. DNA
8. What is the basic unit of nucleic acids?

A. Amino acid
B. Fatty acid
C. Nucleotide
D. Glycerol

Answer: C. Nucleotide
9. Which molecule is involved in gene expression and protein synthesis?

A. RNA
B. Cellulose
C. Glycogen
D. Triglyceride

Answer: A. RNA
10. Which molecule contains carbohydrate and lipid components?

A. Glycoprotein
B. Glycolipid
C. Nucleotide
D. Amino acid

Answer: B. Glycolipid
11. Which molecule contains carbohydrate groups attached to protein?

A. Glycolipid
B. Glycoprotein
C. Triglyceride
D. Cellulose

Answer: B. Glycoprotein
12. What is the general structure of DNA?

A. Single helix
B. Double helix
C. Triple helix
D. Ring only

Answer: B. Double helix
13. Which scientists are associated with the DNA double-helix model?

A. Watson and Crick
B. Darwin and Wallace
C. Schleiden and Schwann
D. Mendel and Morgan

Answer: A. Watson and Crick
14. A gene is best described as:

A. A lipid molecule
B. A sequence of DNA nucleotides coding for a polypeptide
C. A carbohydrate molecule
D. A water molecule

Answer: B. A sequence of DNA nucleotides coding for a polypeptide
15. Which property makes water an effective solvent for many polar substances?

A. Its polarity
B. Its inability to form bonds
C. Its lack of electrical charges
D. Its insolubility

Answer: A. Its polarity
