Chapter Overview

Microbiology is the study of microscopic organisms and biological agents. This chapter focuses on two important groups: viruses and bacteria.

MDCAT Focus: Learn virus structure, viral replication, bacteriophages, bacterial structure, bacterial reproduction, useful and harmful bacteria, and antibiotics.

1. What are Viruses?

Viruses are acellular infectious agents that contain genetic material surrounded by a protein coat.

They cannot carry out independent reproduction and depend on host cells for replication.

  • Very small infectious agents
  • Acellular
  • Contain DNA or RNA as genetic material
  • Have a protein coat called a capsid
  • Replicate inside host cells
High-Yield Point: Viruses are generally described as obligate intracellular parasites because they depend on host cells for replication.

2. Structure of a Virus

A typical virus contains genetic material surrounded by a protective protein coat.

Main Components

  • Genetic material: DNA or RNA
  • Capsid: Protein coat surrounding the viral genetic material
  • Envelope: Present in some viruses and derived from host-cell membranes
Remember: A virus contains either DNA or RNA as its genome, not normally both as its genetic material.

3. Viral Genetic Material

Viral genomes can consist of DNA or RNA.

Depending on the virus, the genetic material may be single-stranded or double-stranded.

  • DNA viruses
  • RNA viruses
  • Single-stranded genomes
  • Double-stranded genomes

4. Viral Replication

Viruses use host-cell machinery to produce new viral components and assemble new virus particles.

General Steps

  1. Attachment to the host cell
  2. Entry into the host cell
  3. Release or access to viral genetic material
  4. Replication of viral genetic material
  5. Synthesis of viral proteins
  6. Assembly of new virus particles
  7. Release from the host cell
Exam Tip: Viral replication depends on the machinery and resources of the host cell.

5. Lytic Cycle

In a lytic cycle, a virus replicates inside a host cell and eventually causes the host cell to break open, releasing newly formed virus particles.

Main Events

  • Attachment
  • Entry
  • Viral replication
  • Assembly
  • Cell lysis
  • Release of new viruses

6. Lysogenic Cycle

In the lysogenic cycle, viral genetic material can become associated with the host cell's genetic material and remain relatively inactive for a period.

Under suitable conditions, the viral genetic material may become active and enter a productive replication cycle.

Remember: Lytic → active replication and cell lysis.
Lysogenic → viral genetic material can remain associated with host DNA before becoming active.

7. Bacteriophages

Bacteriophages, or phages, are viruses that infect bacteria.

  • They infect bacterial cells.
  • They contain viral genetic material.
  • They use bacterial cells to produce new phages.
MDCAT Tip: Bacteriophage = virus that infects bacteria.

8. Viruses and Disease

Many viruses cause diseases in humans, animals and plants.

Examples of Human Viral Diseases

  • Influenza
  • Measles
  • Hepatitis caused by hepatitis viruses
  • Polio
  • COVID-19

Different viruses target different host cells and tissues.

9. Prevention of Viral Diseases

Prevention depends on the particular virus and disease.

  • Vaccination where available
  • Good hygiene
  • Safe food and water practices
  • Respiratory precautions when appropriate
  • Control of disease vectors where relevant
Important: Antibiotics do not treat viral infections because antibiotics target bacterial processes rather than viruses.

10. What are Bacteria?

Bacteria are microscopic single-celled organisms belonging to the prokaryotes.

They do not have a membrane-bound nucleus.

  • Usually unicellular
  • Prokaryotic
  • No membrane-bound nucleus
  • DNA is located in the nucleoid region
  • Many have a cell wall

11. Structure of a Bacterial Cell

Bacterial cells are simpler than eukaryotic cells but contain structures necessary for survival and reproduction.

Important Structures

  • Cell membrane: Controls movement of substances.
  • Cell wall: Provides support and protection.
  • Cytoplasm: Contains cellular components.
  • Nucleoid: Region containing the main bacterial chromosome.
  • Ribosomes: Sites of protein synthesis.
  • Flagellum: May provide movement.
  • Capsule: Present in some bacteria and can provide protection.
High-Yield Point: Bacteria have ribosomes but do not have a membrane-bound nucleus.

12. Bacterial Shapes

Bacteria can occur in different shapes.

  • Coccus: Spherical or round
  • Bacillus: Rod-shaped
  • Spirillum: Spiral-shaped
  • Vibrio: Curved or comma-shaped

13. Bacterial Reproduction

Most bacteria reproduce asexually by a process called binary fission.

Binary Fission

  1. Bacterial DNA is replicated.
  2. The cell grows.
  3. The copies of DNA separate.
  4. The cell divides into two daughter cells.
Remember: Binary fission is an asexual method of reproduction in bacteria.

14. Useful Bacteria

Not all bacteria are harmful. Many bacteria are beneficial to humans and ecosystems.

  • Help in decomposition
  • Participate in nutrient cycles
  • Used in food production
  • Some live beneficially in the human digestive system
  • Some are important in biotechnology
  • Some bacteria help plants by participating in nitrogen cycling

15. Harmful Bacteria

Some bacteria are pathogenic and can cause diseases in humans, animals or plants.

Examples include bacteria associated with:

  • Tuberculosis
  • Cholera
  • Typhoid
  • Tetanus
  • Bacterial pneumonia
Important: Disease is caused by particular pathogenic bacteria; not all bacteria are harmful.

16. Antibiotics

Antibiotics are substances used to treat certain bacterial infections by killing bacteria or inhibiting their growth.

They do not work against viruses.

Why Antibiotic Resistance is Important

Bacteria can develop resistance to antibiotics. Incorrect or unnecessary antibiotic use can contribute to the spread of antibiotic-resistant bacteria.

Exam Tip: Antibiotics → bacterial infections.
Antibiotics are not effective against viral infections.

17. Viruses vs Bacteria

  • Viruses: Acellular infectious agents that require host cells for replication.
  • Bacteria: Living prokaryotic cells capable of independent cellular activities.
  • Viruses: Contain DNA or RNA as genetic material.
  • Bacteria: Have DNA as their main genetic material and contain ribosomes.
  • Antibiotics: Can act against susceptible bacteria but not viruses.

18. Quick Revision

  • Viruses are acellular infectious agents.
  • Viral genetic material can be DNA or RNA.
  • Capsid is the viral protein coat.
  • Some viruses have an envelope.
  • Viruses depend on host cells for replication.
  • Bacteriophages infect bacteria.
  • Bacteria are prokaryotic organisms.
  • Bacteria lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Bacterial DNA is located in the nucleoid region.
  • Ribosomes are present in bacteria.
  • Binary fission is a common bacterial reproductive method.
  • Not all bacteria are harmful.
  • Antibiotics act against susceptible bacteria.
  • Antibiotics do not treat viral infections.

19. MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Viruses are generally described as:

A) Multicellular organisms

B) Acellular infectious agents

C) Prokaryotic cells

D) Eukaryotic cells

Answer: B) Acellular infectious agents

2. The protein coat of a virus is called:

A) Capsule

B) Capsid

C) Cell wall

D) Nucleoid

Answer: B) Capsid

3. Viral genetic material may be:

A) Only DNA

B) Only RNA

C) DNA or RNA

D) Protein only

Answer: C) DNA or RNA

4. A virus that infects bacteria is called:

A) Protozoan

B) Bacteriophage

C) Fungus

D) Viroid

Answer: B) Bacteriophage

5. Which process commonly produces two bacterial daughter cells?

A) Budding

B) Binary fission

C) Meiosis

D) Fertilization

Answer: B) Binary fission

6. Bacteria are:

A) Eukaryotic

B) Prokaryotic

C) Multicellular animals

D) Acellular

Answer: B) Prokaryotic

7. Which structure is absent as a membrane-bound organelle in bacteria?

A) Ribosome

B) Cytoplasm

C) Nucleus

D) Cell membrane

Answer: C) Nucleus

8. The main bacterial chromosome is located in the:

A) Nucleus

B) Nucleoid region

C) Golgi apparatus

D) Lysosome

Answer: B) Nucleoid region

9. Which structure is involved in protein synthesis?

A) Ribosome

B) Capsule

C) Cell wall

D) Flagellum

Answer: A) Ribosome

10. Antibiotics are mainly used against:

A) Bacterial infections

B) All viral infections

C) Genetic disorders

D) Vitamin deficiency

Answer: A) Bacterial infections

11. Antibiotics generally do not work against:

A) Bacteria

B) Viruses

C) Some susceptible pathogens

D) Bacterial infections

Answer: B) Viruses

12. In the lytic cycle, the host cell eventually:

A) Becomes a plant

B) Divides by meiosis

C) Breaks open and releases new viruses

D) Produces chlorophyll

Answer: C) Breaks open and releases new viruses

13. Which bacterial shape is rod-like?

A) Coccus

B) Bacillus

C) Vibrio

D) Spirillum

Answer: B) Bacillus

14. Which statement is correct?

A) All bacteria are harmful

B) All viruses are beneficial

C) Some bacteria are beneficial

D) Antibiotics kill all viruses

Answer: C) Some bacteria are beneficial

15. Antibiotic resistance means that:

A) All bacteria become viruses

B) Bacteria can become less susceptible to an antibiotic

C) Viruses become bacteria

D) Antibiotics become vitamins

Answer: B) Bacteria can become less susceptible to an antibiotic

20. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Virus → acellular infectious agent.
  • Capsid → viral protein coat.
  • Virus genome → DNA or RNA.
  • Bacteriophage → virus that infects bacteria.
  • Bacteria → prokaryotic cells.
  • Nucleoid → region containing bacterial chromosome.
  • Binary fission → common bacterial reproduction.
  • Antibiotics → target susceptible bacteria.
  • Antibiotics do not treat viral infections.
Super High-Yield: If an MCQ asks, “Which agent requires a host cell for replication?” the key answer is VIRUS.
