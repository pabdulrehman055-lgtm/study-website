Unit 11: S- and P-Block Elements

Definition: The periodic table is divided into blocks according to the type of orbital receiving the last electron. The s-block contains elements in which the last electron enters an s orbital, while the p-block contains elements in which the last electron enters a p orbital.
MDCAT Focus: Learn periodic trends, atomic and ionic radii, ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity, bond energy, bond length, s-block and p-block elements, and important reactions of Groups I, II and IV.

1. Atomic Radius

Atomic radius is a measure of the size of an atom, commonly considered as approximately half the distance between the nuclei of two identical bonded atoms.

Atomic radius generally changes across periods and down groups.

Across a period: Atomic radius generally decreases from left to right because effective nuclear attraction increases.
Down a group: Atomic radius generally increases because new electron shells are added.

2. Ionic Radius

Ionic radius is the effective size of an ion in a crystal or ionic environment.

Cations are generally smaller than their parent atoms because they lose electrons.

Anions are generally larger than their parent atoms because they gain electrons.

Example:
Na⁺ is smaller than Na.
Cl⁻ is larger than Cl.

3. Covalent Radius

Covalent radius is approximately half the distance between the nuclei of two identical atoms joined by a covalent bond.

It is useful for comparing the sizes of atoms involved in covalent bonding.

4. Ionization Energy

Ionization energy is the minimum energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom in its ground state.
Ionization energy generally increases from left to right across a period and decreases down a group.

Smaller atoms generally hold their outer electrons more strongly, leading to higher ionization energy.

5. Electron Affinity

Electron affinity describes the energy change associated with adding an electron to an isolated gaseous atom.

Electron affinity is related to an atom's tendency to accept an electron.

Halogens generally have a strong tendency to gain one electron because doing so gives them a more stable valence-shell configuration.

6. Electronegativity

Electronegativity is the ability of an atom in a chemical bond to attract the shared electrons toward itself.
Electronegativity generally increases across a period and decreases down a group.
Important:
Fluorine is the most electronegative element.

7. Bond Energy

Bond energy is the energy required to break a chemical bond in the gaseous state.

A stronger bond generally requires more energy to break.

Higher bond energy generally indicates a stronger chemical bond.

8. Bond Length

Bond length is the average distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms.

For comparable bonds between the same atoms, stronger multiple bonds are generally shorter than single bonds.

General trend for comparable bonds:

Bond strength: Triple > Double > Single
Bond length: Single > Double > Triple

9. What is the S-Block?

The s-block consists mainly of Groups 1 and 2, where the differentiating electron enters an s orbital.
Group General Valence Configuration
Group 1 ns¹
Group 2 ns²
Group 1 elements are called alkali metals. Group 2 elements are called alkaline earth metals.

10. Group 1 — Alkali Metals

Group 1 elements have one valence electron and commonly form +1 ions.

Element Symbol Common Ion
Lithium Li Li⁺
Sodium Na Na⁺
Potassium K K⁺
Rubidium Rb Rb⁺
Cesium Cs Cs⁺
Alkali metals are highly reactive because their single valence electron can be removed relatively easily.

11. Reaction of Group 1 Metals with Water

Alkali metals react with water to form a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas.

2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂
Example:

2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂
Reactivity of Group 1 metals generally increases down the group.

12. Reaction of Group 1 Metals with Oxygen

Alkali metals react with oxygen to form oxygen-containing compounds. The products can vary depending on the metal and reaction conditions.

For MDCAT, focus on the general reactivity and the common chemistry of alkali metals rather than memorizing one universal product for every Group 1 element.

13. Group 2 — Alkaline Earth Metals

Group 2 elements have two valence electrons and commonly form +2 ions.

Element Symbol Common Ion
Beryllium Be Be²⁺
Magnesium Mg Mg²⁺
Calcium Ca Ca²⁺
Strontium Sr Sr²⁺
Barium Ba Ba²⁺

14. Group 2 Reaction with Water

Many Group 2 metals react with water to form metal hydroxides and hydrogen.

M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂
Example:

Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂
Group 2 reactivity generally increases down the group.

15. What is the P-Block?

The p-block consists of elements in which the differentiating electron enters a p orbital.

The p-block contains metals, metalloids and non-metals and includes several important groups of the periodic table.

General outer configuration of p-block elements: ns²np¹–np⁶

16. Group 14 — Carbon Family

Group 14 is commonly called the carbon family.

Element Symbol
Carbon C
Silicon Si
Germanium Ge
Tin Sn
Lead Pb
Group 14 elements have four valence electrons and the general valence configuration is ns²np².

17. Carbon and Silicon

Carbon is a non-metal and forms a very large number of compounds. Silicon is a metalloid and is important in semiconductor technology.

Important compounds:

CO₂ → carbon dioxide
SiO₂ → silicon dioxide

18. General Periodic Trends

Property Across a Period Down a Group
Atomic radius Generally decreases Generally increases
Ionization energy Generally increases Generally decreases
Electronegativity Generally increases Generally decreases
Metallic character Generally decreases Generally increases
These are general trends. Some properties have important exceptions, so pay attention to the specific question.

19. Why Atomic Size Changes

Across a period, electrons are added to the same principal shell while nuclear charge increases. This generally increases the attraction between the nucleus and outer electrons.

Down a group, additional electron shells are added. Increased distance and shielding generally make the outer electrons farther from the nucleus.

Remember:
Across → stronger effective nuclear attraction → smaller atoms
Down → more shells and shielding → larger atoms

20. Shielding Effect

The shielding effect is the reduction in the attraction between the nucleus and an outer electron caused by electrons in inner shells.

Shielding generally becomes greater as the number of occupied electron shells increases.

21. Effective Nuclear Charge

Effective nuclear charge is the net positive attraction experienced by an electron after accounting for shielding by other electrons.
Across a period, effective nuclear charge generally increases, helping explain the decrease in atomic radius.

23. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Which groups mainly belong to the s-block?

A. Groups 1 and 2
B. Groups 13 and 14
C. Groups 15 and 16
D. Groups 17 and 18

Answer: A. Groups 1 and 2

2. The general valence configuration of Group 1 elements is:

A. ns²
B. ns¹
C. ns²np²
D. ns²np⁵

Answer: B. ns¹

3. Group 2 elements commonly form:

A. +1 ions
B. +2 ions
C. −1 ions
D. −2 ions

Answer: B. +2 ions

4. Atomic radius generally decreases:

A. Down a group
B. Across a period
C. From top to bottom
D. With increasing shells

Answer: B. Across a period

5. Atomic radius generally increases:

A. Across a period
B. Down a group
C. From right to left only
D. With increasing nuclear charge only

Answer: B. Down a group

6. Ionization energy generally increases:

A. Down a group
B. Across a period
C. With increasing atomic radius
D. With increasing shielding only

Answer: B. Across a period

7. Which element is the most electronegative?

A. Oxygen
B. Chlorine
C. Fluorine
D. Nitrogen

Answer: C. Fluorine

8. Which ion is generally smaller than its parent atom?

A. Na⁺ compared with Na
B. Cl⁻ compared with Cl
C. O²⁻ compared with O
D. N³⁻ compared with N

Answer: A. Na⁺ compared with Na

9. Group 14 elements have how many valence electrons?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

Answer: D. 4

10. The general outer configuration of p-block elements is:

A. ns¹
B. ns²
C. ns²np¹–np⁶
D. (n−1)d¹–d¹⁰

Answer: C. ns²np¹–np⁶

11. Reactivity of Group 1 metals generally:

A. Decreases down the group
B. Increases down the group
C. Remains exactly constant
D. Becomes zero

Answer: B. Increases down the group

12. Which factor generally increases down a group?

A. Number of occupied electron shells
B. Electronegativity
C. Ionization energy
D. Nuclear stability only

Answer: A. Number of occupied electron shells

13. Shielding effect is mainly caused by:

A. Inner electrons
B. Protons only
C. Neutrons only
D. Photons

Answer: A. Inner electrons

14. Which compound is formed when sodium reacts with water?

A. NaCl
B. NaOH and H₂
C. Na₂O only
D. Na₂CO₃

Answer: B. NaOH and H₂

15. A higher bond energy generally indicates:

A. A weaker bond
B. A stronger bond
C. No bond
D. Greater atomic radius

Answer: B. A stronger bond

24. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the basic periodic trends.
  • Remember atomic radius decreases across a period.
  • Remember atomic radius increases down a group.
  • Ionization energy generally increases across a period.
  • Electronegativity generally increases across a period.
  • Fluorine has the highest electronegativity.
  • Group 1 commonly forms +1 ions.
  • Group 2 commonly forms +2 ions.
  • Group 1 reactivity increases down the group.
  • Group 2 reactivity generally increases down the group.
  • Know the difference between atomic radius and ionic radius.
  • Remember cations are generally smaller than their atoms.
  • Remember anions are generally larger than their atoms.
  • Know the general configuration of s- and p-block elements.
  • Practice periodic-trend MCQs carefully because exceptions can be tested.
