Unit 3: Gases

Gases are highly compressible substances whose particles are in continuous random motion. Their behavior can be explained using the kinetic molecular theory and gas laws.

MDCAT Focus:

Focus on kinetic molecular theory, particle motion, STP, Boyle's law, Charles's law, absolute zero, the ideal gas equation and the difference between ideal and real gases.

1. Kinetic Molecular Theory of Gases

The kinetic molecular theory explains the behavior of gases in terms of the motion of their particles.

Main assumptions:
  • Gas particles are very small compared with the space between them.
  • Gas particles are in continuous random motion.
  • Particles move in straight lines between collisions.
  • Collisions between particles are approximately elastic.
  • There are weak attractive forces between ideal gas particles.
  • The average kinetic energy of gas particles depends on absolute temperature.

2. Motion of Gas Particles

Gas particles move continuously in different directions. Their motion produces collisions with the walls of the container.

These collisions produce the pressure exerted by the gas.

Remember:

Increasing temperature increases the average kinetic energy of gas particles.

3. Temperature and Kinetic Energy

Temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of particles in a gas.

Important:

Higher absolute temperature means greater average kinetic energy and faster average particle motion.

Gas-law calculations involving temperature should normally use the Kelvin scale.

4. Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP)

STP is a standard reference condition used in gas calculations.

Quantity Common STP Value
Temperature 273.15 K (0°C)
Pressure 1 atm
Note:

Always check which standard convention a question specifies when a molar gas volume is required.

5. Boyle's Law

Boyle's law describes the relationship between pressure and volume of a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature.

Statement:

At constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.

P ∝ 1/V

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Therefore, if volume decreases, pressure increases, provided temperature remains constant.

6. Boyle's Law Example

Suppose a gas has a volume of 4 L at a pressure of 2 atm. If the pressure becomes 4 atm at constant temperature, what is the new volume?

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

(2)(4) = (4)(V₂)

V₂ = 2 L

The volume decreases when pressure increases.

7. Charles's Law

Charles's law describes the relationship between volume and absolute temperature at constant pressure.

Statement:

At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed amount of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.

V ∝ T

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Temperature must be expressed in Kelvin for gas-law calculations.

8. Kelvin Temperature Scale

The Kelvin scale is the absolute temperature scale used in gas-law calculations.

K = °C + 273.15

For many basic MDCAT calculations, 273 may be used as an approximate conversion factor.

Example:

27°C ≈ 300 K

9. Absolute Zero

Absolute zero is the lowest theoretical temperature on the Kelvin scale.

Absolute zero = 0 K = −273.15°C

It represents the point at which a system has its minimum possible thermal energy.

10. Ideal Gas Equation

The gas laws can be combined into the ideal gas equation.

PV = nRT

P = Pressure
V = Volume
n = Number of moles
R = Gas constant
T = Absolute temperature
Important:

The units of P, V, n, R and T must be consistent.

11. Gas Constant R

R is called the universal gas constant. Its numerical value depends on the units used.

Units Approximate R Value
L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ 0.0821
J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ 8.314
MDCAT Tip:

Match the value and units of R with the units used for pressure and volume.

12. General Gas Relationship

When pressure, volume and temperature all change for a fixed amount of gas, the combined gas relationship can be used.

P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

This equation is useful when the amount of gas remains constant.

13. Ideal Gas

An ideal gas is a theoretical gas that obeys the gas laws perfectly under all conditions.

Ideal gas assumptions:
  • Particles have negligible volume.
  • Intermolecular attractions are negligible.
  • Collisions are elastic.
  • The gas obeys PV = nRT.

14. Real Gases

Real gases are actual gases and may deviate from ideal behavior, especially at high pressure and low temperature.

Why?

Real gas particles have finite volume and experience intermolecular attractions.

Ideal Gas Real Gas
Particle volume considered negligible Particles have finite volume
Attractions neglected Intermolecular attractions exist
Theoretical model Actual gases

15. Conditions Causing Deviation

Real gases show greater deviation from ideal behavior under conditions where particles are close together or moving less energetically.

Important:

Deviation from ideal behavior is generally greater at high pressure and low temperature.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Gas particles are in continuous random motion.
  • Average kinetic energy increases with absolute temperature.
  • Boyle's law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.
  • Charles's law: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂.
  • Temperature must be in Kelvin for gas-law calculations.
  • 0 K = −273.15°C.
  • Ideal gas equation: PV = nRT.
  • R = 0.0821 L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ when using L and atm.
  • Real gases deviate more at high pressure and low temperature.

📝 MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. According to kinetic molecular theory, gas particles are:

A) Completely stationary

B) In continuous random motion

C) Fixed in a lattice

D) Present only at the bottom of the container

Answer: B) In continuous random motion
2. Boyle's law relates pressure and:

A) Temperature

B) Volume

C) Number of atoms

D) Density only

Answer: B) Volume
3. Boyle's law is represented by:

A) V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

B) P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

C) PV = nRT

D) E = hν

Answer: B) P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
4. Charles's law relates volume to:

A) Absolute temperature

B) Mass

C) Atomic number

D) Density

Answer: A) Absolute temperature
5. Which temperature scale should normally be used in gas-law calculations?

A) Celsius

B) Fahrenheit

C) Kelvin

D) Rankine only

Answer: C) Kelvin
6. Absolute zero is approximately:

A) 0°C

B) −100°C

C) −273.15°C

D) 273°C

Answer: C) −273.15°C
7. The ideal gas equation is:

A) PV = nRT

B) P = mV

C) E = hν

D) P₁V₁ = P₂T₂

Answer: A) PV = nRT
8. The value of R is approximately 0.0821 when its units are:

A) J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹

B) L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹

C) g·mol⁻¹

D) atm·K

Answer: B) L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹
9. If the volume of a gas decreases at constant temperature, its pressure generally:

A) Decreases

B) Increases

C) Becomes zero

D) Remains unrelated

Answer: B) Increases
10. In Charles's law, pressure is kept:

A) Constant

B) Zero

C) Continuously increasing

D) Continuously decreasing

Answer: A) Constant
11. Real gases deviate more from ideal behavior at:

A) Low pressure and high temperature

B) High pressure and low temperature

C) Only 0°C

D) All conditions equally

Answer: B) High pressure and low temperature
12. The pressure of a gas is mainly produced by:

A) Particle collisions with container walls

B) Color of the gas

C) Atomic number

D) Molecular formula only

Answer: A) Particle collisions with container walls
13. If the absolute temperature of a gas increases, its average kinetic energy:

A) Increases

B) Decreases

C) Becomes zero

D) Remains unchanged

Answer: A) Increases
14. Which equation represents the combined gas relationship for a fixed amount of gas?

A) P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

B) E = hν

C) n = m/M

D) c = νλ

Answer: A) P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂
15. Which statement about an ideal gas is correct?

A) Its particles have significant volume

B) Strong attractions always exist

C) It obeys PV = nRT

D) Its particles are stationary

Answer: C) It obeys PV = nRT

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize Boyle's law and Charles's law.
  • Always convert Celsius to Kelvin when required.
  • Practice PV = nRT numerical questions.
  • Remember the units associated with R.
  • Understand the difference between ideal and real gases.
  • Remember that real-gas deviation becomes important at high pressure and low temperature.
