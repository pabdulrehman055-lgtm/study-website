Chapter 15: Plant Physiology & Transport

Plants need water, minerals, carbon dioxide and light to grow and carry out their life processes. Special tissues transport water, minerals and food throughout the plant.

MDCAT Focus: Learn xylem, phloem, transpiration, water movement, mineral transport, stomata and the factors affecting transpiration.

1. Transport in Plants

Plants transport substances between roots, stems and leaves. Two major vascular tissues are responsible for long-distance transport.

  • Xylem: transports water and mineral ions mainly from roots upward.
  • Phloem: transports organic nutrients, especially sugars, from sources to sinks.
Remember: Xylem → water and minerals
Phloem → food/sugars

2. Xylem

Xylem is a vascular tissue that conducts water and dissolved mineral ions from the roots to other parts of the plant.

Main Xylem Elements

  • Vessel elements
  • Tracheids
  • Xylem fibres
  • Xylem parenchyma

Vessel elements and tracheids are important for water conduction.

3. Phloem

Phloem transports organic substances such as sucrose from areas where they are produced or stored to areas where they are needed or stored.

Main Components

  • Sieve tube elements
  • Companion cells
  • Phloem parenchyma
  • Phloem fibres
Exam Point: Transport through phloem is called translocation.

4. Root Absorption

Roots absorb water and mineral ions from the soil. Root hairs greatly increase the surface area available for absorption.

Water Absorption

Water can enter root cells by osmosis when there is a suitable water potential gradient.

Mineral Ion Absorption

Mineral ions may enter roots by passive processes or by active transport when energy is required to move ions against an electrochemical gradient.

5. Osmosis in Plants

Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential toward a region of lower water potential.

Important: Osmosis involves the movement of water, not solute particles.

6. Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants, mainly through the stomata of leaves.

Importance of Transpiration

  • Helps create a transpiration stream.
  • Contributes to the movement of water and minerals.
  • Helps cool the plant.
  • Maintains water movement through the plant.

7. Stomata

Stomata are small pores mainly present in the epidermis of leaves. They regulate gas exchange and water loss.

Guard Cells

Each stoma is controlled by guard cells. Changes in guard-cell turgor can cause the stomatal pore to open or close.

  • Open stomata → increased gas exchange and water loss.
  • Closed stomata → reduced gas exchange and water loss.

8. Factors Affecting Transpiration

Temperature

Higher temperature generally increases evaporation and can increase transpiration.

Humidity

High humidity reduces the water-vapour gradient between the leaf and surrounding air, so transpiration generally decreases.

Wind

Moving air can remove humid air around the leaf and generally increases transpiration, provided stomata remain open.

Light

Light often promotes stomatal opening in many plants and can therefore increase transpiration.

Leaf Surface Area

Greater leaf area generally provides a larger surface for water loss.

9. Cohesion & Adhesion

Water molecules show cohesion because they attract each other. Adhesion is the attraction between water and other surfaces, such as the walls of xylem vessels.

  • Cohesion: water molecules attract each other.
  • Adhesion: water molecules are attracted to other surfaces.
These properties help maintain a continuous column of water within the xylem.

10. Transpiration Pull

Evaporation of water from leaves creates tension in the xylem. This helps pull water upward through the plant.

The continuous water column is supported by cohesion between water molecules and adhesion to xylem walls.

11. Mineral Nutrition

Plants require mineral elements for normal growth and metabolism. Some are needed in relatively large quantities while others are required in smaller amounts.

Examples of Essential Minerals

  • Nitrogen
  • Phosphorus
  • Potassium
  • Magnesium
  • Calcium
  • Iron

Nitrogen is important in amino acids and nucleic acids, while magnesium is an important component of chlorophyll.

12. Translocation

Translocation is the transport of organic solutes through the phloem.

Sucrose produced in photosynthesizing leaves can be transported to growing tissues, roots, fruits and storage organs.

Source: region supplying sugars.
Sink: region using or storing sugars.

13. Water Movement: Quick Comparison

  • Osmosis: movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane.
  • Transpiration: loss of water vapour from aerial parts.
  • Xylem transport: movement of water and mineral ions.
  • Translocation: movement of organic solutes through phloem.

Quick Revision

  • Xylem transports water and mineral ions.
  • Phloem transports organic nutrients.
  • Root hairs increase the surface area for absorption.
  • Osmosis involves movement of water.
  • Transpiration is loss of water vapour from aerial plant parts.
  • Stomata regulate gas exchange and water loss.
  • Guard cells control stomatal opening and closing.
  • Cohesion occurs between water molecules.
  • Adhesion occurs between water and other surfaces.
  • Transpiration pull helps move water through xylem.
  • Phloem transport is called translocation.
  • Magnesium is important for chlorophyll.

MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Which tissue transports water in plants?

A) Phloem

B) Xylem

C) Epidermis

D) Cortex

Answer: B) Xylem

2. Which tissue mainly transports sucrose?

A) Xylem

B) Phloem

C) Epidermis

D) Collenchyma

Answer: B) Phloem

3. Transpiration is the loss of:

A) Oxygen

B) Carbon dioxide

C) Water vapour

D) Glucose

Answer: C) Water vapour

4. Which structures control stomatal opening?

A) Root hairs

B) Guard cells

C) Xylem vessels

D) Sieve tubes

Answer: B) Guard cells

5. Osmosis involves the movement of:

A) Water

B) Proteins

C) DNA

D) Starch

Answer: A) Water

6. Which condition generally increases transpiration?

A) Very high humidity

B) Moving air

C) Complete darkness

D) Closed stomata

Answer: B) Moving air

7. Cohesion is the attraction between:

A) Water molecules

B) Water and soil particles only

C) Sugars and proteins

D) Minerals and starch

Answer: A) Water molecules

8. Adhesion refers to attraction between:

A) Water molecules only

B) Water and another surface

C) Glucose molecules only

D) DNA bases

Answer: B) Water and another surface

9. Which mineral is an important component of chlorophyll?

A) Sodium

B) Magnesium

C) Chlorine

D) Iodine

Answer: B) Magnesium

10. Transport of organic solutes through phloem is called:

A) Transpiration

B) Translocation

C) Respiration

D) Filtration

Answer: B) Translocation

11. Root hairs mainly help in:

A) Increasing absorption surface area

B) Producing seeds

C) Making flowers

D) Producing hormones only

Answer: A) Increasing absorption surface area

12. High humidity generally causes transpiration to:

A) Increase greatly

B) Decrease

C) Stop photosynthesis permanently

D) Produce more glucose directly

Answer: B) Decrease

13. The region that supplies sugars to the phloem is called:

A) Sink

B) Source

C) Stoma

D) Root hair

Answer: B) Source

14. Transpiration pull is associated mainly with:

A) Upward movement of water in xylem

B) Downward movement of DNA

C) Production of ATP in mitochondria

D) Digestion of proteins

Answer: A) Upward movement of water in xylem

15. Which statement is correct?

A) Xylem mainly transports sucrose.

B) Phloem only transports water.

C) Xylem transports water and mineral ions.

D) Stomata are found only in roots.

Answer: C) Xylem transports water and mineral ions.

Exam Tips 🎯

  • Never confuse xylem with phloem.
  • Remember: Xylem → water + minerals.
  • Remember: Phloem → sugars/organic nutrients.
  • Learn the definition of transpiration.
  • Understand the role of stomata and guard cells.
  • Know the difference between cohesion and adhesion.
  • Practice questions about factors affecting transpiration.
