Unit 15: Alkyl Halides

Definition: Alkyl halides are organic compounds in which a halogen atom is attached to an alkyl group.
MDCAT Focus: This unit covers nomenclature, structure, reactivity, nucleophilic substitution and elimination reactions of alkyl halides.

1. What Are Alkyl Halides?

Alkyl halides are derivatives of alkanes in which one or more hydrogen atoms have been replaced by halogen atoms.

The common halogens found in alkyl halides are:

  • Fluorine (F)
  • Chlorine (Cl)
  • Bromine (Br)
  • Iodine (I)
General representation: R–X

R = alkyl group
X = halogen
Examples:

CH₃Cl → Chloromethane
C₂H₅Br → Bromoethane
C₃H₇Cl → Chloropropane

2. Classification of Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides can be classified according to the carbon atom attached directly to the halogen.

Type Description Example
Primary (1°) Halogen-bearing carbon attached to one other carbon CH₃CH₂Cl
Secondary (2°) Halogen-bearing carbon attached to two other carbons CH₃CHClCH₃
Tertiary (3°) Halogen-bearing carbon attached to three other carbons (CH₃)₃CCl
Classification depends on the carbon directly attached to the halogen atom, not on the halogen itself.

3. Nomenclature of Alkyl Halides

  1. Select the longest carbon chain containing the carbon attached to the halogen.
  2. Number the chain to give substituents the lowest possible locants.
  3. Halogens are named as substituents.
  4. Use fluoro-, chloro-, bromo- and iodo- prefixes.
Halogen Prefix
F Fluoro-
Cl Chloro-
Br Bromo-
I Iodo-
Examples:

CH₃Cl → Chloromethane
CH₃CH₂Br → Bromoethane
CH₃CHClCH₃ → 2-Chloropropane

4. Structure and Polarity

The carbon-halogen bond is polar because carbon and halogens have different electronegativities.

Cδ⁺ — Xδ⁻

The halogen atom is relatively electron-rich, while the carbon attached to it becomes relatively electron-poor.

This polarity helps alkyl halides undergo reactions with nucleophiles.

5. Reactivity of Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides are reactive because the carbon-halogen bond can undergo cleavage during chemical reactions.

Two major reaction types studied in this unit are:

  • Nucleophilic substitution
  • Elimination

6. Nucleophiles

A nucleophile is an electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to an electron-deficient atom.

Nucleophiles can be negatively charged ions or neutral molecules containing available electron pairs.

Common nucleophiles include:

OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃ and H₂O
The nucleophile attacks the carbon atom bonded to the halogen.

7. Nucleophilic Substitution

In a nucleophilic substitution reaction, a nucleophile replaces the halogen atom in an alkyl halide.
R–X + Nu⁻ → R–Nu + X⁻
Example:

CH₃Br + OH⁻ → CH₃OH + Br⁻

Hydroxide replaces bromide.

8. Leaving Group

A leaving group is an atom or group that leaves the substrate during a reaction with the electron pair that formed its bond.

In alkyl halides, the halide ion can act as the leaving group.

The common halide leaving groups are: Cl⁻, Br⁻ and I⁻.

9. SN2 Reaction

SN2 means Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular. It occurs in a single concerted step.

The nucleophile attacks the carbon while the leaving group leaves at the same time.

Rate ∝ [Alkyl halide][Nucleophile]
SN2 reactions are especially favored by less hindered alkyl halides, such as primary alkyl halides.
Simplified representation:

Nu⁻ + R–X → R–Nu + X⁻

One-step process.

10. SN1 Reaction

SN1 means Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular. It proceeds through formation of a carbocation intermediate.

The reaction occurs in multiple steps.

Step 1:
R–X → R⁺ + X⁻

Step 2:
R⁺ + Nu → R–Nu
SN1 reactions are favored by alkyl halides that can form relatively stable carbocations, especially tertiary alkyl halides.

11. SN1 vs SN2

Feature SN1 SN2
Number of main steps Two or more One concerted step
Intermediate Carbocation No carbocation intermediate
Rate dependence Depends mainly on substrate Depends on substrate and nucleophile
Favored substrate Often tertiary Often primary

12. Carbocation Stability

A carbocation is a positively charged carbon species.

A useful general order of carbocation stability is:

3° > 2° > 1° > methyl

Greater alkyl substitution generally helps stabilize a carbocation.

This stability order is important for understanding SN1 reactions.

13. Elimination Reactions

In an elimination reaction, atoms or groups are removed from a molecule and a multiple bond is formed.
Alkyl halide → Alkene + HX
Example:

Bromoalkane + base → Alkene + HBr-related products

under suitable elimination conditions.

14. Dehydrohalogenation

Removal of a hydrogen halide from an alkyl halide is called dehydrohalogenation.

Alkyl halide → Alkene + HX

A hydrogen atom is removed from one carbon and the halogen is removed from an adjacent carbon, allowing a C=C bond to form.

Elimination reactions are an important method for preparing alkenes.

15. Factors Affecting Nucleophilic Substitution

  • Structure of the alkyl halide
  • Nature and strength of the nucleophile
  • Leaving group ability
  • Reaction conditions and solvent
  • Steric hindrance around the reactive carbon
Steric hindrance is particularly important for SN2 reactions because the nucleophile must approach the carbon atom directly.

16. Substitution vs Elimination

Feature Substitution Elimination
Main change One group replaces another Groups are removed and a multiple bond forms
Typical product Substituted organic compound Alkene
Common substrate Alkyl halide Alkyl halide
MDCAT Tip: Substitution replaces the leaving group, while elimination forms a double bond.

17. Important Points to Remember

R–X = Alkyl halide

R–X + Nu⁻ → R–Nu + X⁻

Alkyl halide → Alkene + HX
  • R represents an alkyl group.
  • X represents a halogen.
  • Alkyl halides contain a polar C–X bond.
  • Nucleophiles are electron-rich species.
  • SN2 is a one-step concerted reaction.
  • SN1 involves a carbocation intermediate.
  • Tertiary carbocations are generally more stable than primary carbocations.
  • Elimination reactions can produce alkenes.

18. Quick Revision

  • Alkyl halides have the general form R–X.
  • Common halogens are F, Cl, Br and I.
  • Alkyl halides can be primary, secondary or tertiary.
  • The carbon directly attached to the halogen determines classification.
  • Halogen prefixes are fluoro-, chloro-, bromo- and iodo-.
  • The C–X bond is polar.
  • Nucleophiles are electron-rich species.
  • Nucleophilic substitution replaces the halogen.
  • SN2 occurs in one concerted step.
  • SN1 involves a carbocation intermediate.
  • SN2 is favored by less hindered substrates.
  • SN1 is often favored by tertiary substrates.
  • General carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl.
  • Elimination produces an alkene.
  • Dehydrohalogenation removes HX.

19. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The general representation of an alkyl halide is:

A. R–OH
B. R–X
C. R–COOH
D. R–NH₂

Answer: B. R–X

2. Which of the following is a halogen?

A. Na
B. Ca
C. Br
D. Mg

Answer: C. Br

3. In CH₃CH₂Cl, the alkyl halide is:

A. Primary
B. Secondary
C. Tertiary
D. Aromatic

Answer: A. Primary

4. The prefix used for chlorine in organic nomenclature is:

A. Fluoro-
B. Chloro-
C. Bromo-
D. Iodo-

Answer: B. Chloro-

5. A nucleophile is generally:

A. Electron-rich
B. Electron-deficient
C. Always positively charged
D. Always a metal

Answer: A. Electron-rich

6. In nucleophilic substitution, the nucleophile replaces:

A. Hydrogen only
B. Carbon
C. The leaving group
D. All carbon atoms

Answer: C. The leaving group

7. SN2 reaction occurs mainly in:

A. One concerted step
B. Three unrelated steps
C. Only radical steps
D. No step

Answer: A. One concerted step

8. SN1 reactions involve formation of:

A. Carbanion
B. Carbocation
C. Free oxygen
D. Alkene only

Answer: B. Carbocation

9. Which carbocation is generally the most stable?

A. Methyl
B. Primary
C. Secondary
D. Tertiary

Answer: D. Tertiary

10. The usual carbocation stability order is:

A. 1° > 2° > 3°
B. 3° > 2° > 1°
C. Methyl > 3° > 2°
D. 2° > methyl > 3°

Answer: B. 3° > 2° > 1°

11. Elimination of HX from an alkyl halide generally produces:

A. Alkene
B. Alcohol only
C. Alkane only
D. Ether

Answer: A. Alkene

12. Dehydrohalogenation involves removal of:

A. H₂O
B. HX
C. O₂
D. NH₃

Answer: B. HX

13. Which reaction forms a new C=C bond?

A. Substitution
B. Elimination
C. Neutralization
D. Hydrolysis only

Answer: B. Elimination

14. SN2 reactions are particularly affected by:

A. Steric hindrance
B. Nuclear decay
C. Atomic mass only
D. Melting point only

Answer: A. Steric hindrance

15. Which statement is correct?

A. SN1 does not involve a carbocation
B. SN2 always has a carbocation intermediate
C. Elimination can produce an alkene
D. Alkyl halides contain no halogen

Answer: C. Elimination can produce an alkene

20. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember the general formula R–X.
  • Know the prefixes fluoro-, chloro-, bromo- and iodo-.
  • Identify primary, secondary and tertiary alkyl halides correctly.
  • Understand why the C–X bond is polar.
  • Know the definition of a nucleophile.
  • Remember that SN2 is a one-step reaction.
  • Remember that SN1 involves a carbocation.
  • Memorize the general carbocation stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl.
  • Understand the difference between substitution and elimination.
  • Remember that dehydrohalogenation can produce an alkene.
  • Practice identifying the type of alkyl halide from its structure.
