Unit 7: Reaction Kinetics

Definition: Chemical kinetics is the branch of chemistry that deals with the rate of chemical reactions and the factors that affect the rate.

Some chemical reactions occur very quickly, while others are slow. For example, an explosion occurs rapidly, whereas rusting of iron takes a much longer time.

MDCAT Focus: Learn rate of reaction, rate equation, factors affecting rate, order of reaction, rate constant, activation energy and activated complex.

1. Rate of Reaction

The rate of a reaction tells us how quickly reactants are consumed or products are formed.

General idea:
Rate = Change in concentration ÷ Change in time

For a reactant, concentration decreases with time. For a product, concentration increases with time.

Example:
If the concentration of a reactant decreases by 0.20 mol/L in 10 seconds, the average rate is:

Rate = 0.20 ÷ 10 = 0.02 mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹

2. Average Rate and Instantaneous Rate

Average Rate

Average rate is calculated over a particular time interval.

Average rate = Change in concentration / Change in time

Instantaneous Rate

Instantaneous rate is the rate of reaction at a particular moment in time.

Remember: Average rate considers a time interval, while instantaneous rate considers the rate at one specific moment.

3. Rate Equation

The rate equation shows how the rate of a reaction depends on the concentration of reactants.

General form:

Rate = k[A]m[B]n

Where:

  • k = rate constant
  • [A] = concentration of reactant A
  • [B] = concentration of reactant B
  • m and n = orders with respect to A and B
The powers in the rate equation determine the order of the reaction.

4. Factors Affecting Rate of Reaction

1. Concentration

Increasing the concentration of reactants generally increases the reaction rate because particles collide more frequently.

2. Temperature

Increasing temperature generally increases the reaction rate. Particles move faster and more particles have enough energy to overcome the activation energy.

3. Surface Area

A powdered solid usually reacts faster than a large piece of the same solid because powder has a greater surface area.

4. Catalyst

A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

5. Nature of Reactants

Different substances react at different rates because their chemical properties and bonding are different.

Factor General Effect
Concentration Higher concentration usually increases rate
Temperature Higher temperature usually increases rate
Surface area Greater surface area usually increases rate
Catalyst Increases rate by lowering activation energy
Nature of reactants Different substances react at different rates

5. Order of Reaction

The order of a reaction is the sum of the powers of the reactant concentrations in the experimentally determined rate equation.

If:

Rate = k[A]m[B]n

Overall order = m + n
Example:
If Rate = k[A]2[B], then:

Order = 2 + 1 = 3
Therefore, it is a third-order reaction.

6. Zero-Order Reaction

In a zero-order reaction, the rate does not depend on the concentration of the reactant.

Rate = k[A]0

Since A0 = 1:

Rate = k
Key Point: Increasing reactant concentration does not change the rate of an ideal zero-order reaction.

7. First-Order Reaction

In a first-order reaction, the rate is directly proportional to the concentration of one reactant.

Rate = k[A]

If the concentration of A doubles, the rate also doubles.

Example:
If Rate = k[A], then:

[A] doubles → Rate doubles

8. Second-Order Reaction

In a second-order reaction, the total power of reactant concentrations in the rate equation is 2.

Example:

Rate = k[A]2

Overall order = 2
If [A] doubles in Rate = k[A]², the rate becomes four times greater.

9. Rate Constant (k)

The rate constant is represented by k. It is a proportionality constant in the rate equation.

Important: The numerical value and units of k depend on the overall order of the reaction.

For a given reaction at a particular temperature, the rate constant has a characteristic value.

10. Activation Energy

Activation energy (Ea) is the minimum energy that reacting particles must possess for an effective collision and successful reaction.

Not every collision between particles produces a reaction. Particles must collide with sufficient energy and suitable orientation.

Higher activation energy generally means that fewer particles can overcome the energy barrier at a given temperature.

11. Activated Complex / Transition State

During a chemical reaction, reactants pass through a very high-energy unstable state called the activated complex or transition state.

Activated Complex: A temporary, unstable, high-energy arrangement of atoms formed during a chemical reaction.

The activated complex exists for a very short time before forming products or returning to reactants.

12. Effect of Catalyst

A catalyst changes the rate of a chemical reaction without being permanently consumed in the overall reaction.

Catalyst → Alternative pathway → Lower activation energy → Faster reaction
MDCAT Point: A catalyst lowers activation energy. It does not change the overall energy difference between reactants and products.

13. Temperature and Reaction Rate

When temperature increases, particles have greater average kinetic energy.

As a result, more particles can reach or exceed the activation energy required for reaction.

Simple idea:

Higher temperature → More energetic particles → More successful collisions → Faster reaction

14. Collision Theory

Collision theory explains chemical reactions in terms of collisions between reacting particles.

For a collision to produce a reaction:

  • Particles must collide.
  • They must have sufficient energy.
  • They must have suitable orientation.
Effective collision = collision with sufficient energy and suitable orientation that can lead to product formation.

15. Quick Revision

  • Chemical kinetics studies the rate of chemical reactions.
  • Reaction rate describes how quickly concentration changes.
  • Rate equation relates rate to reactant concentrations.
  • k represents the rate constant.
  • Reaction order is obtained from powers in the rate equation.
  • Zero-order: Rate = k.
  • First-order: Rate = k[A].
  • Second-order example: Rate = k[A]².
  • Higher temperature generally increases reaction rate.
  • Greater surface area generally increases rate for solids.
  • A catalyst provides an alternative pathway.
  • A catalyst lowers activation energy.
  • Activated complex is a temporary high-energy state.
  • Effective collisions require sufficient energy and proper orientation.

16. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Chemical kinetics deals with:

A. Atomic structure
B. Rate of chemical reactions
C. Nuclear structure
D. Periodic classification

Answer: B. Rate of chemical reactions

2. The rate constant is represented by:

A. R
B. K
C. k
D. E

Answer: C. k

3. In Rate = k[A]², the order with respect to A is:

A. Zero
B. One
C. Two
D. Three

Answer: C. Two

4. If Rate = k[A][B]², the overall order is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

Answer: C. 3

5. In a zero-order reaction, the rate is:

A. k[A]
B. k[A]²
C. k
D. k/[A]

Answer: C. k

6. Activation energy is the:

A. Energy released by products
B. Minimum energy required for an effective reaction
C. Energy of the catalyst only
D. Energy of the solvent

Answer: B. Minimum energy required for an effective reaction

7. A catalyst generally:

A. Increases activation energy
B. Lowers activation energy
C. Stops the reaction
D. Changes reactants into elements

Answer: B. Lowers activation energy

8. Increasing temperature generally causes reaction rate to:

A. Decrease
B. Increase
C. Become zero
D. Remain exactly unchanged

Answer: B. Increase

9. Powdering a solid reactant generally increases its:

A. Mass
B. Surface area
C. Molecular mass
D. Density

Answer: B. Surface area

10. The temporary high-energy state during a reaction is called:

A. Catalyst
B. Product
C. Activated complex
D. Solvent

Answer: C. Activated complex

11. For a first-order reaction, the rate equation is:

A. Rate = k
B. Rate = k[A]
C. Rate = k[A]²
D. Rate = k/[A]

Answer: B. Rate = k[A]

12. If the concentration of A doubles in Rate = k[A], the rate:

A. Becomes half
B. Doubles
C. Becomes four times
D. Becomes zero

Answer: B. Doubles

13. If the concentration of A doubles in Rate = k[A]², the rate becomes:

A. 2 times
B. 3 times
C. 4 times
D. 8 times

Answer: C. 4 times

14. An effective collision requires:

A. Low energy only
B. Sufficient energy and suitable orientation
C. No collision
D. Only high pressure

Answer: B. Sufficient energy and suitable orientation

15. Which factor can increase reaction rate by providing a lower-energy pathway?

A. Catalyst
B. Product
C. Solvent only
D. Inert gas

Answer: A. Catalyst

17. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the general rate equation: Rate = k[A]m[B]n.
  • Overall order = sum of the powers in the rate equation.
  • Remember zero, first and second-order rate equations.
  • Activation energy is the minimum energy needed for effective reaction.
  • A catalyst lowers activation energy.
  • Higher temperature generally means a faster reaction.
  • Greater surface area generally increases the rate of reaction of solids.
  • Do not confuse activated complex with catalyst.
  • Practice numerical questions involving rate equations.
  • Understand concepts instead of memorizing definitions only.
