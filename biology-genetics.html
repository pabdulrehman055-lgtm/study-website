1. What is Genetics?

Genetics is the branch of biology that deals with heredity and variation.

Heredity is the transmission of characteristics from parents to offspring.

Variation refers to differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

MDCAT Point: Genetics helps us understand how characteristics are transmitted from one generation to the next.

2. Important Genetic Terms

Term Meaning
Gene A unit of hereditary information located at a particular position on DNA.
Allele An alternative form of a gene.
Genotype The genetic makeup of an organism.
Phenotype The observable characteristics of an organism.
Homozygous Having two identical alleles, such as TT or tt.
Heterozygous Having two different alleles, such as Tt.
Dominant allele An allele that expresses its effect in a heterozygous condition in complete dominance.
Recessive allele An allele whose effect is masked by a dominant allele in a heterozygous condition.
Easy Memory:
Genotype = genetic makeup
Phenotype = physical/observable appearance

3. Gregor Mendel

Gregor Mendel is known as the Father of Genetics.

Mendel performed important inheritance experiments using Pisum sativum, commonly known as the pea plant.

He studied contrasting characteristics and used controlled crosses to understand how traits are inherited.

MDCAT Point:
Gregor Mendel → Father of Genetics
Experimental organism → Pea plant

4. Mendel's Law of Segregation

The two alleles of a gene separate from each other during gamete formation.

Therefore, each gamete receives only one allele from a pair.

One allele from each pair → Each gamete
Remember: Law of Segregation is also associated with the separation of allele pairs during gamete formation.

5. Law of Independent Assortment

The Law of Independent Assortment states that alleles of different genes can assort independently during gamete formation when the genes are independently assorting.

This law is demonstrated by a classic dihybrid cross.

MDCAT Point: Independent assortment applies when genes are not linked in a way that prevents independent assortment.

6. Monohybrid Cross

A monohybrid cross studies inheritance of one characteristic.

Suppose:

  • T = tall
  • t = short

Consider the cross:

Tt × Tt
T t
T TT Tt
t Tt tt

Genotypic Ratio

1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt

Phenotypic Ratio

If T is completely dominant:

3 Tall : 1 Short
Important MDCAT Ratio: Monohybrid phenotypic ratio = 3 : 1

7. Dihybrid Cross

A dihybrid cross studies the inheritance of two characteristics at the same time.

Under classic conditions of independent assortment, the F2 phenotypic ratio is:

9 : 3 : 3 : 1

This ratio is associated with Mendel's law of independent assortment.

Important: The 9:3:3:1 ratio assumes independently assorting genes and the appropriate Mendelian conditions.

8. Punnett Square

A Punnett square is a diagram used to predict possible allele combinations in offspring.

It can be used to calculate expected genotypes and phenotypes from a genetic cross.

Remember: A Punnett square shows probabilities. It does not guarantee the exact result for every family.

9. Gene Linkage

Gene linkage refers to the tendency of genes located close together on the same chromosome to be inherited together.

Linked genes do not always follow the expected independent assortment pattern.

MDCAT Point: Genes located close together on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together.

10. Crossing Over

Crossing over is the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during meiosis.

It produces new combinations of alleles and contributes to genetic variation.

Easy Memory:
Linkage → genes tend to stay together.
Crossing over → can separate linked genes and create new combinations.

11. Linkage vs Independent Assortment

Feature Independent Assortment Gene Linkage
Location Genes assort independently when conditions allow Genes are on the same chromosome
Inheritance Alleles of different genes can assort independently Linked genes tend to be inherited together
Crossing Over Can create new combinations Can break linkage and create recombinant combinations

12. Sex Determination in Humans

In the usual human XX/XY system, females normally have XX sex chromosomes and males normally have XY.

An egg normally carries an X chromosome. Sperm can carry either X or Y.

Egg Sperm Possible Chromosomes
X X XX
X Y XY
MDCAT Point: The sperm contributes either X or Y chromosome in the usual human XX/XY system.

13. Sex-Linked Inheritance

A trait controlled by a gene located on a sex chromosome is called a sex-linked trait.

Many important examples involve genes located on the X chromosome.

X-linked recessive traits are often more commonly expressed in males because males normally have only one X chromosome.

Important Examples: Red-green colour blindness and haemophilia are commonly studied X-linked recessive conditions.

14. X-Linked Recessive Inheritance

In X-linked recessive inheritance, the recessive allele is located on the X chromosome.

A male normally has only one X chromosome, so a recessive allele on his X chromosome can be expressed.

A female normally has two X chromosomes, so she generally needs the recessive allele on both X chromosomes to express an X-linked recessive condition.

Male: XY → one X chromosome
Female: XX → two X chromosomes

15. Hemophilia

Hemophilia is a genetic bleeding disorder associated with defective blood clotting.

Classic hemophilia is commonly studied as an X-linked recessive condition.

Because males normally have one X chromosome, an affected X-linked recessive allele can be expressed in a male.

MDCAT Point: Hemophilia → classic example of X-linked recessive inheritance.
Exam Focus: Be able to identify why X-linked recessive traits can appear more frequently in males and how such traits are inherited.

16. DNA and Genes

DNA stands for Deoxyribonucleic Acid. It stores genetic information.

A gene is a sequence of DNA that contains information for a functional product.

Genes are located on chromosomes.

Remember:
DNA → Genes → Chromosomes → Genetic Information

17. Mutation

A mutation is a change in genetic material.

Mutations may occur naturally or may be associated with mutagens such as certain chemicals or radiation.

Depending on the situation, mutations may be harmful, neutral, or beneficial.

Remember: Mutation = change in genetic material.

20. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Who is known as the Father of Genetics?

A) Charles Darwin

B) Gregor Mendel

C) Watson

D) Linnaeus

Answer: B) Gregor Mendel

2. An alternative form of a gene is called:

A) Chromosome

B) Allele

C) Genome

D) Ribosome

Answer: B) Allele

3. The genetic makeup of an organism is called:

A) Phenotype

B) Genotype

C) Variation

D) Mutation

Answer: B) Genotype

4. The observable characteristics of an organism are called:

A) Genotype

B) Phenotype

C) Alleles

D) Genes

Answer: B) Phenotype

5. The two alleles of a gene separate during:

A) Digestion

B) Gamete formation

C) Translation

D) Respiration

Answer: B) Gamete formation

6. The classic phenotypic ratio of a monohybrid cross under complete dominance is:

A) 1:1

B) 2:1

C) 3:1

D) 9:3:3:1

Answer: C) 3:1

7. The classic dihybrid phenotypic ratio under independent assortment is:

A) 1:1

B) 3:1

C) 1:2:1

D) 9:3:3:1

Answer: D) 9:3:3:1

8. Genes located close together on the same chromosome tend to show:

A) Linkage

B) Translation

C) Replication

D) Mutation only

Answer: A) Linkage

9. Crossing over occurs between:

A) Ribosomes

B) Homologous chromosomes

C) Cell membranes

D) Lysosomes

Answer: B) Homologous chromosomes

10. A typical human male has:

A) XX

B) XY

C) YY

D) XO

Answer: B) XY

11. Hemophilia is commonly studied as:

A) Autosomal dominant

B) X-linked recessive

C) Y-linked dominant

D) Mitochondrial inheritance

Answer: B) X-linked recessive

12. Which chromosome normally carries an X-linked recessive allele in a male?

A) Y only

B) X chromosome

C) Both X and Y

D) Neither

Answer: B) X chromosome

13. A change in genetic material is called:

A) Mutation

B) Translation

C) Respiration

D) Diffusion

Answer: A) Mutation

14. Which process can create new combinations of alleles?

A) Crossing over

B) Digestion

C) Transpiration

D) Diffusion

Answer: A) Crossing over

15. The genetic makeup of an organism is its:

A) Phenotype

B) Genotype

C) Variation

D) Environment

Answer: B) Genotype
