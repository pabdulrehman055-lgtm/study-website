Unit 17: Aldehydes and Ketones

Definition: Aldehydes and ketones are carbonyl compounds containing the carbonyl group, C=O.
MDCAT Focus: This unit covers nomenclature, structure, preparation and reactivity of aldehydes and ketones, including nucleophilic addition, reduction to alcohols and oxidation.

1. Carbonyl Group

The carbonyl group is a functional group in which carbon is double-bonded to oxygen.
Carbonyl group: C=O

The carbonyl group is present in both aldehydes and ketones.

The carbonyl carbon is electron-deficient compared with oxygen, so it can be attacked by nucleophiles.

2. Aldehydes

An aldehyde is an organic compound containing a carbonyl group attached to at least one hydrogen atom.
General structure: R–CHO
Examples:

HCHO → Methanal
CH₃CHO → Ethanal
CH₃CH₂CHO → Propanal

3. Ketones

A ketone is an organic compound in which the carbonyl carbon is attached to two carbon-containing groups.
General structure: R–CO–R′
Examples:

CH₃COCH₃ → Propanone
CH₃COCH₂CH₃ → Butan-2-one

4. Aldehydes vs Ketones

Feature Aldehyde Ketone
General structure R–CHO R–CO–R′
Carbonyl carbon Attached to at least one H Attached to two carbon groups
Carbonyl position At the end of the chain Usually within the carbon chain
Example Ethanal Propanone
Easy trick: Aldehyde = carbonyl at the end with H attached.
Ketone = carbonyl carbon attached to two carbon groups.

5. Nomenclature of Aldehydes

  1. Select the longest carbon chain containing the aldehyde group.
  2. The aldehyde carbon is automatically carbon number 1.
  3. Replace the ending -e of the alkane with -al.
Examples:

HCHO → Methanal
CH₃CHO → Ethanal
CH₃CH₂CHO → Propanal
CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO → Butanal
In an aldehyde, the carbonyl carbon of the –CHO group is carbon 1.

6. Nomenclature of Ketones

  1. Select the longest carbon chain containing the carbonyl group.
  2. Number the chain so the carbonyl carbon gets the lowest possible number.
  3. Replace the ending -e of the alkane with -one.
Examples:

CH₃COCH₃ → Propanone
CH₃COCH₂CH₃ → Butan-2-one
CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ → Pentan-3-one

7. Structure of the Carbonyl Group

The carbonyl carbon is bonded to oxygen by a double bond.

C = O

1 σ bond + 1 π bond

The carbonyl carbon is approximately trigonal planar in geometry.

Because of the polarity of C=O, the carbonyl carbon can behave as an electrophilic center.

8. Preparation of Aldehydes

One important method of preparing aldehydes is controlled oxidation of primary alcohols.

Primary alcohol
↓ controlled oxidation
Aldehyde
Ethanol → Ethanal

CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃CHO
Further oxidation of an aldehyde can produce a carboxylic acid.

9. Preparation of Ketones

Ketones can be prepared by oxidation of secondary alcohols.

Secondary alcohol
↓ oxidation
Ketone
Propan-2-ol → Propanone

CH₃CHOHCH₃ → CH₃COCH₃

10. Reactivity of Aldehydes and Ketones

The carbonyl group is highly important in determining the chemical behavior of aldehydes and ketones.

Important reactions include:

  • Nucleophilic addition
  • Reduction
  • Oxidation
The carbonyl carbon is the main reactive site in many reactions of aldehydes and ketones.

11. Nucleophilic Addition

Nucleophilic addition is a reaction in which a nucleophile attacks the electron-deficient carbonyl carbon and adds to the carbonyl compound.
C=O + Nu⁻ → C–O⁻
with addition of the nucleophile to carbon.

The carbonyl π bond is broken during the addition process and new bonds are formed.

Aldehydes and ketones commonly undergo nucleophilic addition because the carbonyl carbon is electrophilic.

12. Reduction of Aldehydes

Reduction of an aldehyde produces a primary alcohol.

Aldehyde
↓ reduction
Primary alcohol
Ethanal → Ethanol

CH₃CHO → CH₃CH₂OH
Remember:
Aldehyde → Primary alcohol

13. Reduction of Ketones

Reduction of a ketone produces a secondary alcohol.

Ketone
↓ reduction
Secondary alcohol
Propanone → Propan-2-ol

CH₃COCH₃ → CH₃CHOHCH₃
Remember:
Ketone → Secondary alcohol

14. Oxidation of Aldehydes

Aldehydes are readily oxidized to carboxylic acids.

Aldehyde
↓ oxidation
Carboxylic acid
Ethanal → Ethanoic acid

CH₃CHO → CH₃COOH
This oxidation is an important difference between aldehydes and ketones at the introductory level.

15. Oxidation of Ketones

Ketones are generally more resistant to oxidation than aldehydes under mild conditions.

MDCAT Point: Aldehydes are easily oxidized, whereas ketones generally resist mild oxidation.

16. Important Reaction Summary

Starting Compound Reaction Main Product
Primary alcohol Oxidation Aldehyde
Secondary alcohol Oxidation Ketone
Aldehyde Reduction Primary alcohol
Ketone Reduction Secondary alcohol
Aldehyde Oxidation Carboxylic acid

17. Important Formulae

Aldehyde: R–CHO

Ketone: R–CO–R′

Carbonyl group: C=O

Primary alcohol → Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid

Secondary alcohol → Ketone

Aldehyde → Primary alcohol (reduction)

Ketone → Secondary alcohol (reduction)

18. Quick Revision

  • Aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl group C=O.
  • Aldehydes have the general structure R–CHO.
  • Ketones have the general structure R–CO–R′.
  • The aldehyde suffix is -al.
  • The ketone suffix is -one.
  • The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic.
  • Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition.
  • Primary alcohols can be oxidized to aldehydes.
  • Secondary alcohols can be oxidized to ketones.
  • Reduction of aldehydes gives primary alcohols.
  • Reduction of ketones gives secondary alcohols.
  • Aldehydes can be oxidized to carboxylic acids.
  • Ketones are generally resistant to mild oxidation.

19. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The functional group common to aldehydes and ketones is:

A. Hydroxyl
B. Carbonyl
C. Carboxyl
D. Amino

Answer: B. Carbonyl

2. The general structure of an aldehyde is:

A. R–OH
B. R–CHO
C. R–CO–R′
D. R–COOH

Answer: B. R–CHO

3. The general structure of a ketone is:

A. R–CHO
B. R–OH
C. R–CO–R′
D. R–X

Answer: C. R–CO–R′

4. CH₃CHO is:

A. Methanal
B. Ethanal
C. Propanal
D. Propanone

Answer: B. Ethanal

5. CH₃COCH₃ is:

A. Methanal
B. Ethanal
C. Propanone
D. Propanal

Answer: C. Propanone

6. The suffix used for aldehydes is:

A. -ol
B. -one
C. -al
D. -oic acid

Answer: C. -al

7. The suffix used for ketones is:

A. -al
B. -one
C. -ol
D. -ene

Answer: B. -one

8. Oxidation of a primary alcohol can produce:

A. Aldehyde
B. Ketone only
C. Ether only
D. Alkene only

Answer: A. Aldehyde

9. Oxidation of a secondary alcohol generally produces:

A. Aldehyde
B. Ketone
C. Carboxylic acid directly
D. Alkane

Answer: B. Ketone

10. Reduction of an aldehyde produces:

A. Primary alcohol
B. Secondary alcohol
C. Ketone
D. Alkene

Answer: A. Primary alcohol

11. Reduction of a ketone produces:

A. Primary alcohol
B. Secondary alcohol
C. Aldehyde
D. Carboxylic acid

Answer: B. Secondary alcohol

12. Oxidation of an aldehyde generally produces:

A. Ketone
B. Carboxylic acid
C. Alkane
D. Ether

Answer: B. Carboxylic acid

13. Aldehydes and ketones commonly undergo:

A. Nucleophilic addition
B. Only substitution
C. Only elimination
D. Nuclear fission

Answer: A. Nucleophilic addition

14. The carbon atom of a carbonyl group is generally:

A. Electron-rich
B. Electrophilic
C. Always negatively charged
D. A halogen

Answer: B. Electrophilic

15. Which statement is correct?

A. Ketones have the structure R–CHO
B. Aldehydes contain no carbonyl group
C. Aldehydes can be oxidized to carboxylic acids
D. Ketones are always primary alcohols

Answer: C. Aldehydes can be oxidized to carboxylic acids

20. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize aldehyde: R–CHO.
  • Memorize ketone: R–CO–R′.
  • Know that both contain the carbonyl group C=O.
  • Remember aldehyde suffix: -al.
  • Remember ketone suffix: -one.
  • Primary alcohol → aldehyde on oxidation.
  • Secondary alcohol → ketone on oxidation.
  • Aldehyde → primary alcohol on reduction.
  • Ketone → secondary alcohol on reduction.
  • Aldehyde → carboxylic acid on oxidation.
  • Understand nucleophilic addition at the carbonyl carbon.
  • Do not confuse aldehydes with ketones when identifying structures.
