1. What is Reproduction?

Reproduction is the biological process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own kind.

Reproduction is essential for the continuation of species.

Two major types:
1. Asexual reproduction
2. Sexual reproduction

2. Asexual Reproduction

In asexual reproduction, new individuals are generally produced from a single parent without fusion of gametes.

The offspring are usually genetically very similar to the parent.

Examples

  • Binary fission
  • Budding
  • Fragmentation
  • Vegetative propagation
Remember: Asexual reproduction usually involves one parent and no gamete fusion.

3. Sexual Reproduction

Sexual reproduction involves the formation of reproductive cells called gametes and their fusion during fertilization.

Sexual reproduction contributes to genetic variation among offspring.

Male Gamete + Female Gamete → Zygote
MDCAT Point: Sexual reproduction involves gametes and fertilization.

4. Gametes

Gametes are reproductive cells that carry haploid genetic material.

Gamete Produced In Condition
Sperm Testes Haploid
Ovum Ovaries Haploid
Human cells: Body cells are normally diploid, while gametes are haploid.

5. Male Reproductive System

The male reproductive system produces sperm and male sex hormones.

Main Parts

  • Testes
  • Epididymis
  • Vas deferens
  • Urethra
  • Penis
  • Accessory glands

Testes

The testes are the male reproductive organs that produce sperm and the hormone testosterone.

Remember:
Testes → Sperm + Testosterone

6. Spermatogenesis

Spermatogenesis is the process of formation and maturation of sperm cells in the testes.

Sperm Structure

Part Function
Head Contains nucleus and acrosome.
Middle piece Contains many mitochondria to provide energy.
Tail Helps sperm movement.
MDCAT Tip: Spermatogenesis = formation of sperm.

7. Female Reproductive System

The female reproductive system produces female gametes and reproductive hormones and provides the site for development of the embryo and fetus.

Main Parts

  • Ovaries
  • Fallopian tubes
  • Uterus
  • Cervix
  • Vagina

Ovaries

The ovaries produce female gametes and hormones including estrogen and progesterone.

Remember:
Ovaries → Ova + Estrogen + Progesterone

8. Oogenesis

Oogenesis is the process of formation and maturation of female gametes.

In humans, oogenesis begins before birth, while maturation of oocytes continues during the reproductive years.

Easy Memory:
Spermatogenesis → Sperm formation
Oogenesis → Female gamete formation

9. Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle consists of recurring changes in the ovaries and uterus during the reproductive years.

Main Stages

  1. Menstruation
  2. Follicular phase
  3. Ovulation
  4. Luteal phase

The cycle is regulated by hormones including FSH, LH, estrogen and progesterone.

Ovulation: Release of an egg from an ovary.

10. Reproductive Hormones

Hormone Main Function
FSH Stimulates ovarian follicle development and supports sperm production.
LH Triggers ovulation and supports testosterone production in males.
Estrogen Important for female reproductive development and menstrual cycle regulation.
Progesterone Helps maintain the uterine lining after ovulation.
Testosterone Important for male reproductive development and sperm production.

11. Fertilization

Fertilization is the fusion of male and female gametes to form a diploid zygote.

In humans, fertilization normally occurs in the fallopian tube.

Sperm + Ovum → Zygote
MDCAT Point: Fertilization → Fallopian tube

12. Zygote, Embryo and Fetus

After fertilization, the zygote undergoes repeated cell divisions and develops into an embryo.

Later in development, the developing organism is called a fetus.

Implantation

Implantation is the attachment of the early embryo to the lining of the uterus.

Remember:
Fertilization → Fallopian tube
Implantation → Uterus

13. Placenta

The placenta is a temporary organ formed during pregnancy that connects the developing fetus with the mother.

Main Functions

  • Exchange of oxygen
  • Transfer of nutrients
  • Removal of fetal waste products
  • Hormone production
Maternal and fetal blood normally do not directly mix, although substances can be exchanged across the placenta.

14. Pregnancy

Pregnancy is the period during which an embryo and later fetus develops inside the uterus.

The uterus provides protection and support for the developing fetus.

The placenta is important for nutrient exchange, gas exchange, waste removal and hormone production.

15. Parturition

Parturition means childbirth.

Near the end of pregnancy, hormonal and muscular changes cause contractions of the uterus that help deliver the baby.

Oxytocin is an important hormone involved in uterine contractions during labour.

Remember: Parturition = Childbirth

16. Lactation

Lactation is the production and secretion of milk by mammary glands after childbirth.

Hormone Role
Prolactin Promotes milk production.
Oxytocin Helps milk ejection.
Easy Memory:
Prolactin → Production
Oxytocin → Ejection

17. Reproductive Health

Reproductive health involves physical, mental and social well-being related to the reproductive system.

Important Areas

  • Personal hygiene
  • Understanding reproductive biology
  • Prevention of reproductive infections
  • Appropriate medical care
  • Responsible reproductive decisions

18. Sexually Transmitted Infections

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are infections that can be transmitted through sexual contact.

Examples

  • HIV infection
  • Syphilis
  • Gonorrhea
  • Chlamydia
Prevention: Accurate information, testing when indicated, appropriate medical care and safer sexual practices can reduce STI transmission.

19. Asexual vs Sexual Reproduction

Asexual Sexual
Usually one parent Usually involves two gametes
No fusion of gametes Fusion of gametes occurs
Usually less genetic variation Usually greater genetic variation
Generally simpler Generally more complex

20. Spermatogenesis vs Oogenesis

Spermatogenesis Oogenesis
Occurs in testes Occurs in ovaries
Produces sperm Produces female gametes
Begins at puberty Begins before birth, with maturation continuing during reproductive years
Produces functional sperm cells Produces a mature ovum and polar bodies during meiosis

22. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The main purpose of reproduction is:

A) Digestion

B) Continuation of species

C) Respiration

D) Excretion

Answer: B) Continuation of species

2. Which type of reproduction generally does not involve fusion of gametes?

A) Sexual reproduction

B) Asexual reproduction

C) Fertilization

D) Internal fertilization

Answer: B) Asexual reproduction

3. The male gamete in humans is:

A) Ovum

B) Zygote

C) Sperm

D) Embryo

Answer: C) Sperm

4. Which organ produces sperm and testosterone?

A) Ovary

B) Testis

C) Uterus

D) Kidney

Answer: B) Testis

5. Spermatogenesis is the process of:

A) Egg formation

B) Sperm formation

C) Fertilization

D) Implantation

Answer: B) Sperm formation

6. Ovulation is the:

A) Fusion of gametes

B) Release of an egg from an ovary

C) Formation of sperm

D) Implantation of embryo

Answer: B) Release of an egg from an ovary

7. Fertilization in humans normally occurs in the:

A) Uterus

B) Ovary

C) Fallopian tube

D) Vagina

Answer: C) Fallopian tube

8. The cell formed after fertilization is called:

A) Embryo

B) Fetus

C) Zygote

D) Gamete

Answer: C) Zygote

9. Implantation normally occurs in the:

A) Ovary

B) Uterus

C) Testis

D) Fallopian tube

Answer: B) Uterus

10. Which hormone promotes milk production?

A) Prolactin

B) Insulin

C) Glucagon

D) Thyroxine

Answer: A) Prolactin

11. Which hormone is important for uterine contractions during labour?

A) Oxytocin

B) Insulin

C) Glucagon

D) Melatonin

Answer: A) Oxytocin

12. Which hormone helps trigger ovulation?

A) LH

B) Insulin

C) Adrenaline

D) Melatonin

Answer: A) LH

13. Which structure provides exchange of nutrients and gases between mother and fetus?

A) Placenta

B) Ovary

C) Testis

D) Cervix

Answer: A) Placenta

14. Oogenesis refers to:

A) Formation of sperm

B) Formation and maturation of female gametes

C) Fertilization

D) Implantation

Answer: B) Formation and maturation of female gametes

15. Which hormone helps in milk ejection?

A) Oxytocin

B) FSH

C) Estrogen

D) Progesterone

Answer: A) Oxytocin

1. Testes → sperm + testosterone.

2. Ovaries → female gametes + estrogen + progesterone.

3. Spermatogenesis → sperm formation.

4. Oogenesis → female gamete formation.

5. Ovulation → release of egg.

6. Fertilization → fallopian tube.

7. Implantation → uterus.

8. Prolactin → milk production.

9. Oxytocin → milk ejection and uterine contractions.
