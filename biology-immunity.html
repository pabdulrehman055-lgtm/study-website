Chapter 14: Immunity & Defense

The immune system protects the body against harmful organisms and foreign substances such as bacteria, viruses and toxins.

MDCAT Focus: Understand types of immunity, immune cells, antibodies, antigens, vaccination and the body's defense mechanisms.

1. Body Defense

The human body has several defense mechanisms that protect it against pathogens.

First Line of Defense

  • Skin acts as a physical barrier.
  • Mucus traps microorganisms.
  • Cilia help remove particles from respiratory passages.
  • Tears help wash microorganisms away.
  • Stomach acid destroys many microorganisms.

Second Line of Defense

  • Phagocytosis
  • Inflammation
  • Fever
  • Antimicrobial substances

2. Innate Immunity

Innate immunity is the natural defense present from birth. It provides a rapid, non-specific response.

  • Skin and mucous membranes
  • Phagocytic cells
  • Inflammatory response
  • Fever
  • Natural killer cells
Remember: Innate immunity is generally non-specific and acts quickly.

3. Adaptive Immunity

Adaptive immunity develops after exposure to a particular antigen. It is specific and can produce immunological memory.

Main Types

  • Humoral immunity: mainly involves B lymphocytes and antibodies.
  • Cell-mediated immunity: mainly involves T lymphocytes.

4. Antigens

An antigen is a foreign substance that can trigger an immune response.

Examples include molecules present on the surface of microorganisms.

Exam Point: Antigen stimulates the immune system, while an antibody is a protein produced by B cells in response to specific antigens.

5. B Lymphocytes & Antibodies

B lymphocytes are important cells of humoral immunity. When activated, B cells can differentiate into plasma cells that produce antibodies.

Antibodies

  • Antibodies are proteins called immunoglobulins.
  • They bind specifically to antigens.
  • They can help neutralize pathogens or toxins.
  • They can help mark pathogens for destruction.

6. T Lymphocytes

T lymphocytes are involved mainly in cell-mediated immunity.

  • Helper T cells: help coordinate immune responses.
  • Cytotoxic T cells: can destroy infected body cells.
  • Memory T cells: contribute to long-term immune memory.

7. Phagocytosis

Phagocytosis is the process in which certain white blood cells engulf and digest microorganisms or foreign particles.

Basic Steps

  1. Phagocyte recognizes the foreign particle.
  2. It surrounds and engulfs the particle.
  3. The particle is enclosed inside the cell.
  4. Digestive enzymes help destroy it.

8. Inflammation

Inflammation is a protective response to injury or infection.

Common signs include:

  • Redness
  • Heat
  • Swelling
  • Pain

Inflammation helps bring immune cells and other defensive substances to the affected area.

9. Active & Passive Immunity

Active Immunity

Active immunity develops when the person's own immune system produces a response to an antigen.

  • Can develop after infection.
  • Can develop after vaccination.
  • Usually produces immune memory.

Passive Immunity

Passive immunity occurs when ready-made antibodies are received from another source.

  • Provides immediate protection.
  • Usually temporary.
  • Does not normally create long-term immune memory in the recipient.

10. Vaccination

Vaccination exposes the immune system to an antigen or antigenic material in a way that stimulates protection without causing the intended disease.

Vaccines help the body develop immune memory so that a future exposure can produce a faster and stronger response.

Important: Vaccination is an example of artificially acquired active immunity.

11. Immune Memory

After an immune response, some B and T cells become memory cells. These cells can remain in the body and respond rapidly when the same antigen is encountered again.

This is one reason why vaccination can provide long-lasting protection against particular infections.

12. Immune Disorders

Allergy

An allergy is an excessive immune response to a normally harmless substance such as pollen, dust or certain foods.

Autoimmune Disease

In an autoimmune disease, the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissues.

Immunodeficiency

Immunodeficiency occurs when the immune system is unable to function properly, increasing susceptibility to infections.

Quick Revision

  • Innate immunity = natural, rapid and generally non-specific.
  • Adaptive immunity = specific and develops after exposure.
  • B cells are important for antibody-mediated immunity.
  • T cells are important for cell-mediated immunity.
  • Antigens trigger specific immune responses.
  • Antibodies bind specifically to antigens.
  • Phagocytosis destroys foreign particles by engulfment and digestion.
  • Active immunity involves the person's own immune response.
  • Passive immunity involves receiving ready-made antibodies.
  • Vaccination helps develop immune memory.

MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Which type of immunity is present from birth?

A) Adaptive immunity

B) Innate immunity

C) Passive immunity

D) Artificial immunity

Answer: B) Innate immunity

2. Which cells produce antibodies?

A) Red blood cells

B) Platelets

C) B lymphocytes

D) Neurons

Answer: C) B lymphocytes

3. Which cells are mainly involved in cell-mediated immunity?

A) T lymphocytes

B) Red blood cells

C) Platelets

D) Osteocytes

Answer: A) T lymphocytes

4. A substance that triggers a specific immune response is called:

A) Enzyme

B) Antigen

C) Hormone

D) Vitamin

Answer: B) Antigen

5. Which process involves engulfing microorganisms?

A) Diffusion

B) Osmosis

C) Phagocytosis

D) Transpiration

Answer: C) Phagocytosis

6. Vaccination mainly produces:

A) Artificial active immunity

B) Natural passive immunity

C) Natural active immunity only

D) No immune response

Answer: A) Artificial active immunity

7. Which cells help provide long-term immune memory?

A) Memory B and T cells

B) Red blood cells

C) Platelets

D) Muscle cells

Answer: A) Memory B and T cells

8. Passive immunity involves:

A) Production of new bones

B) Receiving ready-made antibodies

C) Production of red blood cells

D) Digestion of food

Answer: B) Receiving ready-made antibodies

9. Which is a common sign of inflammation?

A) Swelling

B) Increased bone length

C) Increased vision

D) Hair growth

Answer: A) Swelling

10. An autoimmune disease occurs when:

A) The immune system attacks its own tissues

B) The body produces more oxygen

C) The stomach stops producing acid

D) Bones become longer

Answer: A) The immune system attacks its own tissues

11. Antibodies are best described as:

A) Proteins involved in specific immune responses

B) Carbohydrates used for energy

C) Lipids in cell membranes

D) Nucleic acids

Answer: A) Proteins involved in specific immune responses

12. Which defense forms a physical barrier against pathogens?

A) Skin

B) Antibody

C) Neuron

D) Insulin

Answer: A) Skin

13. Which immunity is generally specific to a particular antigen?

A) Adaptive immunity

B) Innate immunity

C) Physical barrier

D) Mechanical defense

Answer: A) Adaptive immunity

14. Which cells can directly destroy infected body cells?

A) Cytotoxic T cells

B) Red blood cells

C) Platelets

D) Osteocytes

Answer: A) Cytotoxic T cells

15. What is an important benefit of immune memory?

A) Faster response to a previously encountered antigen

B) Slower digestion

C) Reduced oxygen transport

D) Increased bone formation

Answer: A) Faster response to a previously encountered antigen

Exam Tips 🎯

  • Remember the difference between innate and adaptive immunity.
  • Know the roles of B cells and T cells.
  • Learn antigen vs antibody carefully.
  • Active vs passive immunity is an important comparison.
  • Vaccination and immune memory are high-yield concepts.
  • Practice MCQs to identify confusing options quickly.
