Chapter 17: Animal Diversity & Classification

Learn the major groups of animals, their important characteristics, body organization and basic classification for MDCAT preparation.

1. What is Animal Diversity?

Animal diversity refers to the wide variety of animals found on Earth. Animals differ in body structure, habitat, feeding methods, reproduction and level of organization.

Scientists classify animals into groups according to their similarities and differences.

Important: Classification makes it easier to study the huge diversity of animals.

2. Basis of Animal Classification

Animals can be classified on the basis of several characteristics:

  • Body symmetry
  • Number of germ layers
  • Level of organization
  • Presence or absence of a coelom
  • Segmentation
  • Developmental pattern
  • Presence of notochord

3. Levels of Body Organization

Cellular Level

Cells are loosely arranged and do not form true tissues. Example: Sponges.

Tissue Level

Similar cells form tissues. Example: Cnidarians.

Organ Level

Different tissues form organs. Example: Flatworms.

Organ-System Level

Different organs work together as organ systems. Example: Annelids, arthropods and vertebrates.

4. Body Symmetry

Asymmetry

The body cannot be divided into similar halves through any plane. Sponges commonly show asymmetry.

Radial Symmetry

Body parts are arranged around a central axis. Cnidarians commonly show radial symmetry.

Bilateral Symmetry

The body can be divided into two similar right and left halves through one plane.

Bilateral symmetry is common in animals such as worms, arthropods and vertebrates.

5. Germ Layers

Diploblastic Animals

Diploblastic animals develop from two germ layers:

  • Ectoderm
  • Endoderm

Cnidarians are examples of diploblastic animals.

Triploblastic Animals

Triploblastic animals have three germ layers:

  • Ectoderm
  • Mesoderm
  • Endoderm

Most advanced animal groups are triploblastic.

6. Coelom

The coelom is a body cavity that develops within the mesoderm.

Acoelomate

Animals without a body cavity between the digestive tract and body wall. Example: flatworms.

Pseudocoelomate

Animals having a body cavity that is not completely lined by mesoderm. Example: roundworms.

Coelomate

Animals having a true coelom completely lined by mesoderm. Examples include annelids and vertebrates.

7. Major Invertebrate Groups

Porifera

  • Commonly called sponges.
  • Mostly aquatic.
  • Body contains many pores.
  • Cellular level of organization.
  • Usually asymmetrical.

Cnidaria

  • Mostly aquatic.
  • Radial symmetry.
  • Diploblastic.
  • Tissue level of organization.
  • Possess specialized stinging cells.

Platyhelminthes

  • Commonly called flatworms.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Triploblastic.
  • Acoelomate.
  • Some are parasitic.

Nematoda

  • Commonly called roundworms.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Triploblastic.
  • Pseudocoelomate.
  • Some species are parasites.

Annelida

  • Segmented worms.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Triploblastic.
  • True coelom.
  • Example: earthworm.

Mollusca

  • Soft-bodied animals.
  • Many have a shell.
  • Examples: snail, mussel and octopus.

Arthropoda

  • Largest animal phylum.
  • Jointed appendages.
  • Segmented body.
  • Exoskeleton is present.
  • Examples: insects, spiders and crustaceans.

Echinodermata

  • Marine animals.
  • Adults commonly show radial symmetry.
  • Possess a water vascular system.
  • Examples: starfish and sea urchins.

8. Chordates

Chordates are animals that possess certain characteristic structures at some stage of their development.

  • Notochord
  • Dorsal hollow nerve cord
  • Pharyngeal slits
  • Post-anal tail
MDCAT Point: The presence of a notochord is an important characteristic of chordates.

9. Vertebrates

Vertebrates are chordates that possess a vertebral column.

Fish

  • Aquatic vertebrates.
  • Respiration mainly through gills.
  • Usually ectothermic.

Amphibians

  • Can live in water and on land.
  • Moist skin.
  • Many undergo metamorphosis.
  • Example: frog.

Reptiles

  • Usually terrestrial.
  • Dry, scaly skin.
  • Usually lay amniotic eggs.
  • Examples: snake, lizard and crocodile.

Birds

  • Body covered with feathers.
  • Forelimbs modified into wings.
  • Endothermic.
  • Lay eggs.

Mammals

  • Have mammary glands.
  • Usually have hair.
  • Endothermic.
  • Most give birth to young ones.

10. Quick Revision

  • Porifera → Sponges → Pores → Cellular level
  • Cnidaria → Stinging cells → Radial symmetry
  • Platyhelminthes → Flatworms → Acoelomate
  • Nematoda → Roundworms → Pseudocoelomate
  • Annelida → Segmented worms → True coelom
  • Arthropoda → Jointed appendages → Largest phylum
  • Echinodermata → Marine → Water vascular system
  • Chordata → Notochord
  • Vertebrates → Vertebral column

11. MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Which animal phylum is characterized by numerous pores?

A) Annelida

B) Porifera

C) Mollusca

D) Arthropoda

Answer: B) Porifera

2. Cnidarians are generally:

A) Triploblastic

B) Diploblastic

C) Acoelomate only

D) Coelomate

Answer: B) Diploblastic

3. Flatworms belong to:

A) Platyhelminthes

B) Nematoda

C) Annelida

D) Mollusca

Answer: A) Platyhelminthes

4. An acoelomate animal lacks:

A) Digestive system

B) Body cavity

C) Cells

D) Tissues

Answer: B) Body cavity

5. The largest animal phylum is:

A) Mollusca

B) Annelida

C) Arthropoda

D) Chordata

Answer: C) Arthropoda

6. Jointed appendages are characteristic of:

A) Arthropoda

B) Porifera

C) Cnidaria

D) Echinodermata

Answer: A) Arthropoda

7. Earthworm belongs to:

A) Annelida

B) Nematoda

C) Mollusca

D) Cnidaria

Answer: A) Annelida

8. Water vascular system is found in:

A) Arthropoda

B) Echinodermata

C) Mollusca

D) Annelida

Answer: B) Echinodermata

9. Which structure is characteristic of chordates?

A) Notochord

B) Exoskeleton

C) Shell

D) Water vascular system

Answer: A) Notochord

10. Vertebrates possess a:

A) Shell

B) Vertebral column

C) Water vascular system

D) Exoskeleton

Answer: B) Vertebral column

11. Which vertebrate group has feathers?

A) Reptiles

B) Amphibians

C) Birds

D) Mammals

Answer: C) Birds

12. Mammary glands are characteristic of:

A) Birds

B) Mammals

C) Reptiles

D) Amphibians

Answer: B) Mammals

13. Amphibians commonly have:

A) Moist skin

B) Feathers

C) Hair

D) Jointed exoskeleton

Answer: A) Moist skin

14. Which group contains roundworms?

A) Nematoda

B) Annelida

C) Mollusca

D) Porifera

Answer: A) Nematoda

15. A true coelom is completely lined by:

A) Ectoderm

B) Endoderm

C) Mesoderm

D) Epidermis

Answer: C) Mesoderm

12. Exam Tips 📝

  • Remember the main characteristics of each animal phylum.
  • Learn the difference between diploblastic and triploblastic animals.
  • Understand acoelomate, pseudocoelomate and coelomate conditions.
  • Remember Arthropoda as the largest animal phylum.
  • Remember the water vascular system of echinoderms.
  • Remember the four important chordate characteristics.
  • Practice classification-based MCQs regularly.
Quick Trick: Porifera = pores, Cnidaria = stinging cells, Annelida = segments, Arthropoda = jointed legs, Echinodermata = water vascular system.
