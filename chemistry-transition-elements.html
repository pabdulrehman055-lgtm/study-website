Unit 12: Transition Elements

Definition: Transition elements are elements associated with partially filled d-subshells in their atoms or in one or more of their ions.
MDCAT Focus: Learn the electronic structures of d-block elements and their ions, especially the general arrangement of electrons in the d-subshell.

1. The d-Block

The d-block elements are located in the middle of the periodic table, between the s-block and p-block.

General d-block configuration: (n−1)d¹–d¹⁰ ns⁰–ns²

The differentiating electron generally enters a d orbital of the shell one principal level below the outermost shell.

2. Why Are They Called Transition Elements?

They are called transition elements because they occupy the transition region between the s-block and p-block in the periodic table.

The term "transition element" is more specific than simply "d-block element." A strict definition considers partially filled d orbitals in the atom or in at least one oxidation state.

3. d Orbitals

A d subshell contains five orbitals.

One d subshell → 5 orbitals → maximum 10 electrons

Each orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons with opposite spins.

Remember: 5 d orbitals × 2 electrons = 10 electrons

4. General Electronic Configuration

The general electronic configuration of d-block elements can be represented as:

(n−1)d¹–d¹⁰ ns⁰–ns²

For the first transition series, the relevant orbitals are 3d and 4s.

Example:

Scandium (Sc):
[Ar] 3d¹ 4s²

Iron (Fe):
[Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²

MDCAT Tip: Chromium and copper are important exceptions to the simple Aufbau prediction because half-filled or completely filled d-subshell arrangements are especially stable.

6. Chromium Configuration

Chromium is an important exception.

Expected simplified arrangement: [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²

Actual ground-state arrangement: [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹

This arrangement provides a half-filled 3d subshell.

7. Copper Configuration

Copper is another important exception.

Expected simplified arrangement: [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s²

Actual ground-state arrangement: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹

A completely filled 3d subshell contributes to the stability of this arrangement.

8. Electronic Configuration of Transition Ions

When transition metals form positive ions, electrons are generally removed from the 4s orbital before the 3d orbital for the first transition series.

Example:

Fe: [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²

Fe²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁶

Fe³⁺: [Ar] 3d⁵
Very Important: Although 4s fills before 3d in many neutral atoms, the 4s electrons are generally removed first when transition-metal cations form.

9. Variable Oxidation States

Transition elements can often show more than one oxidation state because the energies of the ns and (n−1)d electrons are relatively close.

Example:
Iron commonly forms:
Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺
Variable oxidation states are an important characteristic of many transition elements.

10. Partially Filled d-Orbitals

Many characteristic properties of transition elements are related to their partially filled d orbitals.

  • Different oxidation states
  • Magnetic behavior
  • Formation of many compounds
  • Characteristic colors in many ions and compounds
For MDCAT, understand the connection between d-electron configuration and transition-element behavior.

11. Unpaired Electrons

Electrons occupying separate orbitals with parallel spins according to Hund's rule remain unpaired when orbitals are singly occupied.

The number of unpaired d-electrons is important when discussing magnetic behavior.

Example:
A d⁵ configuration has five unpaired electrons in the free-ion picture.

12. Paramagnetic and Diamagnetic Behavior

A substance with one or more unpaired electrons is generally paramagnetic.
A species with all electrons paired is generally diamagnetic.
Property Paramagnetic Diamagnetic
Unpaired electrons Present Absent
Magnetic field Attracted Weakly repelled

13. Example: Fe²⁺

Neutral iron has the configuration:

Fe = [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²

After losing two electrons:

Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶

A d⁶ configuration contains unpaired electrons in the free-ion arrangement, so Fe²⁺ is paramagnetic.

15. Why Transition Elements Show Different Oxidation States

The energy difference between the outer s electrons and the inner d electrons is relatively small. Therefore, different numbers of these electrons can participate in chemical bonding or be removed during ion formation.

Example:
Iron can form both Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ ions.

16. Important Configuration Rules

  • Each orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons.
  • Electrons occupy equal-energy orbitals singly before pairing.
  • Transition metals involve the ns and (n−1)d orbitals.
  • 4s electrons are generally removed before 3d electrons when first-row transition-metal ions form.
  • Chromium has an important 3d⁵ 4s¹ configuration.
  • Copper has an important 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ configuration.

17. Quick Revision

  • Transition elements are associated with partially filled d subshells.
  • The d-block lies between the s-block and p-block.
  • A d subshell contains 5 orbitals.
  • A d subshell can hold a maximum of 10 electrons.
  • General d-block configuration is (n−1)d¹–d¹⁰ ns⁰–ns².
  • Chromium: [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹.
  • Copper: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.
  • Transition elements commonly show variable oxidation states.
  • Unpaired electrons produce paramagnetic behavior.
  • Species with all electrons paired are diamagnetic.
  • Fe = [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s².
  • Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶.
  • Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵.
  • 4s electrons are generally removed before 3d electrons in first-row transition-metal ions.

18. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The d-block is located:

A. At the extreme left of the periodic table
B. In the middle of the periodic table
C. Only at the extreme right
D. Outside the periodic table

Answer: B. In the middle of the periodic table

2. How many orbitals are present in a d subshell?

A. 1
B. 3
C. 5
D. 7

Answer: C. 5

3. Maximum number of electrons in a d subshell is:

A. 2
B. 6
C. 8
D. 10

Answer: D. 10

4. The general configuration of d-block elements is:

A. ns¹
B. ns²np⁶
C. (n−1)d¹–d¹⁰ ns⁰–ns²
D. np⁶

Answer: C. (n−1)d¹–d¹⁰ ns⁰–ns²

5. The ground-state configuration of chromium is:

A. [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²
B. [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹
C. [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s⁰
D. [Ar] 3d³ 4s³

Answer: B. [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹

6. The ground-state configuration of copper is:

A. [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s²
B. [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s³
C. [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹
D. [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s⁴

Answer: C. [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹

7. How many d orbitals are present in a d subshell?

A. 2
B. 3
C. 5
D. 10

Answer: C. 5

8. Which ion has the configuration [Ar] 3d⁶?

A. Fe²⁺
B. Fe³⁺
C. Fe⁺
D. Mn²⁺

Answer: A. Fe²⁺

9. Fe³⁺ has the configuration:

A. [Ar] 3d⁴
B. [Ar] 3d⁵
C. [Ar] 3d⁶
D. [Ar] 3d⁷

Answer: B. [Ar] 3d⁵

10. A species containing unpaired electrons is generally:

A. Diamagnetic
B. Paramagnetic
C. Non-magnetic
D. Radioactive

Answer: B. Paramagnetic

11. A species with all electrons paired is generally:

A. Paramagnetic
B. Diamagnetic
C. Ferromagnetic only
D. Ionic

Answer: B. Diamagnetic

12. When first-row transition metals form cations, electrons are generally removed first from:

A. 3d
B. 4s
C. 4p
D. 3p

Answer: B. 4s

13. Which element commonly shows +2 and +3 oxidation states?

A. Iron
B. Neon
C. Fluorine
D. Helium

Answer: A. Iron

14. The d subshell can contain a maximum of:

A. 5 electrons
B. 8 electrons
C. 10 electrons
D. 14 electrons

Answer: C. 10 electrons

15. Variable oxidation states in transition elements are mainly related to:

A. Large energy difference between s and d electrons
B. Relatively close energies of s and d electrons
C. Absence of d electrons
D. Absence of valence electrons

Answer: B. Relatively close energies of s and d electrons

19. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember that a d subshell has 5 orbitals and holds 10 electrons.
  • Memorize the general d-block configuration.
  • Learn the chromium exception: 3d⁵ 4s¹.
  • Learn the copper exception: 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.
  • Remember that 4s electrons are generally removed first when first-row transition-metal ions form.
  • Practice Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ configurations.
  • Connect unpaired electrons with paramagnetism.
  • Remember that transition elements commonly show variable oxidation states.
  • Understand the difference between d-block elements and the stricter definition of transition elements.
