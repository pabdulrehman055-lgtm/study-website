Chapter 8: Ecology

Ecology is the branch of biology that studies the relationships between organisms and their environment.

MDCAT Definition: Ecology is the study of interactions among organisms and between organisms and their physical environment.

1. Levels of Ecological Organization

Ecology can be studied at different levels:

  1. Organism: A single living individual.
  2. Population: Members of the same species living in the same area.
  3. Community: Different populations living and interacting in an area.
  4. Ecosystem: A community plus its non-living environment.
  5. Biosphere: The global zone where life exists.
Remember: Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere

2. Habitat and Niche

Habitat

A habitat is the place or environment where an organism normally lives.

Example: A pond can be the habitat of fish and aquatic plants.

Niche

A niche describes the role and position of an organism in its environment, including how it obtains resources and interacts with other organisms.

Exam Point: Habitat = where an organism lives.
Niche = the role an organism performs in its ecosystem.

3. Biotic and Abiotic Factors

Biotic Factors

Biotic factors are the living components of an ecosystem.

  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Bacteria
  • Fungi
  • Other microorganisms

Abiotic Factors

Abiotic factors are the non-living physical and chemical components of the environment.

  • Temperature
  • Light
  • Water
  • Soil
  • Air
  • pH
  • Minerals

4. Population

A population consists of individuals of the same species living in the same geographical area at the same time.

Population Growth

Population size can change because of births, deaths, immigration and emigration.

  • Birth rate: Number of individuals born.
  • Death rate: Number of individuals that die.
  • Immigration: Individuals entering a population.
  • Emigration: Individuals leaving a population.

Carrying Capacity

Carrying capacity is the maximum population size that an environment can sustainably support with its available resources.

Important: Limited food, water, space and other resources can restrict population growth.

5. Community Interactions

Organisms in a community interact with one another in different ways.

Interaction Description Example
Competition Organisms compete for limited resources. Plants competing for sunlight
Predation One organism kills and eats another. Cat eating a mouse
Mutualism Both organisms benefit. Bees and flowering plants
Commensalism One benefits while the other is generally unaffected. Epiphytes growing on trees
Parasitism One benefits while the host is harmed. Tapeworm in humans

6. Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of living organisms interacting with one another and with the non-living environment.

Components of an Ecosystem

  • Biotic components: Living organisms.
  • Abiotic components: Non-living environmental factors.

Producers

Producers make their own food, usually through photosynthesis. Green plants are the major producers in many ecosystems.

Consumers

Consumers obtain energy by eating other organisms.

  • Primary consumers - usually herbivores
  • Secondary consumers - feed on primary consumers
  • Tertiary consumers - may feed on secondary consumers

Decomposers

Decomposers break down dead organic matter and help return nutrients to the environment.

Bacteria and fungi are important decomposers.

7. Food Chain

A food chain shows the transfer of food and energy from one organism to another.

Example:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk

The arrow shows the direction of energy transfer.

Trophic Levels

  1. Producers
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Secondary consumers
  4. Tertiary consumers

8. Food Web

A food web is a network of interconnected food chains in an ecosystem.

Because organisms often eat more than one type of food, several food chains can be connected to form a food web.

Exam Tip: Food chain = usually one pathway of energy transfer.
Food web = many interconnected pathways.

9. Energy Flow in an Ecosystem

Most ecosystems ultimately receive energy from the Sun. Producers capture light energy through photosynthesis.

Energy then passes from producers to consumers and eventually through decomposers.

10% Rule

On average, only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level becomes available to the next trophic level. The exact amount varies in real ecosystems.

Example:
If producers contain approximately 10,000 units of energy, the next trophic level may receive about 1,000 units.

Much energy is lost as heat through metabolic processes.

10. Ecological Pyramids

Ecological pyramids represent relationships between trophic levels.

Pyramid of Energy

It represents the amount of energy available at different trophic levels.

The energy pyramid is normally upright because energy decreases at higher trophic levels.

Pyramid of Biomass

It represents the total mass of living organisms at different trophic levels.

Pyramid of Numbers

It represents the number of organisms present at different trophic levels.

11. Water Cycle

Water continuously moves between the atmosphere, land and living organisms.

  1. Evaporation
  2. Condensation
  3. Precipitation
  4. Collection and runoff
  5. Infiltration and groundwater movement
Plants also release water vapor into the atmosphere through transpiration.

12. Carbon Cycle

Carbon is an essential element found in carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.

Important processes in the carbon cycle include:

  • Photosynthesis
  • Cellular respiration
  • Decomposition
  • Combustion

Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, while respiration and combustion release carbon dioxide.

13. Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen is required for important biological molecules such as proteins and nucleic acids.

Atmospheric nitrogen cannot be directly used by most organisms. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria help convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable nitrogen compounds.

Important Processes

  • Nitrogen fixation
  • Nitrification
  • Assimilation
  • Ammonification
  • Denitrification

14. Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of life present in an area or on Earth.

It includes diversity of genes, species and ecosystems.

Importance of Biodiversity

  • Provides food and medicines
  • Supports ecosystem stability
  • Provides useful biological resources
  • Maintains ecological relationships

15. Pollution

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or forms of energy into the environment.

Types of Pollution

  • Air pollution: Harmful gases and particles in air.
  • Water pollution: Harmful substances entering water.
  • Soil pollution: Contamination of soil.
  • Noise pollution: Excessive unwanted sound.
Pollution can harm organisms, reduce biodiversity and disturb ecosystem balance.

16. Conservation

Conservation means protecting natural resources, organisms and ecosystems for present and future generations.

Methods of Conservation

  • Protection of forests
  • Wildlife conservation
  • Establishment of national parks
  • Reducing pollution
  • Recycling
  • Using resources responsibly
  • Protecting endangered species

17. Renewable and Non-Renewable Resources

Renewable Resources Non-Renewable Resources
Solar energy Coal
Wind energy Oil
Hydropower Natural gas
Biomass Minerals that are available in limited amounts

Renewable resources can naturally replenish over relatively short periods, while non-renewable resources are limited and generally form much more slowly.

MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Ecology is the study of:

A) Cells only

B) Organisms and their environment

C) DNA only

D) Human anatomy only

Answer: B) Organisms and their environment

2. A group of individuals of the same species living in an area is called:

A) Community

B) Ecosystem

C) Population

D) Biosphere

Answer: C) Population

3. Which is an abiotic factor?

A) Bacteria

B) Grass

C) Temperature

D) Fungi

Answer: C) Temperature

4. Which organisms are the major producers in many ecosystems?

A) Green plants

B) Carnivores

C) Fungi

D) Parasites

Answer: A) Green plants

5. Which organisms are important decomposers?

A) Birds and mammals

B) Bacteria and fungi

C) Fish and reptiles

D) Grass and algae only

Answer: B) Bacteria and fungi

6. In mutualism:

A) Both organisms benefit

B) Both organisms are harmed

C) One benefits and the other is harmed

D) Neither organism is affected

Answer: A) Both organisms benefit

7. Approximately what percentage of energy is transferred to the next trophic level?

A) 1%

B) 10%

C) 50%

D) 90%

Answer: B) 10%

8. Which process removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere?

A) Photosynthesis

B) Respiration

C) Combustion

D) Decomposition

Answer: A) Photosynthesis

9. Which gas is especially important in the nitrogen cycle?

A) Oxygen

B) Nitrogen

C) Hydrogen

D) Helium

Answer: B) Nitrogen

10. The variety of life in an area is called:

A) Population density

B) Biodiversity

C) Carrying capacity

D) Succession

Answer: B) Biodiversity

MDCAT Exam Tip

Ecology MCQs often test definitions and differences. Remember these especially:

Population: Same species in an area.
Community: Different populations interacting.
Ecosystem: Community + abiotic environment.
Producer: Makes food.
Consumer: Gets food from other organisms.
Decomposer: Breaks down dead matter.
Energy: Generally decreases at higher trophic levels.
