Chapter 8: Ecology

Ecology is the branch of biology that studies interactions among organisms and between organisms and their physical environment.

MDCAT Definition: Ecology = study of interactions among organisms and between organisms and their environment.

1. Levels of Ecological Organization

Ecology can be studied at different levels:

  1. Organism: A single living individual.
  2. Population: Individuals of the same species living in the same area.
  3. Community: Different populations living and interacting in an area.
  4. Ecosystem: A community interacting with its non-living environment.
  5. Biosphere: The global zone of Earth where life exists.
Remember:
Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere

2. Habitat and Niche

Habitat

A habitat is the place or environment where an organism normally lives.

Niche

A niche describes the role and position of an organism in its environment, including how it uses resources and interacts with other organisms.

Exam Point:
Habitat = where an organism lives.
Niche = the role of an organism in its ecosystem.

3. Biotic and Abiotic Factors

Biotic Factors

Biotic factors are the living components of an ecosystem.

  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Bacteria
  • Fungi
  • Other microorganisms

Abiotic Factors

Abiotic factors are the non-living physical and chemical components of the environment.

  • Temperature
  • Light
  • Water
  • Soil
  • Air
  • pH
  • Minerals
Easy Trick: Biotic = living. Abiotic = non-living.

4. Population

A population consists of individuals of the same species living in the same geographical area at the same time.

Factors Affecting Population Size

Factor Meaning
Birth rate Number of individuals born in a population.
Death rate Number of individuals that die.
Immigration Movement of individuals into a population.
Emigration Movement of individuals out of a population.

Carrying Capacity

Carrying capacity is the maximum population size that an environment can sustainably support with its available resources.

Limited food, water, space and other resources can restrict population growth.

5. Community Interactions

Organisms in a community interact with one another in different ways.

Interaction Effect Example
Competition Organisms compete for limited resources. Plants competing for sunlight.
Predation One organism kills and eats another. Snake eating a mouse.
Mutualism Both organisms benefit. Bees and flowering plants.
Commensalism One benefits while the other is generally unaffected. Epiphytes growing on trees.
Parasitism One benefits while the host is harmed. Tapeworm in humans.

6. Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of living organisms interacting with one another and with the non-living environment.

Components

  • Biotic components: Living organisms.
  • Abiotic components: Non-living environmental factors.

Producers

Producers make their own food, usually through photosynthesis. Green plants are major producers in many ecosystems.

Consumers

Consumers obtain energy by eating other organisms.

  • Primary consumers — usually herbivores
  • Secondary consumers — feed on primary consumers
  • Tertiary consumers — may feed on secondary consumers

Decomposers

Decomposers break down dead organic matter and help return nutrients to the environment.

Bacteria and fungi are important decomposers.

7. Food Chain

A food chain shows a pathway through which food and energy pass from one organism to another.

Example:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk

The arrow indicates the direction of energy transfer.

Trophic Levels

  1. Producers
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Secondary consumers
  4. Tertiary consumers

8. Food Web

A food web is a network of interconnected food chains in an ecosystem.

Because organisms may consume more than one type of food, several food chains can be connected.

Exam Tip:
Food chain = one main pathway.
Food web = many interconnected pathways.

9. Energy Flow in an Ecosystem

Most ecosystems ultimately receive energy from the Sun. Producers capture light energy through photosynthesis.

Energy passes from producers to consumers through feeding relationships.

10% Rule

On average, only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level becomes available to the next trophic level. The exact amount varies among ecosystems.

Example:
10,000 energy units in producers → approximately 1,000 units available to the next trophic level.

Much energy is lost as heat during metabolic processes.

10. Ecological Pyramids

Ecological pyramids represent relationships between different trophic levels.

Pyramid Represents
Pyramid of Energy Energy available at each trophic level.
Pyramid of Biomass Total mass of living organisms at each trophic level.
Pyramid of Numbers Number of organisms at each trophic level.
MDCAT Point: The pyramid of energy is normally upright because energy decreases at higher trophic levels.

11. Water Cycle

Water continuously moves between the atmosphere, land, water bodies and living organisms.

  1. Evaporation
  2. Condensation
  3. Precipitation
  4. Runoff and collection
  5. Infiltration and groundwater movement
Transpiration: Plants release water vapour into the atmosphere through their aerial parts, mainly leaves.

12. Carbon Cycle

Carbon is an essential element found in carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.

Important Processes

  • Photosynthesis
  • Cellular respiration
  • Decomposition
  • Combustion

Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, while respiration and combustion release carbon dioxide.

Remember:
Photosynthesis → removes CO₂
Respiration → releases CO₂
Combustion → releases CO₂

13. Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen is required for important biological molecules, especially proteins and nucleic acids.

Most organisms cannot directly use atmospheric nitrogen gas. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria help convert nitrogen into forms that can enter biological systems.

Important Processes

  • Nitrogen fixation
  • Nitrification
  • Assimilation
  • Ammonification
  • Denitrification

14. Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of life present in an area or on Earth.

Biodiversity includes genetic diversity, species diversity and ecosystem diversity.

Importance

  • Provides food and medicines.
  • Supports ecosystem stability.
  • Provides biological resources.
  • Maintains ecological relationships.

15. Pollution and Environmental Problems

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or forms of energy into the environment.

Types of Pollution

Type Description
Air pollution Harmful gases and particles in the atmosphere.
Water pollution Harmful substances entering water bodies.
Soil pollution Contamination of soil by harmful substances.
Noise pollution Excessive unwanted sound.

Global Warming

An increase in the average temperature of Earth's climate system is called global warming. Increased concentrations of greenhouse gases contribute to this warming.

Pollution and environmental changes can damage ecosystems and reduce biodiversity.

16. Conservation

Conservation means protecting organisms, natural resources and ecosystems for present and future generations.

Methods of Conservation

  • Protection of forests
  • Wildlife conservation
  • National parks
  • Reducing pollution
  • Recycling
  • Responsible use of resources
  • Protection of endangered species

17. Renewable and Non-Renewable Resources

Renewable Resources Non-Renewable Resources
Solar energy Coal
Wind energy Oil
Hydropower Natural gas
Biomass Many mineral resources

Renewable resources can naturally replenish over relatively short periods, while non-renewable resources are limited and generally form much more slowly.

19. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Ecology is the study of:

A) Cells only

B) Organisms and their environment

C) DNA only

D) Human anatomy only

Answer: B) Organisms and their environment

2. A group of individuals of the same species living in an area is called:

A) Community

B) Ecosystem

C) Population

D) Biosphere

Answer: C) Population

3. Which is an abiotic factor?

A) Bacteria

B) Grass

C) Temperature

D) Fungi

Answer: C) Temperature

4. Which organisms are major producers in many ecosystems?

A) Green plants

B) Carnivores

C) Fungi

D) Parasites

Answer: A) Green plants

5. Which organisms are important decomposers?

A) Birds and mammals

B) Bacteria and fungi

C) Fish and reptiles

D) Grass and algae only

Answer: B) Bacteria and fungi

6. In mutualism:

A) Both organisms benefit

B) Both organisms are harmed

C) One benefits and the other is harmed

D) Neither is affected

Answer: A) Both organisms benefit

7. Approximately what percentage of energy is transferred to the next trophic level?

A) 1%

B) 10%

C) 50%

D) 90%

Answer: B) 10%

8. Which process removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere?

A) Photosynthesis

B) Respiration

C) Combustion

D) Decomposition

Answer: A) Photosynthesis

9. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria help convert:

A) Atmospheric nitrogen into usable nitrogen compounds

B) Oxygen into carbon dioxide

C) Carbon dioxide into oxygen

D) Water into glucose

Answer: A) Atmospheric nitrogen into usable nitrogen compounds

10. The variety of life in an area is called:

A) Population

B) Biodiversity

C) Carrying capacity

D) Habitat

Answer: B) Biodiversity

11. Which interaction benefits both organisms?

A) Parasitism

B) Competition

C) Mutualism

D) Predation

Answer: C) Mutualism

12. Which ecological pyramid is normally upright?

A) Pyramid of energy

B) Every pyramid of numbers

C) Every pyramid of biomass

D) None

Answer: A) Pyramid of energy

13. Which process releases carbon dioxide?

A) Photosynthesis

B) Cellular respiration

C) Nitrogen fixation

D) Transpiration

Answer: B) Cellular respiration

14. The maximum population size an environment can sustainably support is called:

A) Population density

B) Carrying capacity

C) Community

D) Niche

Answer: B) Carrying capacity

15. Which of the following is a renewable resource?

A) Coal

B) Oil

C) Solar energy

D) Natural gas

Answer: C) Solar energy

20. MDCAT Exam Tip

Important concepts to memorize:

Population = same species in the same area.

Community = different populations interacting.

Ecosystem = community + abiotic environment.

Producer = makes its own food.

Consumer = obtains food from other organisms.

Decomposer = breaks down dead organic matter.

Food chain = pathway of energy transfer.

Food web = interconnected food chains.

Energy generally decreases at higher trophic levels.
