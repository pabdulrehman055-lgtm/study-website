Prokaryotes

Prokaryotes are organisms whose cells do not have a membrane-bound nucleus. Their genetic material is located in a region called the nucleoid.

MDCAT Point: Bacteria are prokaryotic organisms. Their DNA is not enclosed inside a membrane-bound nucleus.

1. Prokaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells are generally smaller and structurally simpler than eukaryotic cells.

Main characteristics

  • No membrane-bound nucleus
  • DNA is present in the nucleoid region
  • No mitochondria or chloroplasts
  • Usually contain 70S ribosomes
  • Usually have a cell membrane
  • Many have a cell wall
  • Reproduce mainly by binary fission

2. Bacterial Cell Structure

Bacteria are the main examples of prokaryotic organisms studied in Biology.

Important structures

  • Capsule or slime layer in some bacteria
  • Cell wall
  • Cell membrane
  • Cytoplasm
  • Nucleoid
  • Ribosomes
  • Plasmids
  • Flagella in some bacteria
  • Pili or fimbriae in many bacteria

3. Nucleoid

The nucleoid is the region of a prokaryotic cell where the main bacterial chromosome is located.

Unlike the nucleus of a eukaryotic cell, the nucleoid is not surrounded by a nuclear membrane.

Remember: Prokaryote → nucleoid, not a membrane-bound nucleus.

4. Bacterial Cell Wall

The bacterial cell wall provides shape and protection. In most bacteria, the cell wall contains peptidoglycan.

The structure of the cell wall differs between major bacterial groups.

Gram-positive bacteria

Gram-positive bacteria generally have a thick peptidoglycan layer.

Gram-negative bacteria

Gram-negative bacteria have a thinner peptidoglycan layer and an additional outer membrane.

5. Capsule

Some bacteria have an outer capsule or slime layer. It can help protect bacteria from environmental conditions and may help them avoid host defenses.

6. Cell Membrane

The cell membrane forms a selective barrier around the bacterial cytoplasm.

It controls movement of substances into and out of the cell and contains proteins involved in important cellular processes.

7. Ribosomes

Bacterial cells contain 70S ribosomes. Ribosomes are responsible for protein synthesis.

MDCAT Tip: Prokaryotic ribosomes → 70S
Eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes → 80S

8. Plasmids

Plasmids are small, usually circular DNA molecules separate from the main bacterial chromosome.

Plasmids can carry genes that provide particular advantages. Some plasmids contain genes associated with antibiotic resistance.

9. Flagella

Some bacteria possess flagella, which are structures used mainly for movement.

Flagellar movement can help bacteria move toward favorable environments.

10. Pili and Fimbriae

Fimbriae are short surface structures that can help bacteria attach to surfaces or host cells.

Some specialized pili, such as the sex pilus, are involved in transfer of genetic material between bacterial cells.

Remember: Flagella → mainly movement
Fimbriae → attachment
Sex pilus → bacterial conjugation

11. Binary Fission

Most bacteria reproduce asexually through binary fission.

Basic steps

  1. The bacterial DNA is replicated.
  2. The cell grows.
  3. The two DNA copies move apart.
  4. The cell membrane and wall begin to divide.
  5. Two daughter cells are produced.
One bacterial cell → two daughter cells
This is called binary fission.

12. Bacterial Nutrition

Bacteria show a wide variety of nutritional modes.

Autotrophic bacteria

Some bacteria can produce organic food from inorganic substances. They may use light energy or energy obtained from chemical reactions.

Heterotrophic bacteria

Other bacteria obtain organic nutrients from their surroundings. They may be decomposers, parasites or live in mutualistic relationships.

13. Useful Bacteria

Many bacteria are beneficial to humans and ecosystems.

  • Decomposition of organic matter
  • Nutrient recycling
  • Nitrogen fixation
  • Production of some foods
  • Production of useful chemicals and medicines
  • Normal microbial communities in the human body

14. Harmful Bacteria

Some bacteria cause diseases in humans, animals or plants.

Bacterial diseases can be caused by direct bacterial activity or by toxins produced by bacteria.

Examples of bacterial diseases

  • Tuberculosis
  • Cholera
  • Typhoid
  • Tetanus
  • Diphtheria

15. Antibiotic Resistance

Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria acquire characteristics that allow them to survive antibiotics that would normally inhibit or kill them.

Resistance can spread through bacterial populations by natural selection and by transfer of genetic material.

Antibiotics act against bacteria, not viruses. Misuse and unnecessary use of antibiotics can contribute to antibiotic resistance.

16. Bacterial Conjugation

Conjugation is a process in which genetic material can be transferred from one bacterial cell to another, commonly through a conjugative pilus.

Plasmids are often involved in this transfer.

17. Prokaryotes vs Eukaryotes

  • Prokaryotes: no membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Eukaryotes: have a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Prokaryotic cells generally have 70S ribosomes.
  • Eukaryotic cytoplasm generally contains 80S ribosomes.
  • Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria and chloroplasts.
Easy Trick: Pro = simple cell structure.
Eu = true nucleus.

Quick Revision ⭐

  • Bacteria are prokaryotes.
  • Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • The bacterial chromosome is located in the nucleoid.
  • Most bacteria have a cell wall containing peptidoglycan.
  • Prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S.
  • Plasmids are small DNA molecules separate from the main chromosome.
  • Flagella help many bacteria move.
  • Fimbriae commonly help with attachment.
  • Sex pili can participate in conjugation.
  • Bacteria commonly reproduce by binary fission.
  • Some bacteria are autotrophic and others are heterotrophic.
  • Some bacteria are beneficial while others cause disease.
  • Antibiotic resistance is a major bacterial health concern.

MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Prokaryotic cells lack:

A) Ribosomes
B) Cell membrane
C) Membrane-bound nucleus
D) DNA

Answer: C) Membrane-bound nucleus

2. The main bacterial chromosome is found in the:

A) Nucleus
B) Nucleoid
C) Mitochondrion
D) Chloroplast

Answer: B) Nucleoid

3. Bacterial ribosomes are generally:

A) 30S
B) 50S
C) 70S
D) 80S

Answer: C) 70S

4. The bacterial cell wall commonly contains:

A) Chitin
B) Peptidoglycan
C) Cellulose only
D) Glycogen

Answer: B) Peptidoglycan

5. Plasmids are:

A) Protein molecules
B) Small DNA molecules
C) Ribosomes
D) Cell walls

Answer: B) Small DNA molecules

6. Which structure mainly helps many bacteria in movement?

A) Capsule
B) Flagellum
C) Ribosome
D) Plasmid

Answer: B) Flagellum

7. Binary fission produces:

A) Two daughter cells
B) Four daughter cells
C) One cell only
D) Spores only

Answer: A) Two daughter cells

8. Fimbriae are commonly involved in:

A) Photosynthesis
B) Attachment
C) DNA replication
D) Protein digestion

Answer: B) Attachment

9. Conjugation can transfer:

A) Genetic material between bacteria
B) Oxygen between bacteria
C) Water between bacteria
D) Ribosomes between bacteria

Answer: A) Genetic material between bacteria

10. Which is a bacterial disease?

A) Tuberculosis
B) Influenza
C) Measles
D) AIDS

Answer: A) Tuberculosis

11. Which statement about prokaryotes is correct?

A) They contain mitochondria
B) They have a membrane-bound nucleus
C) They lack membrane-bound organelles
D) They always contain chloroplasts

Answer: C) They lack membrane-bound organelles

12. Gram-positive bacteria generally have:

A) A thick peptidoglycan layer
B) No cell wall
C) A nucleus
D) Chloroplasts

Answer: A) A thick peptidoglycan layer

13. Which structure can help protect some bacteria and aid attachment?

A) Capsule
B) Nucleus
C) Mitochondrion
D) Chloroplast

Answer: A) Capsule

14. Antibiotics are primarily used against:

A) Viruses
B) Bacteria
C) All parasites
D) All fungi

Answer: B) Bacteria

15. Antibiotic resistance can become more common because of:

A) Natural selection
B) Photosynthesis
C) Digestion
D) Transpiration

Answer: A) Natural selection

Exam Tips 🎯

  • Remember: Prokaryotes have no membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Learn the difference between nucleoid and nucleus.
  • Remember 70S ribosomes in bacteria.
  • Peptidoglycan is an important component of bacterial cell walls.
  • Plasmids are small extra-chromosomal DNA molecules.
  • Flagella → movement.
  • Fimbriae → attachment.
  • Sex pilus → conjugation.
  • Binary fission → common bacterial reproduction.
  • Antibiotics do not treat viral infections.
