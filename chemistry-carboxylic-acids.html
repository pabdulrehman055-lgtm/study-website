Unit 18: Carboxylic Acids

Definition: Carboxylic acids are organic compounds containing the carboxyl group (–COOH).

The general formula of a carboxylic acid is:

R–COOH

The carboxyl group contains both a carbonyl group (C=O) and a hydroxyl group (–OH).

1. Carboxyl Group

The functional group of carboxylic acids is called the carboxyl group.

–COOH = –C(=O)OH

The carbon atom of the carboxyl group is attached to:

  • A carbonyl oxygen through a double bond.
  • An –OH group through a single bond.
  • An organic group represented by R.
Important: The –COOH group is responsible for the acidic properties of carboxylic acids.

2. Nomenclature of Carboxylic Acids

The names of simple carboxylic acids are obtained by replacing the final -e of the corresponding alkane with -oic acid.

Formula Name
HCOOH Methanoic acid
CH₃COOH Ethanoic acid
CH₃CH₂COOH Propanoic acid
CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH Butanoic acid
C₆H₅COOH Benzoic acid
Example:
CH₃CH₂COOH contains three carbon atoms, so its name is propanoic acid.

3. Structure and Polarity

The carboxyl group is strongly polar because the oxygen atoms are more electronegative than carbon and hydrogen.

R–C(=O)–OH

Carboxylic acids can form hydrogen bonds. This contributes to their relatively high boiling points compared with many hydrocarbons of similar molecular mass.

MDCAT Point: The –COOH group contains both C=O and O–H bonds.

4. Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids can donate the hydrogen of their –OH group and form a carboxylate ion.

R–COOH ⇌ R–COO⁻ + H⁺

The carboxylate ion is stabilized because its negative charge can be distributed over the two oxygen atoms.

Remember: Carboxylic acids are generally more acidic than alcohols.

5. Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

Oxidation of Primary Alcohols

A primary alcohol can be oxidized first to an aldehyde and then further to a carboxylic acid.

Primary alcohol → Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid
Example:
Ethanol → Ethanal → Ethanoic acid

Oxidation of Aldehydes

Aldehydes can be oxidized to the corresponding carboxylic acids.

R–CHO + [O] → R–COOH

6. Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

A. Reaction with Active Metals

Carboxylic acids react with some active metals to produce salts and hydrogen gas.

2R–COOH + 2Na → 2R–COONa + H₂

B. Reaction with Bases

Carboxylic acids react with bases in neutralization reactions.

R–COOH + NaOH → R–COONa + H₂O

C. Reaction with Carbonates

Carboxylic acids react with carbonates to produce salt, water and carbon dioxide.

2R–COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2R–COONa + H₂O + CO₂

D. Reaction with Bicarbonates

R–COOH + NaHCO₃ → R–COONa + H₂O + CO₂
MDCAT Tip: Reaction of a carboxylic acid with sodium bicarbonate produces visible bubbles of CO₂ gas.

7. Esterification

Carboxylic acids react with alcohols to form esters and water. This reaction is called esterification.

Carboxylic acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water

General reaction:

R–COOH + R′–OH ⇌ R–COOR′ + H₂O
Example:
Ethanoic acid + Ethanol ⇌ Ethyl ethanoate + Water
Esterification is a reversible reaction.

8. Derivatives of Carboxylic Acids

Important derivatives of carboxylic acids include:

Derivative General Formula
Acyl halide R–COX
Acid anhydride R–CO–O–CO–R
Ester R–COOR′

9. Acyl Halides

Acyl halides are derivatives in which the –OH group of a carboxylic acid is replaced by a halogen.

R–COOH → R–COX

Here X can be a halogen such as chlorine.

Example:
CH₃COCl is ethanoyl chloride.

10. Acid Anhydrides

Acid anhydrides contain two acyl groups connected through an oxygen atom.

R–CO–O–CO–R

They can be considered derivatives formed by removal of water from two carboxylic acid molecules.

11. Esters

Esters contain the functional group:

–COOR′

Esters are commonly formed by the reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.

General reaction:
R–COOH + R′–OH ⇌ R–COOR′ + H₂O

12. Reactivity of Carboxylic Acids

Important reactions to remember include:

  • Reaction with active metals.
  • Reaction with bases.
  • Reaction with carbonates.
  • Reaction with bicarbonates.
  • Esterification with alcohols.
  • Formation of carboxylic acid derivatives.
Exam Focus: Learn the functional groups and general formulas of carboxylic acids, acyl halides, acid anhydrides and esters.

Quick Revision

  • Functional group of carboxylic acids = –COOH
  • General formula = R–COOH
  • Methanoic acid = HCOOH
  • Ethanoic acid = CH₃COOH
  • Propanoic acid = CH₃CH₂COOH
  • Carboxylic acids are acidic because they can donate H⁺.
  • Primary alcohols can be oxidized to carboxylic acids.
  • Carboxylic acids react with bases to form salts and water.
  • Reaction with bicarbonate produces CO₂.
  • Carboxylic acid + alcohol → ester + water.
  • Acyl halide = R–COX
  • Acid anhydride = R–CO–O–CO–R
  • Ester = R–COOR′

15 MDCAT MCQs

1. What is the functional group of carboxylic acids?

A) –OH

B) –CHO

C) –COOH

D) –CO–

Answer: C) –COOH
2. What is the general formula of a carboxylic acid?

A) R–OH

B) R–COOH

C) R–CHO

D) R–X

Answer: B) R–COOH
3. HCOOH is called:

A) Ethanoic acid

B) Methanoic acid

C) Propanoic acid

D) Benzoic acid

Answer: B) Methanoic acid
4. CH₃COOH is:

A) Methanoic acid

B) Ethanoic acid

C) Propanoic acid

D) Butanoic acid

Answer: B) Ethanoic acid
5. Which compound is benzoic acid?

A) C₆H₆

B) C₆H₅OH

C) C₆H₅COOH

D) CH₃COOH

Answer: C) C₆H₅COOH
6. Oxidation of a primary alcohol can ultimately produce:

A) Alkane

B) Carboxylic acid

C) Ether

D) Alkyl halide

Answer: B) Carboxylic acid
7. Carboxylic acids react with NaOH to produce:

A) Ester + water

B) Salt + water

C) Alcohol + CO₂

D) Alkene + water

Answer: B) Salt + water
8. Reaction of a carboxylic acid with NaHCO₃ produces:

A) O₂

B) H₂

C) CO₂

D) N₂

Answer: C) CO₂
9. Esterification involves a carboxylic acid and:

A) Alkane

B) Alcohol

C) Amine

D) Alkene

Answer: B) Alcohol
10. The general formula of an ester is:

A) R–OH

B) R–COOH

C) R–COOR′

D) R–CHO

Answer: C) R–COOR′
11. The general formula of an acyl halide is:

A) R–COX

B) R–OH

C) R–COOH

D) R–X

Answer: A) R–COX
12. Which group makes carboxylic acids acidic?

A) Methyl group

B) Carboxyl group

C) Alkyl group

D) Ether group

Answer: B) Carboxyl group
13. Which gas is released when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium bicarbonate?

A) H₂

B) CO₂

C) O₂

D) Cl₂

Answer: B) CO₂
14. Esterification produces:

A) Ester and water

B) Salt and hydrogen

C) Alkene and water

D) Aldehyde and hydrogen

Answer: A) Ester and water
15. Which one is a carboxylic acid derivative?

A) Acyl halide

B) Alkane

C) Alkene

D) Alkyne

Answer: A) Acyl halide

Exam Tips 🎯

  • Memorize the –COOH functional group.
  • Know the names and formulas of common carboxylic acids.
  • Remember oxidation: primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid.
  • Remember the reaction with NaHCO₃ gives CO₂.
  • Know the esterification equation.
  • Learn the formulas of acyl halides, acid anhydrides and esters.
  • Practice identifying functional groups quickly.
