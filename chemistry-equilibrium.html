Official Chemistry Unit 6

Chemical Equilibrium

This StudyHub topic covers chemical equilibrium, reversible reactions, Le Chatelier's principle, solubility product, common ion effect, buffer solutions and the Haber process.

MDCAT Focus: Understand dynamic equilibrium and how concentration, pressure and temperature affect the position of equilibrium.

1. Chemical Equilibrium

Many chemical reactions are reversible. This means that products can react to form the original reactants.

Chemical equilibrium: A state in a reversible reaction where the forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates.

At equilibrium, the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant with time, although the reactions continue.

Equilibrium does not mean that the amounts of reactants and products are necessarily equal.

2. Reversible Reactions

A reversible reaction can proceed in both forward and reverse directions.

Reactants ⇌ Products

The double arrow (⇌) indicates that both directions are possible.

Example:

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

In the forward reaction, nitrogen and hydrogen form ammonia. In the reverse reaction, ammonia breaks down into nitrogen and hydrogen.

3. Dynamic Equilibrium

Chemical equilibrium is called dynamic because both forward and reverse reactions continue to occur.

At equilibrium:

  • Forward reaction continues.
  • Reverse reaction continues.
  • Rate of forward reaction = rate of reverse reaction.
  • Macroscopic concentrations remain constant.
At equilibrium:
Rate of forward reaction = Rate of reverse reaction

4. Equilibrium Constant

For a general reversible reaction:

aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

The equilibrium constant in terms of concentration can be represented as:

Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ

Pure solids and pure liquids are generally not included in the equilibrium expression.

MDCAT Tip: The powers in the equilibrium expression come from the coefficients in the balanced chemical equation.

5. Le Chatelier's Principle

Le Chatelier's principle: When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, the system shifts in a direction that tends to oppose the disturbance.

The major changes considered are:

  • Change in concentration
  • Change in pressure
  • Change in temperature

6. Effect of Concentration

If the concentration of a reactant is increased, the equilibrium tends to shift toward the products and consume some of the added reactant.

If the concentration of a product is increased, the equilibrium tends to shift toward the reactants.

Change General Equilibrium Shift
Add reactant Toward products
Remove reactant Toward reactants
Add product Toward reactants
Remove product Toward products

7. Effect of Pressure

Pressure changes are especially important for equilibrium systems containing gases.

Increasing pressure generally shifts equilibrium toward the side having fewer moles of gas.

Decreasing pressure generally shifts equilibrium toward the side having more moles of gas.

Example:

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)

Left side = 4 moles of gas
Right side = 2 moles of gas

Increasing pressure favors the right side.
If both sides contain the same number of gaseous moles, changing pressure does not shift the equilibrium position.

8. Effect of Temperature

Temperature affects equilibrium because heat can be considered as a reactant or product depending on whether the reaction is endothermic or exothermic.

Exothermic Reaction

Reactants ⇌ Products + Heat

Increasing temperature favors the reverse direction, while decreasing temperature favors the forward direction.

Endothermic Reaction

Reactants + Heat ⇌ Products

Increasing temperature favors the endothermic direction.

Temperature is the important condition that can change the value of the equilibrium constant.

9. Solubility Equilibrium

Some ionic compounds are only slightly soluble in water. They establish an equilibrium between the undissolved solid and its ions in solution.

Example:

AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

This type of equilibrium can be described using a solubility product constant.

10. Solubility Product (Ksp)

Solubility product constant (Ksp): The equilibrium constant for the dissolution of a sparingly soluble ionic compound.

For:

AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻

Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]

The concentration of the solid AgCl is not included in the Ksp expression.

Another Example

CaF₂(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺ + 2F⁻

Ksp = [Ca²⁺][F⁻]²
Remember: coefficients in the balanced equation become powers in the Ksp expression.

11. Common Ion Effect

Common ion effect: The decrease in the ionization or solubility of a weak electrolyte when a strong electrolyte containing a common ion is added.

The common ion shifts the equilibrium according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Example:

Consider a weak electrolyte producing ion X. Adding a soluble compound that also produces X increases the concentration of X and shifts the equilibrium toward the reactant side.
Common ion effect generally decreases the solubility of a sparingly soluble salt.

12. Buffer Solutions

Buffer solution: A solution that resists significant changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added.

Types of Buffers

  • Acidic buffer
  • Basic buffer

Acidic Buffer

An acidic buffer usually contains a weak acid and its conjugate base, commonly supplied by a salt of that weak acid.

Example:

Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) + sodium acetate (CH₃COONa)

Basic Buffer

A basic buffer commonly contains a weak base and its conjugate acid.

Example:

Ammonia (NH₃) + ammonium salt such as NH₄Cl
The main function of a buffer is to resist sudden changes in pH.

13. Haber Process

The Haber process is used industrially to manufacture ammonia.

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)

Important Conditions

  • High pressure
  • Elevated temperature
  • Iron catalyst

The reaction is reversible and exothermic. Because there are fewer gas molecules on the product side, increasing pressure favors ammonia formation.

MDCAT Focus: In the Haber process, high pressure favors NH₃ because the product side contains fewer moles of gas.

Role of Catalyst

A catalyst increases the rate at which equilibrium is reached. It does not change the final equilibrium position.

14. Catalyst and Equilibrium

A catalyst lowers the activation energy of both the forward and reverse reactions.

Catalyst → Faster forward reaction + Faster reverse reaction

Therefore, a catalyst helps the system reach equilibrium faster but does not shift the equilibrium position.

Remember: Catalyst does NOT change the equilibrium constant.

15. Quick Revision

  • Equilibrium occurs in a reversible reaction.
  • At equilibrium, forward rate = reverse rate.
  • Equilibrium is dynamic, not static.
  • Reactant and product concentrations remain constant at equilibrium.
  • Le Chatelier's principle predicts equilibrium shifts.
  • Increasing reactant concentration generally favors products.
  • Increasing product concentration generally favors reactants.
  • Increasing pressure favors the side with fewer gas moles.
  • Decreasing pressure favors the side with more gas moles.
  • Temperature can change the equilibrium constant.
  • Ksp describes equilibrium involving a sparingly soluble salt.
  • Common ion effect generally decreases solubility.
  • Buffers resist sudden changes in pH.
  • Haber process produces ammonia.
  • A catalyst helps equilibrium establish faster but does not shift it.

16. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. At chemical equilibrium:
  1. Reaction stops completely
  2. Forward rate equals reverse rate
  3. Only forward reaction occurs
  4. Only reverse reaction occurs
Answer: B
2. Chemical equilibrium is:
  1. Static
  2. Dynamic
  3. Impossible in gases
  4. Only found in solids
Answer: B
3. Le Chatelier's principle describes the response of an equilibrium system to:
  1. A disturbance
  2. Gravity only
  3. Light only
  4. Sound only
Answer: A
4. Increasing the concentration of a reactant generally shifts equilibrium:
  1. Toward reactants
  2. Toward products
  3. Nowhere
  4. Toward a solid only
Answer: B
5. Increasing pressure generally favors the side with:
  1. More moles of gas
  2. Fewer moles of gas
  3. More liquid
  4. More solid
Answer: B
6. For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, increasing pressure favors:
  1. N₂ and H₂
  2. NH₃
  3. Neither side
  4. Only H₂
Answer: B
7. The equilibrium constant expression for AgCl ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ is:
  1. [AgCl]
  2. [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]
  3. [Ag⁺]/[Cl⁻]
  4. [AgCl][Ag⁺]
Answer: B
8. Ksp is associated mainly with:
  1. Highly soluble gases
  2. Sparingly soluble ionic compounds
  3. Organic gases only
  4. Pure liquids only
Answer: B
9. The common ion effect generally:
  1. Increases solubility
  2. Decreases solubility
  3. Stops all reactions
  4. Changes an ion into an atom
Answer: B
10. A buffer solution resists changes in:
  1. Mass
  2. Volume
  3. pH
  4. Density only
Answer: C
11. Which is an example of an acidic buffer?
  1. CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa
  2. NaCl + water
  3. HCl + NaCl
  4. NaOH + KOH
Answer: A
12. The Haber process produces:
  1. Oxygen
  2. Ammonia
  3. Chlorine
  4. Carbon dioxide
Answer: B
13. The catalyst used in the Haber process is mainly:
  1. Iron
  2. Copper
  3. Silver
  4. Sodium
Answer: A
14. A catalyst at equilibrium:
  1. Changes the equilibrium constant
  2. Shifts equilibrium to products
  3. Helps equilibrium establish faster
  4. Stops the reverse reaction
Answer: C
15. For an exothermic equilibrium, increasing temperature generally favors:
  1. The exothermic direction
  2. The endothermic direction
  3. Both equally
  4. No reaction
Answer: B

17. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember: forward rate = reverse rate at equilibrium.
  • Equilibrium is dynamic, not static.
  • Use Le Chatelier's principle for concentration, pressure and temperature questions.
  • For gases, higher pressure generally favors fewer gas moles.
  • Learn the Ksp expression carefully.
  • Remember the common ion effect.
  • Know that buffers resist changes in pH.
  • Remember the Haber process equation and role of iron catalyst.
  • A catalyst does not change the equilibrium position.
Golden Point: In a reversible reaction at equilibrium, the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.
