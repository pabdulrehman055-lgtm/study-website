Chapter 10 — Coordination & Control

MDCAT Biology Notes, Quick Revision & Practice MCQs

1. What is Coordination?

Coordination is the process by which different parts of an organism work together and respond appropriately to internal and external changes.

In humans, coordination mainly occurs through:

  • Nervous coordination
  • Hormonal coordination
MDCAT Point: The nervous system generally produces rapid and short-lived responses, while hormones usually produce slower and longer-lasting effects.

2. Nervous System

The nervous system receives information, processes it and coordinates responses.

Main Divisions

  • Central Nervous System (CNS): Brain and spinal cord.
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS): Nerves connecting the CNS with the rest of the body.
Remember: CNS = Brain + Spinal cord.

3. Neuron

A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

Main Parts of a Neuron

  • Cell body: Contains the nucleus.
  • Dendrites: Receive signals.
  • Axon: Conducts nerve impulses away from the cell body.
  • Myelin sheath: Insulates many axons and helps increase impulse conduction speed.
  • Axon terminals: Release neurotransmitters.

4. Types of Neurons

Sensory Neuron

Carries information from receptors toward the central nervous system.

Relay Neuron

Connects neurons within the central nervous system.

Motor Neuron

Carries impulses from the central nervous system toward effectors such as muscles or glands.

Easy Order: Receptor → Sensory neuron → CNS → Motor neuron → Effector

5. Nerve Impulse

A nerve impulse is an electrical signal that travels along a neuron.

Communication between neurons or between neurons and effectors commonly involves chemical messengers called neurotransmitters.

Synapse

A synapse is the junction between two neurons or between a neuron and an effector cell.

Neurotransmitters carry chemical signals across the synaptic gap.

6. Brain

The brain is the major control center of the central nervous system.

Cerebrum

  • Conscious activities
  • Learning and memory
  • Thinking and reasoning
  • Interpretation of sensory information
  • Voluntary movement

Cerebellum

  • Balance
  • Posture
  • Coordination of movement

Medulla Oblongata

Helps control important involuntary activities such as breathing and heart rate.

7. Spinal Cord

The spinal cord connects the brain with many parts of the body and plays an important role in reflex actions.

MDCAT Point: Reflex responses can be coordinated through the spinal cord, allowing rapid responses to certain stimuli.

8. Reflex Action

A reflex action is a rapid and automatic response to a stimulus.

Reflex Arc

  1. Receptor detects stimulus.
  2. Sensory neuron carries the impulse.
  3. Integration occurs in the CNS.
  4. Motor neuron carries the response.
  5. Effector produces the response.
Example: Quickly withdrawing your hand after touching a hot object.

9. Endocrine System

The endocrine system consists of glands that release hormones into the bloodstream.

Hormones are chemical messengers that affect specific target cells or tissues.

Important Endocrine Glands

  • Pituitary gland
  • Thyroid gland
  • Parathyroid glands
  • Adrenal glands
  • Pancreas
  • Ovaries
  • Testes

10. Pituitary Gland

The pituitary gland is often called the master gland because it produces hormones that regulate several other endocrine functions.

Growth Hormone

Growth hormone promotes growth of bones and other body tissues.

Remember: The pituitary gland is closely associated with the hypothalamus and plays an important role in endocrine regulation.

11. Thyroid Gland

The thyroid gland produces thyroid hormones that help regulate metabolic activity and support normal growth and development.

Thyroxine

Thyroxine contributes to regulation of the body's metabolic rate.

Iodine is required for normal synthesis of thyroid hormones.

12. Pancreas

The pancreas has both endocrine and digestive functions.

Insulin

Insulin helps lower blood glucose concentration by promoting glucose uptake and storage.

Glucagon

Glucagon helps increase blood glucose concentration, especially by promoting breakdown of stored glycogen in the liver.

Easy Memory: Insulin ↓ blood glucose   |   Glucagon ↑ blood glucose

13. Adrenal Glands

Adrenal glands are located above the kidneys.

Adrenaline

Adrenaline is released during situations such as fear, excitement or stress.

It prepares the body for a rapid response by increasing cardiovascular activity and making more energy available.

14. Reproductive Hormones

Testosterone

Testosterone is the major male sex hormone and contributes to development of male reproductive structures and secondary sexual characteristics.

Estrogen

Estrogens are important female sex hormones involved in female reproductive development and regulation of the reproductive cycle.

Progesterone

Progesterone plays an important role in maintaining the uterine lining during the reproductive cycle and pregnancy.

15. Nervous vs Hormonal Coordination

  • Nervous system: Usually rapid, precise and relatively short-lasting.
  • Hormonal system: Usually slower, can affect multiple tissues and often has longer-lasting effects.
Both systems work together to maintain coordination and homeostasis in the body.

16. Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the maintenance of relatively stable internal conditions despite changes in the external environment.

Examples

  • Regulation of body temperature
  • Regulation of blood glucose
  • Maintenance of water balance
  • Regulation of internal chemical conditions
Key Point: Homeostasis usually involves feedback mechanisms.

17. Negative Feedback

In negative feedback, a change in a condition triggers responses that oppose the original change and help restore the normal range.

Example: Blood Glucose

When blood glucose rises, insulin helps lower it. When blood glucose falls, glucagon helps raise it.

Negative feedback is a major mechanism involved in maintaining homeostasis.

18. Quick Revision

  • CNS = brain + spinal cord.
  • PNS connects the CNS with the rest of the body.
  • Neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.
  • Dendrites generally receive signals.
  • Axon carries impulses away from the cell body.
  • Sensory neurons carry information toward the CNS.
  • Motor neurons carry signals toward effectors.
  • Cerebrum is involved in conscious activities and thinking.
  • Cerebellum helps coordinate movement and balance.
  • Medulla controls important involuntary functions.
  • Reflex actions are rapid and automatic.
  • Hormones are chemical messengers.
  • Insulin lowers blood glucose.
  • Glucagon raises blood glucose.
  • Homeostasis maintains stable internal conditions.

19. MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. The central nervous system consists of:

A) Brain and nerves

B) Brain and spinal cord

C) Spinal cord and muscles

D) Nerves and glands

Answer: B) Brain and spinal cord

2. The structural and functional unit of the nervous system is:

A) Nephron

B) Neuron

C) Alveolus

D) Hormone

Answer: B) Neuron

3. Which part of a neuron generally receives signals?

A) Dendrite

B) Axon

C) Myelin sheath

D) Axon terminal only

Answer: A) Dendrite

4. Sensory neurons carry information:

A) From CNS to muscles

B) Toward the CNS

C) From glands to muscles

D) Only inside the brain

Answer: B) Toward the CNS

5. Which part of the brain is mainly associated with balance and coordination?

A) Cerebrum

B) Cerebellum

C) Medulla

D) Spinal cord

Answer: B) Cerebellum

6. A rapid automatic response to a stimulus is called:

A) Hormonal response

B) Reflex action

C) Digestion

D) Homeostasis

Answer: B) Reflex action

7. Hormones are mainly:

A) Chemical messengers

B) Digestive enzymes

C) Structural proteins

D) Antibodies

Answer: A) Chemical messengers

8. Which hormone lowers blood glucose?

A) Glucagon

B) Insulin

C) Adrenaline

D) Thyroxine

Answer: B) Insulin

9. Which hormone helps increase blood glucose?

A) Insulin

B) Glucagon

C) Estrogen

D) Progesterone

Answer: B) Glucagon

10. Thyroxine is produced by the:

A) Pancreas

B) Thyroid gland

C) Adrenal gland

D) Pituitary gland

Answer: B) Thyroid gland

11. Adrenaline is associated with:

A) Emergency or stress responses

B) Digestion only

C) Bone formation only

D) Blood clotting only

Answer: A) Emergency or stress responses

12. Homeostasis means:

A) Complete absence of change

B) Maintenance of relatively stable internal conditions

C) Growth of the organism

D) Production of energy only

Answer: B) Maintenance of relatively stable internal conditions

13. Which mechanism commonly helps maintain homeostasis?

A) Negative feedback

B) Random mutation

C) Natural selection

D) Fertilization

Answer: A) Negative feedback

14. Which gland is often called the master gland?

A) Thyroid

B) Pituitary

C) Adrenal

D) Pancreas

Answer: B) Pituitary

15. Compared with hormonal coordination, nervous coordination is generally:

A) Slower

B) Faster

C) Always longer-lasting

D) Never specific

Answer: B) Faster

20. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember CNS and PNS definitions.
  • Know the functions of dendrites and axons.
  • Understand sensory, relay and motor neurons.
  • Learn the major functions of cerebrum, cerebellum and medulla.
  • Understand the reflex arc sequence.
  • Memorize important endocrine glands and hormones.
  • Remember insulin ↓ glucose and glucagon ↑ glucose.
  • Understand the concept of homeostasis and negative feedback.
  • Practice comparison-based MCQs.
