⚡ Unit 1: Vectors and Equilibrium

MDCAT Physics - Complete Notes & Practice MCQs

1. Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is a measurable property of a physical system. It is expressed using a numerical value and a unit.

Physical quantities can be divided into scalar and vector quantities.

2. Scalar Quantities

A scalar quantity has magnitude only. It does not have a specific direction.

Examples of Scalars

  • Mass
  • Time
  • Temperature
  • Distance
  • Speed
  • Energy
  • Work

3. Vector Quantities

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

Examples of Vectors

  • Displacement
  • Velocity
  • Acceleration
  • Force
  • Momentum

A vector is commonly represented by an arrow. The length of the arrow represents its magnitude and the arrowhead represents its direction.

4. Representation of a Vector

A vector can be represented by a directed line segment.

If a vector has magnitude A, it may be written as:

A⃗

The magnitude of a vector is represented by |A⃗|.

5. Addition of Vectors

Two or more vectors can be added to find their resultant vector.

Head-to-Tail Method

In the head-to-tail method, the tail of the second vector is placed at the head of the first vector.

The resultant vector is drawn from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last vector.

6. Resultant of Two Vectors

When two vectors act at an angle θ, their resultant magnitude can be calculated using:

R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cos θ)

Where A and B are the magnitudes of the two vectors.

7. Special Cases of Vector Addition

Same Direction

If two vectors act in the same direction:

R = A + B

Opposite Directions

If two vectors act in opposite directions:

R = |A - B|

Perpendicular Vectors

If two vectors are perpendicular:

R = √(A² + B²)

8. Resolution of a Vector

A vector can be resolved into two perpendicular components, usually along the x-axis and y-axis.

Ax = A cos θ

Ay = A sin θ

Here Ax is the horizontal component and Ay is the vertical component.

9. Unit Vector

A unit vector is a vector having magnitude equal to one. It is used to specify direction.

Common unit vectors are:

  • î → x-direction
  • ĵ → y-direction
  • k̂ → z-direction

10. Equilibrium

A body is said to be in equilibrium when the net force acting on it is zero.

ΣF = 0

An object in equilibrium has no acceleration due to the net force.

11. Conditions of Equilibrium

For translational equilibrium, the resultant force must be zero.

ΣFx = 0
ΣFy = 0

For complete mechanical equilibrium of a rigid body, the net torque must also be zero.

Στ = 0

12. Torque

Torque is the turning effect of a force about a pivot or axis.

τ = rF sin θ

Where r is the perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of action of the force.

13. Important Points for MDCAT

  • Scalar has magnitude only.
  • Vector has magnitude and direction.
  • Force is a vector quantity.
  • Speed is a scalar quantity.
  • Velocity is a vector quantity.
  • Resultant force is zero in equilibrium.
  • Perpendicular vector resultant uses Pythagoras theorem.
  • Unit vector has magnitude one.
  • Torque is the turning effect of force.
  • Complete equilibrium requires zero net force and zero net torque.

📝 MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Which of the following is a scalar quantity?

A) Force

B) Velocity

C) Speed

D) Displacement

Answer: C) Speed

2. Which quantity has both magnitude and direction?

A) Mass

B) Time

C) Temperature

D) Force

Answer: D) Force

3. Which of the following is a vector quantity?

A) Distance

B) Speed

C) Energy

D) Displacement

Answer: D) Displacement

4. The resultant of two vectors acting in the same direction is:

A) A - B

B) A + B

C) A × B

D) A ÷ B

Answer: B) A + B

5. If two vectors act in opposite directions, their resultant magnitude is:

A) A + B

B) AB

C) |A - B|

D) A/B

Answer: C) |A - B|

6. The magnitude of a unit vector is:

A) 0

B) 1

C) 2

D) 10

Answer: B) 1

7. Which unit vector represents the x-direction?

A) î

B) ĵ

C) k̂

D) n̂

Answer: A) î

8. For two perpendicular vectors A and B, the resultant is:

A) A + B

B) A - B

C) √(A² + B²)

D) AB

Answer: C) √(A² + B²)

9. A body is in translational equilibrium when:

A) Its velocity is always zero

B) Its acceleration is maximum

C) Net force is zero

D) Its mass is zero

Answer: C) Net force is zero

10. The condition for translational equilibrium is:

A) ΣF = 0

B) ΣF = ma

C) ΣF > 0

D) ΣF < 0

Answer: A) ΣF = 0

11. Torque represents the:

A) Heating effect of force

B) Turning effect of force

C) Gravitational effect

D) Magnetic effect only

Answer: B) Turning effect of force

12. The formula for torque is:

A) τ = rF sin θ

B) τ = mv

C) τ = ma

D) τ = F/r

Answer: A) τ = rF sin θ

13. For complete equilibrium of a rigid body:

A) Only net force must be zero

B) Only net torque must be zero

C) Net force and net torque must both be zero

D) Mass must be zero

Answer: C) Net force and net torque must both be zero

14. The horizontal component of a vector A making angle θ with x-axis is:

A) A sin θ

B) A cos θ

C) A tan θ

D) A/θ

Answer: B) A cos θ

15. The vertical component of a vector A making angle θ with x-axis is:

A) A cos θ

B) A sin θ

C) A tan θ

D) A + θ

Answer: B) A sin θ

🎯 Quick Revision

  • Scalar → magnitude only.
  • Vector → magnitude + direction.
  • Resultant same direction → R = A + B.
  • Resultant opposite direction → R = |A - B|.
  • Perpendicular vectors → R = √(A² + B²).
  • Unit vector → magnitude = 1.
  • Equilibrium → net force = 0.
  • Torque → turning effect of force.
  • τ = rF sin θ.
  • Complete equilibrium → ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0.
