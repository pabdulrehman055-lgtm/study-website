Chapter 2: Vectors & Equilibrium

Vectors

A vector quantity is a physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction.

Examples of Vector Quantities

  • Displacement
  • Velocity
  • Acceleration
  • Force
  • Momentum

Scalar Quantities

A scalar quantity has magnitude only and does not have a specific direction.

  • Distance
  • Speed
  • Mass
  • Time
  • Temperature

Vector Representation

A vector is commonly represented by an arrow. The length of the arrow represents its magnitude, while the arrowhead shows its direction.

Vector Addition

Vectors can be added to find their resultant vector.

Important Cases

  • For vectors in the same direction, their magnitudes are added.
  • For vectors in opposite directions, their magnitudes are subtracted.

Resultant Vector

The resultant vector is the single vector that represents the combined effect of two or more vectors.

Equilibrium

A body is said to be in equilibrium when the net force acting on it is zero.

Condition of Equilibrium

Net Force = 0

When the forces acting on a body balance each other, the body remains in equilibrium.

Types of Equilibrium

  • Static equilibrium
  • Dynamic equilibrium

📝 Practice MCQs

1. Which quantity has both magnitude and direction?

A) Mass

B) Time

C) Force

D) Temperature

Answer: C) Force

2. Which of the following is a scalar quantity?

A) Velocity

B) Force

C) Displacement

D) Speed

Answer: D) Speed

3. Which quantity is a vector?

A) Distance

B) Mass

C) Velocity

D) Time

Answer: C) Velocity

4. A body is in equilibrium when the net force is:

A) Maximum

B) Minimum

C) Zero

D) Infinite

Answer: C) Zero

5. Which of the following is a scalar quantity?

A) Acceleration

B) Momentum

C) Displacement

D) Mass

Answer: D) Mass

🎯 Quick Revision

  • Vector → magnitude + direction
  • Scalar → magnitude only
  • Force → vector quantity
  • Speed → scalar quantity
  • Velocity → vector quantity
  • Net Force = 0 → equilibrium
