Unit 2: Atomic Structure

Atomic structure explains the arrangement and behavior of particles inside an atom. This topic is important for understanding electronic configuration, periodic properties and chemical bonding.

MDCAT Focus:

Pay special attention to the proton, quantum theory, photons, quantum numbers, orbitals, hydrogen spectrum, electronic configuration, Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle and Hund's rule.

1. Structure of an Atom

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains the chemical properties of that element.

The atom consists mainly of a small, dense nucleus surrounded by electrons.

Particle Charge Location
Proton +1 Nucleus
Neutron 0 Nucleus
Electron -1 Outside nucleus
Important:

The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, while electrons occupy regions around the nucleus.

2. Proton

A proton is a positively charged subatomic particle found in the nucleus of an atom.

Key Point:

The number of protons in an atom determines its atomic number and therefore identifies the element.

Atomic Number (Z) = Number of Protons

3. Planck's Quantum Theory

Max Planck proposed that energy is emitted or absorbed in discrete packets called quanta.

Quantum:

A quantum is a small discrete packet of energy.

E = hν

E = Energy
h = Planck's constant
ν = Frequency

Planck's constant is approximately:

h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s

4. Photon

A photon is a discrete packet of electromagnetic radiation.

The energy of a photon depends on its frequency.

E = hν

Higher frequency means higher photon energy.

Remember:

Frequency and photon energy are directly proportional.

5. Relationship Between Frequency and Wavelength

Electromagnetic radiation travels at the speed of light in vacuum.

c = νλ

c = Speed of light
ν = Frequency
λ = Wavelength

Therefore, frequency and wavelength are inversely related.

6. Quantum Numbers

Quantum numbers are used to describe the state and location of an electron in an atom.

Quantum Number Symbol Describes
Principal n Energy level / shell
Azimuthal l Subshell / orbital type
Magnetic mₗ Orientation of orbital
Spin mₛ Spin of electron

7. Principal Quantum Number (n)

The principal quantum number identifies the main energy level or shell occupied by an electron.

Its possible values are:

n = 1, 2, 3, 4, ...

The first shell is called K, the second L, the third M and the fourth N.

8. Azimuthal Quantum Number (l)

The azimuthal quantum number identifies the subshell and general shape of an orbital.

l value Subshell General shape
0 s Spherical
1 p Dumbbell
2 d More complex
3 f More complex

9. Orbitals

An orbital is a region around the nucleus where there is a high probability of finding an electron.

Important:

An orbital can contain a maximum of two electrons.

Subshell Number of Orbitals Maximum Electrons
s 1 2
p 3 6
d 5 10
f 7 14

10. Shapes of Orbitals

The s orbital is approximately spherical in shape.

The p orbitals have a dumbbell-like shape and occur in three orientations: pₓ, pᵧ and p𝓏.

MDCAT Tip:

Remember: s = 1 orbital, p = 3 orbitals, d = 5 orbitals and f = 7 orbitals.

11. Hydrogen Spectrum

When electrons in hydrogen atoms move between different energy levels, energy may be absorbed or emitted.

The emitted energy can appear as electromagnetic radiation of specific wavelengths.

Key Idea:

An electron emits energy when it moves from a higher energy level to a lower energy level.

An electron absorbs energy when it moves from a lower energy level to a higher energy level.

12. Electronic Configuration

Electronic configuration describes the distribution of electrons among the orbitals of an atom.

Electrons occupy orbitals according to specific rules.

Example: Hydrogen = 1s¹

Helium = 1s²

Lithium = 1s² 2s¹

13. Aufbau Principle

The Aufbau principle states that electrons occupy lower-energy orbitals before higher-energy orbitals.

Common filling order:

1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p → 5s → 4d → 5p

This order helps us write electronic configurations.

14. Pauli Exclusion Principle

According to the Pauli exclusion principle, no two electrons in the same atom can have exactly the same set of four quantum numbers.

Simple Rule:

One orbital can contain a maximum of two electrons, and they must have opposite spins.

15. Hund's Rule

Hund's rule states that electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly with parallel spins before pairing occurs.

For example, the three p orbitals are filled one at a time before electrons begin pairing.

Remember:

Single occupancy comes before pairing in equal-energy orbitals.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Atomic number = number of protons.
  • Planck's equation: E = hν.
  • Photon energy increases with frequency.
  • c = νλ.
  • Four quantum numbers are n, l, mₗ and mₛ.
  • s has 1 orbital and holds 2 electrons.
  • p has 3 orbitals and holds 6 electrons.
  • d has 5 orbitals and holds 10 electrons.
  • f has 7 orbitals and holds 14 electrons.
  • Aufbau: lower-energy orbitals fill first.
  • Pauli: maximum two electrons per orbital with opposite spins.
  • Hund: singly occupy equal-energy orbitals before pairing.

📝 MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The atomic number of an element represents:

A) Number of neutrons

B) Number of protons

C) Number of shells

D) Number of orbitals

Answer: B) Number of protons
2. According to Planck's theory, energy is emitted or absorbed in packets called:

A) Orbitals

B) Quanta

C) Neutrons

D) Ions

Answer: B) Quanta
3. The equation E = hν represents:

A) Mass-energy relation

B) Photon energy

C) Gas pressure

D) Atomic mass

Answer: B) Photon energy
4. Which quantum number represents the principal energy level?

A) n

B) l

C) mₗ

D) mₛ

Answer: A) n
5. How many orbitals are present in a p subshell?

A) 1

B) 2

C) 3

D) 5

Answer: C) 3
6. The maximum number of electrons in a d subshell is:

A) 2

B) 6

C) 10

D) 14

Answer: C) 10
7. Which subshell contains one orbital?

A) s

B) p

C) d

D) f

Answer: A) s
8. The maximum number of electrons in one orbital is:

A) 1

B) 2

C) 4

D) 6

Answer: B) 2
9. Which principle states that lower-energy orbitals fill first?

A) Pauli principle

B) Hund's rule

C) Aufbau principle

D) Dalton's law

Answer: C) Aufbau principle
10. Which principle states that an orbital can contain a maximum of two electrons with opposite spins?

A) Aufbau principle

B) Pauli exclusion principle

C) Hund's rule

D) Avogadro's law

Answer: B) Pauli exclusion principle
11. According to Hund's rule, electrons first:

A) Pair immediately

B) Occupy equal-energy orbitals singly

C) Leave the atom

D) Enter the nucleus

Answer: B) Occupy equal-energy orbitals singly
12. Which equation relates speed of light, frequency and wavelength?

A) E = hν

B) c = νλ

C) PV = nRT

D) n = m/M

Answer: B) c = νλ
13. Which quantum number describes orbital shape?

A) n

B) l

C) mₗ

D) mₛ

Answer: B) l
14. The electronic configuration of hydrogen is:

A) 1s¹

B) 1s²

C) 2s¹

D) 2p¹

Answer: A) 1s¹
15. Which particle has a positive charge?

A) Electron

B) Neutron

C) Proton

D) Photon

Answer: C) Proton

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the four quantum numbers and what each represents.
  • Remember the maximum electrons in s, p, d and f subshells.
  • Practice electronic configurations of common elements.
  • Do not confuse Aufbau, Pauli and Hund's rule.
  • Remember E = hν and c = νλ.
  • Practice questions involving quantum numbers and orbitals.
