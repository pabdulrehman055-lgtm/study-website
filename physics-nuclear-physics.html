1. Atomic Nucleus

The nucleus is the small, dense central part of an atom. It contains protons and neutrons, which are collectively called nucleons.

Protons have positive charge, while neutrons have no electric charge.

2. Atomic Number and Mass Number

The number of protons in the nucleus is called the atomic number (Z).

The total number of protons and neutrons is called the mass number (A).

A = Z + N

Here, N represents the number of neutrons.

3. Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

This means isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

4. Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei.

Radioactive decay is a random nuclear process.

Common types of radioactive radiation are alpha (α), beta (β) and gamma (γ) radiation.

5. Alpha Radiation

Alpha particles consist of two protons and two neutrons.

They have a positive charge and relatively large mass compared with beta particles.

Alpha radiation has relatively low penetrating power.

6. Beta Radiation

Beta radiation consists of high-speed particles emitted from unstable nuclei.

In beta-minus decay, the emitted particle is an electron.

Beta radiation has greater penetrating power than alpha radiation.

7. Gamma Radiation

Gamma radiation consists of high-energy electromagnetic radiation.

Gamma rays have no electric charge and no rest mass.

Gamma radiation has very high penetrating power.

8. Half-Life

The half-life of a radioactive substance is the time required for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay.

N = N₀ (1/2)t/T½

Here, N₀ is the initial number of nuclei, N is the number remaining, t is time and T½ is the half-life.

9. Nuclear Fission

Nuclear fission is the process in which a heavy atomic nucleus splits into smaller nuclei, releasing energy.

Fission can produce additional neutrons, which may cause a chain reaction.

10. Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process in which light atomic nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus and release energy.

The Sun and other stars produce energy through nuclear fusion.

11. Mass-Energy Equivalence

Nuclear reactions can involve the conversion of mass into energy.

E = mc²

A small amount of mass can correspond to a very large amount of energy because the speed of light squared is very large.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. The nucleus of an atom contains:

A) Electrons only

B) Protons and neutrons

C) Electrons and photons

D) Neutrons and electrons

Answer: B) Protons and neutrons

2. The atomic number represents the number of:

A) Neutrons

B) Electrons only

C) Protons

D) Nucleons

Answer: C) Protons

3. Which radiation has no electric charge?

A) Alpha

B) Beta

C) Gamma

D) Proton

Answer: C) Gamma

4. Nuclear fission involves:

A) Combining light nuclei

B) Splitting a heavy nucleus

C) Removing all electrons

D) Creating an atom from nothing

Answer: B) Splitting a heavy nucleus

5. The Sun mainly produces its energy through:

A) Nuclear fission

B) Chemical reactions

C) Nuclear fusion

D) Radioactive decay

Answer: C) Nuclear fusion

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
  • Atomic number = number of protons.
  • Mass number = protons + neutrons.
  • Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.
  • Radioactivity is spontaneous nuclear decay.
  • Alpha, beta and gamma are common types of radiation.
  • Half-life is the time required for half the nuclei to decay.
  • Fission splits a heavy nucleus.
  • Fusion combines light nuclei.
  • Mass-energy relation: E = mc².
