Unit 9: Electrochemistry

Definition: Electrochemistry is the branch of chemistry that deals with the relationship between chemical reactions and electrical energy.

Electrochemical reactions involve the transfer of electrons. These reactions are commonly called redox reactions.

MDCAT Focus: Focus on oxidation, reduction, oxidation numbers, balancing redox reactions, anode, cathode, electrode potential and standard electrode potential.

1. Oxidation

Oxidation is the loss of electrons by a substance.
Oxidation → Loss of electrons

Example:
Na → Na+ + e−

In oxidation, the oxidation number of an element generally increases.

Memory Trick: OIL = Oxidation Is Loss of electrons.

2. Reduction

Reduction is the gain of electrons by a substance.
Reduction → Gain of electrons

Example:
Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu

In reduction, the oxidation number of an element generally decreases.

Memory Trick: RIG = Reduction Is Gain of electrons.

3. Redox Reaction

A redox reaction is a reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.
Example:

Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu

Zn loses electrons → oxidation
Cu2+ gains electrons → reduction

Oxidation and reduction always occur together because electrons lost by one species are gained by another.

4. Oxidation Number

Oxidation number is a number assigned to an atom to indicate its apparent charge in a compound or ion according to standard rules.

Important Rules

  • The oxidation number of a free element is 0.
  • The oxidation number of a monoatomic ion equals its charge.
  • Oxygen is usually −2.
  • Hydrogen is usually +1 when bonded to nonmetals.
  • The sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral compound is 0.
  • The sum in a polyatomic ion equals the charge of the ion.
Example: H2O

Hydrogen = +1
Oxygen = −2

2(+1) + (−2) = 0

5. Oxidation and Reduction Using Oxidation Number

A substance is oxidized when its oxidation number increases.

A substance is reduced when its oxidation number decreases.

Process Electrons Oxidation Number
Oxidation Loss Increases
Reduction Gain Decreases
Remember: Oxidation = loss of electrons + increase in oxidation number.
Reduction = gain of electrons + decrease in oxidation number.

6. Balancing Redox Reactions

Redox equations must be balanced so that atoms and electrical charge are conserved.

Basic Half-Reaction Method

  1. Separate oxidation and reduction half-reactions.
  2. Balance atoms other than H and O first.
  3. Balance oxygen using H2O.
  4. Balance hydrogen using H+ in acidic solution.
  5. Balance charge using electrons.
  6. Multiply half-reactions so electrons cancel.
  7. Add the half-reactions and simplify.
MDCAT Tip: The number of electrons lost must equal the number of electrons gained in a balanced redox reaction.

7. Electrochemical Cells

An electrochemical cell is a system in which chemical and electrical energy are interconverted.

Electrochemical cells involve oxidation and reduction at separate electrodes.

Anode

Oxidation always occurs at the anode.

Cathode

Reduction always occurs at the cathode.

Very Important:
Anode = Oxidation
Cathode = Reduction

8. Anode and Cathode

Anode Cathode
Oxidation occurs Reduction occurs
Electrons are produced Electrons are consumed
Site of oxidation half-reaction Site of reduction half-reaction
Remember:

AN OX = Anode Oxidation
RED CAT = Reduction Cathode

9. Electrode

An electrode is a conductor through which electrons enter or leave an electrochemical system.

Electrodes provide a surface where oxidation or reduction reactions can occur.

Common electrode materials include metals and other conducting materials.

10. Electrode Potential

Electrode potential is the potential difference developed between an electrode and the solution with which it is in contact.

It is related to the tendency of a species to undergo oxidation or reduction.

Electrode potentials are commonly measured relative to a reference electrode.

11. Standard Electrode Potential

Standard electrode potential (E°) is the electrode potential measured under standard conditions relative to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode.

Standard electrode potentials are usually listed as reduction potentials.

Higher positive E° → Greater tendency for reduction
A species with a more positive standard reduction potential has a stronger tendency to accept electrons under standard conditions.

12. Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

The Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is a reference electrode assigned a standard electrode potential of 0.00 V.

It is used as a reference for measuring the standard electrode potentials of other half-cells.

Standard electrode potential of SHE:

E° = 0.00 V

The hydrogen electrode involves hydrogen gas, hydrogen ions and an inert platinum electrode.

13. Cell Potential

The potential difference of an electrochemical cell is related to the electrode potentials of its two half-cells.

E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode
Example:

If:
E°cathode = +0.80 V
E°anode = −0.76 V

E°cell = 0.80 − (−0.76)
E°cell = 1.56 V

14. Spontaneous Redox Reactions

In a galvanic or voltaic cell, a spontaneous redox reaction can produce electrical energy.

Under standard conditions, a positive E°cell indicates a thermodynamically favorable spontaneous cell reaction.

Electrons flow through the external circuit from the electrode where oxidation occurs toward the electrode where reduction occurs.

15. Galvanic and Electrolytic Cells

Galvanic / Voltaic Cell Electrolytic Cell
Produces electrical energy from a spontaneous reaction Uses electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous reaction
Chemical energy → Electrical energy Electrical energy → Chemical energy
Oxidation at anode Oxidation at anode
Reduction at cathode Reduction at cathode
Important: The definitions of anode and cathode do not change: oxidation is always at the anode and reduction is always at the cathode.

16. Quick Revision

  • Electrochemistry connects chemical reactions with electrical energy.
  • Oxidation is loss of electrons.
  • Reduction is gain of electrons.
  • Oxidation number increases during oxidation.
  • Oxidation number decreases during reduction.
  • Oxidation and reduction occur together in redox reactions.
  • Oxidation always occurs at the anode.
  • Reduction always occurs at the cathode.
  • Electrode potential describes the tendency of an electrode system to undergo electron transfer.
  • Standard electrode potentials are measured relative to SHE.
  • SHE has E° = 0.00 V.
  • E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode.
  • A positive E°cell indicates a favorable spontaneous cell reaction under standard conditions.
  • Galvanic cells convert chemical energy into electrical energy.
  • Electrolytic cells use electrical energy to drive chemical reactions.

17. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Oxidation is the:

A. Gain of electrons
B. Loss of electrons
C. Gain of protons
D. Loss of neutrons

Answer: B. Loss of electrons

2. Reduction involves:

A. Loss of electrons
B. Gain of electrons
C. Loss of protons only
D. Gain of neutrons

Answer: B. Gain of electrons

3. Oxidation number increases during:

A. Reduction
B. Oxidation
C. Neutralization only
D. Precipitation only

Answer: B. Oxidation

4. Reduction always occurs at the:

A. Anode
B. Cathode
C. Salt bridge
D. Wire

Answer: B. Cathode

5. Oxidation always occurs at the:

A. Cathode
B. Anode
C. Electrolyte only
D. Salt bridge

Answer: B. Anode

6. The standard electrode potential of SHE is:

A. 1.00 V
B. −1.00 V
C. 0.00 V
D. 2.00 V

Answer: C. 0.00 V

7. SHE stands for:

A. Standard Heat Energy
B. Standard Hydrogen Electrode
C. Simple Hydrogen Energy
D. Standard Halogen Electrode

Answer: B. Standard Hydrogen Electrode

8. The equation for standard cell potential is:

A. E°cell = E°anode + E°cathode
B. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode
C. E°cell = E°anode − E°cathode
D. E°cell = E°cathode × E°anode

Answer: B. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode

9. A redox reaction involves:

A. Only oxidation
B. Only reduction
C. Oxidation and reduction
D. Only neutralization

Answer: C. Oxidation and reduction

10. OIL represents:

A. Oxidation Is Loss
B. Oxygen Is Large
C. Oxidation Is Low
D. Oxygen Increases Level

Answer: A. Oxidation Is Loss

11. RIG represents:

A. Reduction Is Gain
B. Reaction Is Good
C. Reduction Is Greater
D. Reactants Increase Generally

Answer: A. Reduction Is Gain

12. A more positive standard reduction potential indicates a greater tendency to:

A. Lose electrons
B. Gain electrons
C. Lose protons
D. Produce neutrons

Answer: B. Gain electrons

13. A galvanic cell converts:

A. Electrical energy into chemical energy
B. Chemical energy into electrical energy
C. Heat into mass
D. Light into sound

Answer: B. Chemical energy into electrical energy

14. In a balanced redox reaction, electrons lost must equal electrons:

A. Produced
B. Gained
C. Destroyed
D. Heated

Answer: B. Gained

15. Which statement is correct?

A. Oxidation occurs at cathode
B. Reduction occurs at anode
C. Oxidation occurs at anode
D. Neither occurs at electrodes

Answer: C. Oxidation occurs at anode

18. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember OIL: Oxidation Is Loss of electrons.
  • Remember RIG: Reduction Is Gain of electrons.
  • Oxidation number increases during oxidation.
  • Oxidation number decreases during reduction.
  • Never confuse anode and cathode.
  • Anode = Oxidation.
  • Cathode = Reduction.
  • Memorize SHE = 0.00 V.
  • Learn E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode.
  • Practice oxidation-number questions carefully.
  • In redox balancing, electrons lost must equal electrons gained.
  • Remember that anode/cathode definitions remain the same in galvanic and electrolytic cells.
