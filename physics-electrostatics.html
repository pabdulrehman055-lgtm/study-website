⚡ 1. What is Electrostatics?

Electrostatics is the branch of physics that deals with electric charges at rest.

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter. There are two types of electric charge: positive and negative.

➕➖ 2. Electric Charge

A proton has a positive charge, while an electron has a negative charge. A neutron has no electric charge.

The SI unit of electric charge is the coulomb (C).

q = ne

Where q is the total charge, n is the number of elementary charges and e is the elementary charge.

🧲 3. Properties of Electric Charge

  • Like charges repel each other.
  • Unlike charges attract each other.
  • Electric charge is conserved.
  • Electric charge is quantized.

📐 4. Coulomb's Law

Coulomb's law describes the electrostatic force between two point charges.

F = k(q₁q₂ / r²)

Where:

  • F = electrostatic force
  • q₁ and q₂ = electric charges
  • r = distance between the charges
  • k = Coulomb's constant

The force increases when the charges increase and decreases as the distance between them increases.

🌐 5. Electric Field

An electric field is the region around a charged body in which another electric charge experiences an electric force.

E = F / q

The SI unit of electric field is N/C.

➡️ 6. Electric Field Lines

Electric field lines are imaginary lines used to represent the direction and strength of an electric field.

  • Field lines start from positive charges.
  • Field lines end on negative charges.
  • Field lines never cross each other.
  • Closer field lines represent a stronger field.

🔋 7. Electric Potential

Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive test charge in bringing it from infinity to that point.

V = W / q

The SI unit of electric potential is the volt (V).

🔋 8. Potential Difference

Potential difference is the work done per unit charge in moving a charge between two points.

V = W / q

A potential difference can cause electric charges to move through a conductor.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. Electrostatics deals with:

A) Charges at rest

B) Moving objects only

C) Sound waves

D) Heat transfer

Answer: A) Charges at rest

2. The SI unit of electric charge is:

A) Volt

B) Ampere

C) Coulomb

D) Ohm

Answer: C) Coulomb

3. Like electric charges:

A) Attract

B) Repel

C) Disappear

D) Become neutral

Answer: B) Repel

4. Which law gives the force between two point charges?

A) Newton's law

B) Ohm's law

C) Coulomb's law

D) Hooke's law

Answer: C) Coulomb's law

5. The SI unit of electric potential is:

A) Coulomb

B) Volt

C) Newton

D) Joule

Answer: B) Volt

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Electrostatics deals with electric charges at rest.
  • There are positive and negative electric charges.
  • Like charges repel and unlike charges attract.
  • Coulomb's law: F = k(q₁q₂/r²).
  • Electric field: E = F/q.
  • Electric potential: V = W/q.
  • The SI unit of charge is coulomb.
  • The SI unit of potential is volt.
