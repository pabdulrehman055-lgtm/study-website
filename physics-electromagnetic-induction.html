🧲 1. What is Electromagnetic Induction?

Electromagnetic induction is the phenomenon in which an electromotive force (emf) is produced in a conductor when the magnetic flux linked with the conductor changes.

If the circuit is closed, the induced emf can produce an induced current.

🌐 2. Magnetic Flux

Magnetic flux is a measure of the magnetic field passing through a given surface.

Φ = BA cos θ

Where Φ is magnetic flux, B is magnetic field strength, A is area and θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal to the surface.

⚡ 3. Faraday's Law

Faraday's law states that the induced emf in a circuit is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit.

ε = -N ΔΦ / Δt

Where ε is induced emf, N is the number of turns, and ΔΦ/Δt represents the rate of change of magnetic flux.

↩️ 4. Lenz's Law

Lenz's law gives the direction of the induced current.

It states that the induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it.

The negative sign in Faraday's law represents this opposition.

🔄 5. Factors Affecting Induced EMF

The induced emf can be increased by increasing the rate at which magnetic flux changes.

  • Increasing the strength of the magnetic field.
  • Increasing the speed of relative motion.
  • Increasing the number of turns in the coil.
  • Increasing the effective area of the coil.

⚙️ 6. Electric Generator

An electric generator is a device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

It works on the principle of electromagnetic induction.

When a coil rotates in a magnetic field, the magnetic flux through the coil changes and an emf is induced.

🔌 7. Transformer

A transformer is a device used to increase or decrease alternating voltage.

It works through electromagnetic induction and normally operates with alternating current.

Vₛ / Vₚ = Nₛ / Nₚ

Where Vₛ and Vₚ are secondary and primary voltages, while Nₛ and Nₚ are the numbers of turns in the secondary and primary coils.

⬆️ 8. Step-Up and Step-Down Transformer

Step-Up Transformer

A step-up transformer increases the output voltage. Its secondary coil has more turns than the primary coil.

Step-Down Transformer

A step-down transformer decreases the output voltage. Its secondary coil has fewer turns than the primary coil.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. Electromagnetic induction is the production of:

A) Heat only

B) Induced emf due to changing magnetic flux

C) Mass

D) Sound

Answer: B) Induced emf due to changing magnetic flux

2. Which law determines the magnitude of induced emf?

A) Ohm's law

B) Faraday's law

C) Newton's law

D) Boyle's law

Answer: B) Faraday's law

3. Lenz's law determines the:

A) Mass of an object

B) Direction of induced current

C) Resistance of a wire

D) Temperature of a body

Answer: B) Direction of induced current

4. An electric generator converts:

A) Electrical energy into mechanical energy

B) Mechanical energy into electrical energy

C) Heat into sound

D) Chemical energy into mass

Answer: B) Mechanical energy into electrical energy

5. A step-up transformer:

A) Decreases voltage

B) Increases voltage

C) Produces no voltage

D) Converts AC into DC

Answer: B) Increases voltage

  • A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

  • Step-down transformer decreases voltage.
