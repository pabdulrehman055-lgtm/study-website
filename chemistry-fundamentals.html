Unit 1: Fundamentals of Chemistry

This topic covers the basic quantitative concepts used in Chemistry and is important for MDCAT preparation.

MDCAT Focus:

Pay special attention to moles, Avogadro's number, stoichiometric calculations, limiting and excess reactants, and percentage yield.

1. Mole Concept

A mole is the amount of a substance that contains a fixed number of particles.

Definition:

One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 10²³ particles. This number is called Avogadro's number.

Avogadro's Number = 6.022 × 10²³ particles/mol

The particles may be atoms, molecules, ions or other specified particles.

2. Avogadro's Number

Avogadro's number tells us how many particles are present in one mole of a substance.

Quantity Number of Particles
1 mole of atoms 6.022 × 10²³ atoms
1 mole of molecules 6.022 × 10²³ molecules
1 mole of ions 6.022 × 10²³ ions
Number of particles = Moles × Avogadro's number

3. Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance. It is usually expressed in grams per mole (g/mol).

Moles = Mass ÷ Molar Mass

For example, the molar mass of water (H₂O) is approximately 18 g/mol.

Remember:

Always check the chemical formula before calculating molar mass.

4. Relationship Between Mass and Moles

The number of moles can be calculated from the mass of a substance using its molar mass.

n = m / M

n = number of moles
m = mass in grams
M = molar mass in g/mol

Example:

If 18 g of water has a molar mass of 18 g/mol:

n = 18 / 18 = 1 mol

5. Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry deals with the quantitative relationships between reactants and products in a chemical reaction.

Important:

A balanced chemical equation provides the mole ratio between reactants and products.

Example:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

From this equation:

  • 2 moles of H₂ react with 1 mole of O₂.
  • 2 moles of H₂ produce 2 moles of H₂O.
  • 1 mole of O₂ produces 2 moles of H₂O.

6. Limiting Reactant

The limiting reactant is the reactant that is completely consumed first in a chemical reaction.

Definition:

The limiting reactant determines the maximum amount of product that can be formed.

MDCAT Tip:

If reactants are present in unequal amounts, determine which reactant runs out first. That reactant is the limiting reactant.

7. Excess Reactant

An excess reactant is a reactant that remains after the limiting reactant has been completely consumed.

In a reaction, the limiting reactant is used up first, while some amount of the excess reactant may remain.

8. Theoretical Yield

The theoretical yield is the maximum amount of product that can be produced according to the balanced chemical equation.

Remember:

Theoretical yield assumes that the reaction proceeds perfectly without loss of product.

9. Actual Yield

Actual yield is the amount of product that is actually obtained from an experiment.

Actual yield is often less than theoretical yield because of practical losses and incomplete reactions.

10. Percentage Yield

Percentage yield compares the actual yield with the theoretical yield.

Percentage Yield = (Actual Yield ÷ Theoretical Yield) × 100
Example:

If the theoretical yield is 20 g and the actual yield is 15 g:

Percentage Yield = (15 ÷ 20) × 100 = 75%

⚡ Quick Revision

  • 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
  • Moles = Mass ÷ Molar Mass.
  • Balanced equations give mole ratios.
  • Limiting reactant is consumed first.
  • Excess reactant remains after the reaction.
  • Theoretical yield is the maximum possible yield.
  • Actual yield is the experimentally obtained yield.
  • Percentage yield = Actual ÷ Theoretical × 100.

📝 MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. How many particles are present in one mole?

A) 3.011 × 10²³

B) 6.022 × 10²³

C) 9.022 × 10²³

D) 1.022 × 10²³

Answer: B) 6.022 × 10²³
2. The number of moles in 18 g of water is:

A) 0.5 mol

B) 1 mol

C) 2 mol

D) 18 mol

Answer: B) 1 mol
3. Which reactant is completely consumed first?

A) Excess reactant

B) Catalyst

C) Limiting reactant

D) Product

Answer: C) Limiting reactant
4. What does a balanced chemical equation provide?

A) Color of products

B) Mole ratios

C) Temperature only

D) Density only

Answer: B) Mole ratios
5. Percentage yield is calculated by:

A) Actual ÷ Theoretical × 100

B) Theoretical ÷ Actual × 100

C) Actual × Theoretical

D) Actual + Theoretical

Answer: A) Actual ÷ Theoretical × 100
6. Which quantity represents the mass of one mole?

A) Atomic number

B) Molar mass

C) Density

D) Volume

Answer: B) Molar mass
7. If theoretical yield is 40 g and actual yield is 30 g, percentage yield is:

A) 25%

B) 50%

C) 75%

D) 100%

Answer: C) 75%
8. The reactant left after a reaction is completed is called:

A) Limiting reactant

B) Excess reactant

C) Product

D) Catalyst

Answer: B) Excess reactant
9. Theoretical yield represents:

A) Maximum possible product

B) Product lost

C) Reactant remaining

D) Catalyst amount

Answer: A) Maximum possible product
10. The symbol commonly used for number of moles is:

A) n

B) m

C) M

D) V

Answer: A) n
11. Avogadro's number is approximately:

A) 6.022 × 10²³

B) 6.022 × 10²⁰

C) 3.011 × 10²³

D) 9.81 × 10²³

Answer: A) 6.022 × 10²³
12. Stoichiometry mainly deals with:

A) Chemical quantities

B) Atomic color

C) Light only

D) Temperature only

Answer: A) Chemical quantities
13. Actual yield is obtained from:

A) Experiment

B) Atomic number

C) Periodic table only

D) Molecular formula only

Answer: A) Experiment
14. One mole of oxygen molecules contains:

A) 6.022 × 10²³ molecules

B) 3.011 × 10²³ molecules

C) 1 × 10²³ molecules

D) 12.044 × 10²³ molecules

Answer: A) 6.022 × 10²³ molecules
15. Which reactant determines the maximum amount of product?

A) Excess reactant

B) Limiting reactant

C) Solvent

D) Catalyst

Answer: B) Limiting reactant

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize Avogadro's number.
  • Practice mole-to-mass and mass-to-mole conversions.
  • Always balance the chemical equation before stoichiometric calculations.
  • Learn how to identify the limiting reactant.
  • Remember the percentage-yield formula.
  • Practice numerical questions regularly.
