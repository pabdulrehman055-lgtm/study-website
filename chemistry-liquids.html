Official Chemistry Unit 4

Liquids

This StudyHub topic covers the important concepts of liquids for MDCAT preparation, including evaporation, vapor pressure, boiling point, hydrogen bonding and the unusual behavior of water.

MDCAT Focus: Learn the relationship between intermolecular forces, evaporation, vapor pressure and boiling point. Water's maximum density at 4°C is especially important.

1. Properties of Liquids

A liquid is a state of matter in which particles are close together but can move past one another.

Liquids have a definite volume but do not have a definite shape. They take the shape of their container.

Important Properties

  • Liquids have a definite volume.
  • Liquids do not have a definite shape.
  • Particles are relatively close together.
  • Particles can move from one place to another.
  • Liquids are generally much less compressible than gases.
  • Intermolecular forces affect liquid properties.
Intermolecular forces: Forces of attraction between molecules. Stronger intermolecular forces generally result in higher boiling points.

2. Evaporation

Evaporation is the process in which molecules escape from the surface of a liquid and enter the gaseous state.

Evaporation can occur at temperatures below the boiling point.

Why Does Evaporation Occur?

Liquid molecules have different kinetic energies. Some molecules at the surface have enough energy to overcome intermolecular attractions and escape into the gas phase.

Important: Evaporation is a surface phenomenon.

Factors Affecting Evaporation

  • Temperature: Higher temperature increases evaporation.
  • Surface area: Larger surface area increases evaporation.
  • Wind speed: Faster moving air increases evaporation.
  • Intermolecular forces: Stronger forces decrease evaporation.

3. Vapor Pressure

When a liquid is kept in a closed container, some molecules escape from the liquid and form vapor.

Eventually, a dynamic equilibrium can develop between evaporation and condensation.

Vapor pressure: The pressure exerted by the vapor of a liquid when the vapor and liquid are in dynamic equilibrium at a particular temperature.

Effect of Temperature

As temperature increases, more molecules have sufficient kinetic energy to escape from the liquid. Therefore, vapor pressure increases.

Higher temperature → Higher vapor pressure

Effect of Intermolecular Forces

Strong intermolecular forces make it harder for molecules to escape from the liquid.

Stronger intermolecular forces → Lower vapor pressure

4. Boiling Point

The boiling point is the temperature at which the vapor pressure of a liquid becomes equal to the external pressure.

Boiling point: The temperature at which the vapor pressure of a liquid becomes equal to the surrounding external pressure.

Effect of External Pressure

When external pressure decreases, a liquid can boil at a lower temperature.

When external pressure increases, the boiling point increases.

Lower external pressure → Lower boiling point
Higher external pressure → Higher boiling point

Relationship with Intermolecular Forces

Stronger intermolecular forces require more energy to separate molecules.

Strong intermolecular forces generally mean a higher boiling point.

5. Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bonding is a particularly strong intermolecular attraction involving hydrogen attached to a highly electronegative atom, especially nitrogen, oxygen or fluorine.

Hydrogen bond: An attractive interaction involving hydrogen attached to an electronegative atom such as N, O or F and another electronegative atom.

Important Elements

  • Nitrogen (N)
  • Oxygen (O)
  • Fluorine (F)

Hydrogen bonding is responsible for many unusual physical properties of substances such as water.

6. Hydrogen Bonding in H₂O

Water molecules form strong hydrogen bonds with one another. Oxygen is highly electronegative and attracts the shared electrons strongly.

This gives water molecules partial positive and partial negative regions, allowing hydrogen bonding between neighboring molecules.

MDCAT Point: Hydrogen bonding gives water unusually high boiling point compared with many molecules of similar molecular mass.

Effects of Hydrogen Bonding in Water

  • High boiling point
  • High surface tension
  • Strong cohesion between water molecules
  • Unusual density behavior

7. Hydrogen Bonding in NH₃

Ammonia (NH₃) can form hydrogen bonds because hydrogen is attached to electronegative nitrogen.

Nitrogen also has a lone pair of electrons that can participate in hydrogen bonding.

NH₃: Hydrogen bonding occurs between the hydrogen of one ammonia molecule and the nitrogen of another molecule.

8. Hydrogen Bonding in HF

Hydrogen fluoride (HF) shows strong hydrogen bonding because fluorine is extremely electronegative.

The H–F bond is highly polar, producing strong attractions between HF molecules.

Remember: Hydrogen bonding is particularly important in compounds containing H bonded to N, O or F.

9. Anomalous Behavior of Water

Most substances contract when cooled. Water behaves differently over a certain temperature range.

When water is cooled from higher temperatures toward 4°C, its volume decreases and density increases.

However, when water is cooled below 4°C, its volume increases and its density decreases.

Very Important MDCAT Fact: Water has its maximum density at 4°C.

10. Why Does Water Have Maximum Density at 4°C?

Hydrogen bonding plays an important role in the unusual behavior of water.

As water cools below 4°C, hydrogen bonds arrange molecules into a more open structure. This structure occupies more space.

Therefore, the density of water decreases below 4°C.

Maximum density of water = 4°C

Important Comparison

Temperature Change Effect on Water
Above 4°C → cooling Density increases
At 4°C Maximum density
Below 4°C → cooling Density decreases
This unusual expansion below 4°C helps explain why ice floats on liquid water.

11. Quick Revision

  • Liquids have definite volume but no definite shape.
  • Evaporation occurs at the surface of a liquid.
  • Evaporation increases with temperature.
  • Greater surface area increases evaporation.
  • Vapor pressure increases with temperature.
  • Strong intermolecular forces lower vapor pressure.
  • Boiling occurs when vapor pressure equals external pressure.
  • Lower external pressure lowers boiling point.
  • Strong intermolecular forces generally increase boiling point.
  • Hydrogen bonding is important when H is bonded to N, O or F.
  • Water, ammonia and hydrogen fluoride show hydrogen bonding.
  • Water has maximum density at 4°C.
  • Water expands when cooled below 4°C.

12. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Liquids have:
  1. Definite shape and definite volume
  2. Definite volume but no definite shape
  3. No volume and no shape
  4. Definite shape but no volume
Answer: B
2. Evaporation is mainly a:
  1. Bulk phenomenon
  2. Surface phenomenon
  3. Solid phenomenon
  4. Gas-only phenomenon
Answer: B
3. Increasing temperature generally causes vapor pressure to:
  1. Decrease
  2. Remain constant
  3. Increase
  4. Become zero
Answer: C
4. Stronger intermolecular forces generally produce:
  1. Higher vapor pressure
  2. Lower boiling point
  3. Lower vapor pressure
  4. Faster evaporation
Answer: C
5. A liquid boils when its vapor pressure becomes equal to:
  1. Zero pressure
  2. External pressure
  3. Internal pressure of solid
  4. Atmospheric temperature
Answer: B
6. Which element is NOT normally associated with hydrogen bonding?
  1. Nitrogen
  2. Oxygen
  3. Fluorine
  4. Sodium
Answer: D
7. Hydrogen bonding is especially important in:
  1. H₂O
  2. CH₄
  3. NaCl
  4. CO₂
Answer: A
8. The vapor pressure of a liquid increases when:
  1. Temperature decreases
  2. Temperature increases
  3. Intermolecular forces increase
  4. Molecular motion stops
Answer: B
9. Water has maximum density at:
  1. 0°C
  2. 2°C
  3. 4°C
  4. 100°C
Answer: C
10. Below 4°C, cooling water causes its density to:
  1. Increase
  2. Decrease
  3. Remain exactly the same
  4. Become infinite
Answer: B
11. Which substance can form strong hydrogen bonding?
  1. HF
  2. CH₄
  3. Cl₂
  4. CO₂
Answer: A
12. Decreasing external pressure causes the boiling point to:
  1. Increase
  2. Decrease
  3. Remain unchanged
  4. Become 1000°C
Answer: B
13. Which factor increases the rate of evaporation?
  1. Lower temperature
  2. Smaller surface area
  3. Higher temperature
  4. Stronger intermolecular forces
Answer: C
14. Ammonia can form hydrogen bonds because it contains:
  1. Hydrogen bonded to nitrogen
  2. Hydrogen bonded to carbon
  3. Sodium ions
  4. Chlorine atoms only
Answer: A
15. The unusual behavior of water is strongly related to:
  1. Metallic bonding
  2. Hydrogen bonding
  3. Only ionic bonding
  4. Nuclear forces
Answer: B

13. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember: water has maximum density at 4°C.
  • Know the difference between evaporation and boiling.
  • Evaporation occurs at the surface.
  • Boiling occurs when vapor pressure equals external pressure.
  • Higher temperature means higher vapor pressure.
  • Strong intermolecular forces usually mean higher boiling point.
  • Remember the hydrogen-bonding elements: N, O and F.
  • Understand why water expands below 4°C.
Golden Point: For MDCAT, never forget: Maximum density of water = 4°C.
