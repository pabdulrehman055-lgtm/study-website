Unit 16: Alcohols and Phenols

Definition: Alcohols are organic compounds containing a hydroxyl group (–OH) attached to a saturated carbon atom. Phenols contain an –OH group directly attached to an aromatic ring.
MDCAT Focus: This unit covers alcohol nomenclature, structure and reactivity, ethers and esters, phenol nomenclature and structure, phenol reactivity, electrophilic aromatic substitution, and the difference between alcohols and phenols.

1. What Are Alcohols?

An alcohol is an organic compound in which a hydroxyl group (–OH) is attached to a saturated carbon atom.
General representation: R–OH

R = alkyl group
–OH = hydroxyl group
Examples:

CH₃OH → Methanol
C₂H₅OH → Ethanol
CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → Propan-1-ol

2. Classification of Alcohols

Alcohols can be classified according to the number of carbon groups attached to the carbon bearing the –OH group.

Type Description Example
Primary (1°) OH-bearing carbon attached to one carbon CH₃CH₂OH
Secondary (2°) OH-bearing carbon attached to two carbons CH₃CHOHCH₃
Tertiary (3°) OH-bearing carbon attached to three carbons (CH₃)₃COH
The classification depends on the carbon atom directly attached to the –OH group.

3. Nomenclature of Alcohols

  1. Select the longest carbon chain containing the –OH group.
  2. Number the chain so the –OH group receives the lowest possible number.
  3. Replace the final -e of the alkane name with -ol.
  4. Indicate the position of the –OH group when necessary.
Examples:

CH₃OH → Methanol
CH₃CH₂OH → Ethanol
CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → Propan-1-ol
CH₃CHOHCH₃ → Propan-2-ol
In alcohol nomenclature, the hydroxyl group gets priority when numbering the parent chain.

4. Structure and Polarity of Alcohols

The oxygen atom in the hydroxyl group is more electronegative than carbon and hydrogen.

R–O–H

The O–H bond is polar.

Alcohol molecules can form hydrogen bonds because of their hydroxyl groups.

Hydrogen bonding strongly influences the boiling points and solubility of many alcohols.

5. Reactivity of Alcohols

Alcohols can undergo several important reactions.

  • Oxidation
  • Dehydration
  • Reaction with active metals
  • Formation of ethers
  • Formation of esters
Important idea: The –OH group is responsible for many characteristic reactions of alcohols.

6. Oxidation of Alcohols

Primary and secondary alcohols can be oxidized under suitable conditions.

Primary alcohol
↓ oxidation
Aldehyde
↓ further oxidation
Carboxylic acid
Secondary alcohol
↓ oxidation
Ketone
Tertiary alcohols are much more resistant to oxidation under ordinary conditions.

7. Dehydration of Alcohols

Dehydration is the removal of water from an alcohol.
Alcohol → Alkene + H₂O
Example:

Ethanol → Ethene + H₂O

under suitable acidic and heating conditions.
Dehydration of alcohols is an important method for preparing alkenes.

8. Reaction of Alcohols with Sodium

Alcohols can react with active metals such as sodium.

2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H₂

The reaction produces an alkoxide and hydrogen gas.

Example:

2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂

9. Ethers

Ethers are organic compounds in which an oxygen atom is bonded to two carbon-containing groups.
General representation: R–O–R′
Example:

CH₃–O–CH₃ → Dimethyl ether
C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ → Diethyl ether

10. Esters

Esters are derivatives of carboxylic acids in which the acidic hydrogen of the –OH group is replaced by an organic group.
General representation: R–COO–R′

Esters can be formed by reaction between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid.

Carboxylic acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water
Ethanoic acid + Ethanol ⇌ Ethyl ethanoate + Water
Many esters have pleasant fruity or sweet-smelling odors.

11. What Are Phenols?

A phenol is an aromatic compound in which a hydroxyl group (–OH) is directly attached to an aromatic ring.
Phenol: C₆H₅OH
The simplest phenol is:

C₆H₅OH → Phenol
The key difference is that the –OH group in a phenol is directly attached to the aromatic ring.

12. Nomenclature of Phenols

The parent compound containing an –OH group directly attached to a benzene ring is called phenol.

Examples:

C₆H₅OH → Phenol
CH₃–C₆H₄–OH → Methylphenol

When other substituents are present on the ring, their positions are indicated by numbers.

13. Structure of Phenol

In phenol, the oxygen atom of the –OH group is directly bonded to a carbon of the benzene ring.

C₆H₅–OH

The lone pair on oxygen can interact with the aromatic ring. This affects the electron distribution and reactivity of phenol.

Phenol is more acidic than ordinary alcohols because the phenoxide ion formed after loss of H⁺ is stabilized by resonance.

14. Acidity of Phenol

Phenol can lose the hydrogen of its hydroxyl group to form a phenoxide ion.

C₆H₅OH ⇌ C₆H₅O⁻ + H⁺

The phenoxide ion is stabilized by delocalization of negative charge through the aromatic ring.

MDCAT Point: Phenol is more acidic than a typical aliphatic alcohol.

15. Reactivity of Phenol

The hydroxyl group influences the electron density of the benzene ring.

Phenol readily participates in electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions.

Electrophilic aromatic substitution is a reaction in which an electrophile replaces a hydrogen atom on an aromatic ring.
The –OH group activates the aromatic ring toward electrophilic substitution and generally directs incoming electrophiles to the ortho and para positions.

16. Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

Benzene and substituted aromatic compounds can undergo electrophilic substitution reactions.

Important examples include:

  • Nitration
  • Halogenation
  • Sulfonation
In phenol, the –OH group increases electron density at the ortho and para positions.
Phenol + Electrophile
↓
Mainly ortho/para substituted products

17. Alcohols vs Phenols

Feature Alcohol Phenol
–OH attachment Attached to saturated carbon Directly attached to aromatic ring
General example C₂H₅OH C₆H₅OH
Acidity Generally weaker More acidic than ordinary alcohols
Aromatic ring Not required Present
Easy trick: Alcohol → OH on a saturated carbon.
Phenol → OH directly on benzene/aromatic ring.

18. Important Formulae

Alcohol: R–OH

Ether: R–O–R′

Ester: R–COO–R′

Phenol: C₆H₅OH

Alcohol dehydration:
Alcohol → Alkene + H₂O

Alcohol + Sodium:
2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H₂

19. Quick Revision

  • Alcohols contain the hydroxyl group –OH.
  • The general representation of an alcohol is R–OH.
  • Alcohols can be primary, secondary or tertiary.
  • Alcohol nomenclature uses the suffix -ol.
  • Alcohols can form hydrogen bonds.
  • Primary alcohols can oxidize to aldehydes and then carboxylic acids.
  • Secondary alcohols can oxidize to ketones.
  • Dehydration of alcohols can produce alkenes.
  • Ethers have the form R–O–R′.
  • Esters have the form R–COO–R′.
  • Phenol has the formula C₆H₅OH.
  • In phenol, –OH is directly attached to an aromatic ring.
  • Phenol is more acidic than ordinary alcohols.
  • Phenol undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • The –OH group generally directs electrophiles to ortho and para positions.

20. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The functional group of alcohols is:

A. –COOH
B. –OH
C. –CHO
D. –CO–

Answer: B. –OH

2. The general formula of an alcohol is:

A. R–OH
B. R–COOH
C. R–X
D. R–CHO

Answer: A. R–OH

3. CH₃CH₂OH is:

A. Methanol
B. Ethanol
C. Propanol
D. Phenol

Answer: B. Ethanol

4. CH₃CHOHCH₃ is a:

A. Primary alcohol
B. Secondary alcohol
C. Tertiary alcohol
D. Phenol

Answer: B. Secondary alcohol

5. The suffix used for alcohols in IUPAC nomenclature is:

A. -ane
B. -ene
C. -ol
D. -al

Answer: C. -ol

6. Oxidation of a secondary alcohol generally produces:

A. Ketone
B. Aldehyde only
C. Alkane
D. Ether

Answer: A. Ketone

7. Dehydration of an alcohol generally produces:

A. Alkene
B. Alkane
C. Amine
D. Ester only

Answer: A. Alkene

8. The general structure of an ether is:

A. R–OH
B. R–O–R′
C. R–COOH
D. R–X

Answer: B. R–O–R′

9. The general structure of an ester is:

A. R–O–R′
B. R–COO–R′
C. R–OH
D. R–NH₂

Answer: B. R–COO–R′

10. The formula of phenol is:

A. C₆H₆
B. C₆H₅OH
C. CH₃OH
D. C₂H₅OH

Answer: B. C₆H₅OH

11. In phenol, the –OH group is directly attached to:

A. An alkane carbon
B. An aromatic ring
C. An oxygen atom
D. A halogen

Answer: B. An aromatic ring

12. Phenol is generally more acidic than:

A. Ordinary aliphatic alcohols
B. Strong mineral acids
C. Carboxylic acids
D. Water only

Answer: A. Ordinary aliphatic alcohols

13. Phenoxide ion is stabilized mainly by:

A. Resonance
B. Nuclear reaction
C. Hydrogen gas
D. Metallic bonding

Answer: A. Resonance

14. The –OH group in phenol generally directs electrophilic substitution toward:

A. Meta only
B. Ortho and para positions
C. Carbon outside the ring
D. No position

Answer: B. Ortho and para positions

15. Which statement is correct?

A. Phenol has no oxygen
B. Alcohols have no –OH group
C. Phenol contains –OH directly attached to an aromatic ring
D. Ethers contain –COOH

Answer: C. Phenol contains –OH directly attached to an aromatic ring

21. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the general formula R–OH for alcohols.
  • Know primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols.
  • Remember the -ol suffix.
  • Know the oxidation products of primary and secondary alcohols.
  • Remember that dehydration produces an alkene.
  • Memorize ether: R–O–R′.
  • Memorize ester: R–COO–R′.
  • Remember phenol: C₆H₅OH.
  • Do not confuse an alcohol with a phenol.
  • Phenol is more acidic than ordinary alcohols.
  • Phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance.
  • The –OH group activates the aromatic ring.
  • Phenol generally gives ortho/para electrophilic substitution.
