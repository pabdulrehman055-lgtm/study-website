1. Electronics

Electronics is the branch of physics that deals with the movement and control of electrons in electrical devices.

2. Conductors, Insulators and Semiconductors

Conductors: Materials that allow electric current to flow easily. Examples include copper and aluminium.

Insulators: Materials that strongly resist the flow of electric current. Examples include rubber and glass.

Semiconductors: Materials whose electrical conductivity lies between conductors and insulators.

Common semiconductor materials are silicon and germanium.

3. Intrinsic and Extrinsic Semiconductors

An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure semiconductor without intentionally added impurities.

An extrinsic semiconductor is a semiconductor whose electrical properties are changed by adding a small amount of impurity.

4. P-Type and N-Type Semiconductors

In a P-type semiconductor, holes are the majority charge carriers.

In an N-type semiconductor, electrons are the majority charge carriers.

P-Type → Majority carriers = Holes
N-Type → Majority carriers = Electrons

5. PN Junction

When a P-type semiconductor is joined with an N-type semiconductor, a PN junction is formed.

Near the junction, a region called the depletion region is formed.

The depletion region contains very few mobile charge carriers.

6. Diode and Rectification

A diode is a semiconductor device that mainly allows current to flow in one direction.

A diode can be used for rectification, which means converting alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC).

7. LED

LED stands for Light Emitting Diode.

An LED emits light when electric current passes through it in the appropriate direction.

LEDs are commonly used as indicator lights and in display devices.

8. Transistor

A transistor is a semiconductor device used mainly for switching and amplification.

Common types include NPN and PNP transistors.

A transistor has three terminals:

  • Emitter
  • Base
  • Collector

📝 Practice MCQs

1. Which of the following is a semiconductor?

A) Copper

B) Silicon

C) Rubber

D) Glass

Answer: B) Silicon

2. What is the majority charge carrier in a P-type semiconductor?

A) Electrons

B) Protons

C) Holes

D) Neutrons

Answer: C) Holes

3. What is the majority charge carrier in an N-type semiconductor?

A) Holes

B) Electrons

C) Protons

D) Neutrons

Answer: B) Electrons

4. A diode mainly allows current to flow in:

A) Both directions equally

B) One direction

C) No direction

D) Random directions

Answer: B) One direction

5. What does LED stand for?

A) Light Energy Device

B) Light Emitting Diode

C) Low Energy Diode

D) Light Electronic Device

Answer: B) Light Emitting Diode
