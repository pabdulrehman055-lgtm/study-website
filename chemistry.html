1. Fundamentals of Chemistry

Fundamental chemistry provides the basic calculations needed to understand chemical reactions.

Important: A balanced chemical equation gives the mole ratio between reactants and products.

1.1 Mole Concept

A mole is the amount of substance containing approximately 6.022 × 1023 representative particles.

Avogadro's Number: 6.022 × 1023 particles per mole.

1.2 Important Formula

Quantity Formula
Moles n = mass / molar mass
Particles particles = moles × 6.022 × 1023
Percentage Yield (Actual yield / Theoretical yield) × 100

1.3 Limiting Reactant

The limiting reactant is the reactant that is completely consumed first and limits the amount of product formed.

Quick Revision

  • 1 mole = 6.022 × 1023 particles.
  • Use balanced equations for mole ratios.
  • Limiting reactant controls product formation.
  • Percentage yield = actual/theoretical × 100.

2. Atomic Structure

Atomic structure explains the arrangement of subatomic particles and the behavior of electrons in atoms.

Important: For electronic configuration, remember the Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle and Hund's rule.

2.1 Quantum Numbers

Quantum numbers describe the energy level and location of an electron in an atom.

Quantum Number Meaning
n Principal energy level
l Subshell
m Orbital orientation
s Electron spin

2.2 Orbitals

  • s orbital is approximately spherical.
  • p orbitals have dumbbell-like shapes.
  • Orbitals are regions where electrons are most likely to be found.

2.3 Electronic Configuration

Electrons occupy orbitals according to their energy. The Aufbau principle describes filling lower-energy orbitals first.

Quick Revision: Aufbau → filling order; Pauli → maximum two electrons per orbital with opposite spins; Hund → electrons occupy equal-energy orbitals singly before pairing.

3. Gases

Gases have particles that are far apart and move continuously.

Important: According to kinetic molecular theory, gas particles are in continuous random motion.

3.1 Kinetic Molecular Theory

  • Gas particles are in continuous motion.
  • Collisions are treated as elastic.
  • Particles are separated by relatively large spaces.
  • Average kinetic energy depends on temperature.

3.2 Gas Laws

Law Relationship
Boyle's Law P ∝ 1/V at constant temperature
Charles's Law V ∝ T at constant pressure
Ideal Gas Equation PV = nRT
Quick Revision: Increasing temperature increases the average kinetic energy of gas particles.

4. Liquids

Liquids have particles that are close together but can move past one another.

Important Properties

  • Evaporation
  • Vapor pressure
  • Boiling point
  • Intermolecular forces
  • Hydrogen bonding
Important: Hydrogen bonding is especially important in H2O, NH3 and HF.

Anomalous Behavior of Water

Water has maximum density at approximately 4°C. This unusual behavior is important in understanding water's physical properties.

5. Solids

Solids have closely packed particles and generally possess a definite shape and volume.

Crystalline Solids

Crystalline solids have particles arranged in an ordered repeating pattern.

Type Main Feature
Ionic solid Positive and negative ions
Molecular solid Molecules held by intermolecular forces

6. Chemical Equilibrium

Chemical equilibrium occurs in a reversible reaction when the forward and reverse reaction rates become equal.

Important: At equilibrium, reactions do not stop. Both forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates.

Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts in a direction that tends to oppose the disturbance.

Equilibrium Constant

For a general reaction, equilibrium expressions relate the concentrations of products and reactants.

Quick Revision: Equal forward and reverse rates = dynamic equilibrium.

7. Reaction Kinetics

Chemical kinetics is the study of the rates of chemical reactions.

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

  • Concentration
  • Temperature
  • Surface area
  • Catalyst
  • Nature of reactants
Important: A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

Activation Energy

Activation energy is the minimum energy required for a reaction to occur.

8. Thermochemistry and Energetics

Thermochemistry deals with heat and energy changes during chemical reactions.

Reaction Energy Change
Exothermic Releases heat
Endothermic Absorbs heat
Important: Exothermic reactions generally have a negative enthalpy change, while endothermic reactions have a positive enthalpy change.

Hess's Law

The overall enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the route taken between initial and final states.

9. Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry studies the relationship between chemical reactions and electrical energy.

Redox Reactions

  • Oxidation involves loss of electrons.
  • Reduction involves gain of electrons.
  • Oxidation and reduction occur together.
Remember: OIL RIG = Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain of electrons.

10. Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonds hold atoms together in molecules and compounds.

Bond Description
Ionic Transfer of electrons
Covalent Sharing of electrons
Coordinate covalent Shared pair donated by one atom

Hybridization

Hybridization describes the mixing of atomic orbitals to form hybrid orbitals used in bonding.

Quick Revision: Understand bond type, molecular shape, polarity and hybridization for MDCAT questions.

11. S- and P-Block Elements

The periodic table is divided into blocks according to the subshell being filled by the outermost electrons.

Periodic Trends

  • Atomic radius
  • Ionization energy
  • Electronegativity
  • Electron affinity
Important: Periodic trends are frequently useful for comparing elements.

12. Transition Elements

Transition elements are associated with partially filled d orbitals in atoms or ions.

Important Properties

  • Variable oxidation states
  • Formation of colored compounds
  • Complex formation
  • Catalytic activity

13. Fundamental Principles of Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry is mainly the study of carbon-containing compounds.

Functional Groups

Functional Group Example
Alcohol –OH
Aldehyde –CHO
Ketone >C=O
Carboxylic acid –COOH
Important: Learn functional groups carefully because they help identify organic compounds and predict their reactions.

14. Chemistry of Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen.

Class Bond
Alkanes Single bonds
Alkenes At least one C=C
Alkynes At least one C≡C
Benzene Aromatic ring

Benzene

Benzene is an aromatic compound with a six-carbon ring and delocalized electrons.

15. Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides contain a carbon-halogen bond.

Important Reactions

  • Nucleophilic substitution
  • Elimination
Important: Know the difference between substitution and elimination reactions.

16. Alcohols and Phenols

Alcohols contain a hydroxyl group attached to a saturated carbon, while phenols have the hydroxyl group attached directly to an aromatic ring.

Important Concepts

  • Nomenclature
  • Structure
  • Physical properties
  • Reactivity
  • Preparation of ethers and esters
Important: Phenol and alcohol both contain –OH, but their chemical behavior is not identical.

17. Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl group, C=O.

Compound Carbonyl Position
Aldehyde At the end of carbon chain
Ketone Within carbon chain

Important Reactions

  • Nucleophilic addition
  • Reduction
  • Oxidation
Remember: Aldehydes are generally more easily oxidized than ketones.

18. Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group –COOH.

Properties

  • Acidic character
  • Hydrogen bonding
  • Characteristic reactions with bases
  • Ester formation

Important Derivatives

  • Acyl halides
  • Acid anhydrides
  • Esters
Important: Esterification commonly involves a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.

19. Macromolecules

Macromolecules are large molecules with important biological functions.

Proteins

Proteins are made from amino acids joined by peptide bonds.

Peptide Bond: The bond formed between amino acids during protein formation.

Enzymes

Enzymes act as biological catalysts and increase the rate of biochemical reactions.

Important: Protein structure is closely related to protein function.

20. Industrial Chemistry

Industrial chemistry applies chemical principles to large-scale production and useful materials.

Adhesives

Adhesives are substances used to join materials together. Different types are used for different applications.

Dyes

Dyes are colored substances used to impart color to materials.

Polymers

Type Description
Addition polymer Forms through addition of monomers without eliminating small molecules.
Condensation polymer Forms with elimination of small molecules in the polymerization process.
Important: Know the difference between addition and condensation polymers.

📝 Chemistry Practice MCQs

1. One mole contains approximately:
A. 6.022 × 1020
B. 6.022 × 1023
C. 3.011 × 1010
D. 9.8 × 1023

Answer: B

2. The limiting reactant determines:
A. Atomic number
B. Maximum product formed
C. Temperature only
D. Pressure only

Answer: B

3. The shape of an s orbital is:
A. Dumbbell
B. Spherical
C. Linear
D. Square

Answer: B

4. Which law relates pressure and volume at constant temperature?
A. Charles's law
B. Boyle's law
C. Hess's law
D. Faraday's law

Answer: B

5. A catalyst generally:
A. Increases activation energy
B. Decreases activation energy
C. Stops a reaction
D. Changes the equilibrium constant

Answer: B

6. Oxidation involves:
A. Gain of electrons
B. Loss of electrons
C. Gain of protons only
D. Loss of neutrons

Answer: B

7. A bond formed by transfer of electrons is:
A. Covalent
B. Ionic
C. Hydrogen
D. Metallic

Answer: B

8. Hydrocarbons contain:
A. C and O
B. C and H
C. H and O
D. C and N

Answer: B

9. The functional group of carboxylic acids is:
A. –OH
B. –CHO
C. –COOH
D. –NH2

Answer: C

10. Amino acids in proteins are joined by:
A. Ionic bonds
B. Peptide bonds
C. Metallic bonds
D. Hydrogen bonds only

Answer: B

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Mole → 6.022 × 1023 particles.
  • Limiting reactant → controls maximum product.
  • Aufbau, Pauli and Hund → electronic configuration.
  • Boyle's law → pressure-volume relationship.
  • Equilibrium → forward rate = reverse rate.
  • Catalyst → lowers activation energy.
  • Oxidation → loss of electrons.
  • Ionic bond → electron transfer.
  • Covalent bond → electron sharing.
  • Hydrocarbons → carbon + hydrogen.
  • Carboxylic acid → –COOH.
  • Protein → amino acids + peptide bonds.
  • Enzyme → biological catalyst.
  • Industrial chemistry → practical large-scale chemical applications.
