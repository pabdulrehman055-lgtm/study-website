1. Fundamentals of Chemistry

What is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the composition, structure, properties and transformations of matter.

Definition: Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.

Mole Concept

The mole is the SI unit used to express the amount of a substance. One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 1023 particles.

Important: 6.022 × 1023 is called Avogadro's number.

Important Formulae

Quantity Formula
Number of moles n = mass / molar mass
Number of particles Particles = moles × 6.022 × 1023
Percentage yield (Actual yield / Theoretical yield) × 100

Limiting Reactant

The limiting reactant is the reactant that is completely consumed first in a chemical reaction. It determines the maximum amount of product that can be formed.

Excess Reactant

The reactant that remains after the limiting reactant has been completely consumed is called the excess reactant.

Percentage Yield

The actual amount of product obtained in an experiment may be less than the theoretical amount. Percentage yield compares these two values.

⚡ Quick Revision
  • 1 mole = 6.022 × 1023 particles.
  • Moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • Limiting reactant controls product formation.
  • Excess reactant remains after the reaction.
  • Percentage yield = actual yield ÷ theoretical yield × 100.

2. Atomic Structure

Introduction

Atomic structure deals with the arrangement of subatomic particles inside an atom and the behavior of electrons around the nucleus.

Important: The three main subatomic particles are protons, neutrons and electrons.

Subatomic Particles

Particle Charge Location
Proton Positive (+1) Nucleus
Neutron Neutral (0) Nucleus
Electron Negative (-1) Outside nucleus

Quantum Numbers

Quantum numbers describe the state and location of an electron in an atom.

Quantum Number Symbol Purpose
Principal n Energy level
Azimuthal l Subshell
Magnetic m Orbital orientation
Spin s Electron spin

Orbitals

  • s orbital is spherical.
  • p orbitals have dumbbell-like shapes.
  • Electrons occupy orbitals according to their energy.

Electronic Configuration

Electrons are arranged in orbitals according to their energy. The Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle and Hund's rule help determine electronic configurations.

⚡ Quick Revision
  • Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus.
  • Electrons occupy orbitals outside the nucleus.
  • n = principal quantum number.
  • l = azimuthal quantum number.
  • m = magnetic quantum number.
  • s = spin quantum number.

3. Gases

Introduction

Gases have no fixed shape or volume. Their particles are relatively far apart and move continuously.

Important: The kinetic molecular theory explains the behavior of gases in terms of the motion of their particles.

Kinetic Molecular Theory

  • Gas particles are in continuous random motion.
  • Collisions between particles are treated as elastic.
  • The particles are separated by relatively large spaces.
  • Average kinetic energy increases with temperature.

Important Gas Laws

Law Relationship
Boyle's Law P ∝ 1/V
Charles's Law V ∝ T
Ideal Gas Equation PV = nRT
⚡ Quick Revision
  • Increasing temperature increases average kinetic energy.
  • Boyle's law relates pressure and volume.
  • Charles's law relates volume and temperature.
  • PV = nRT is the ideal gas equation.

4. Liquids

Introduction

Liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container. Their particles are close together but can move past each other.

Important: Intermolecular forces strongly affect the properties of liquids.

Important Properties

  • Vapor pressure
  • Boiling point
  • Surface tension
  • Viscosity
  • Hydrogen bonding
⚡ Quick Revision: Stronger intermolecular forces generally produce higher boiling points and lower vapor pressures.

5. Solids

Introduction

Solids have a definite shape and volume because their particles are closely packed.

Types of Solids

Type Particles
Ionic Ions
Molecular Molecules
Covalent network Atoms connected by covalent bonds
⚡ Quick Revision: Solids generally have closely packed particles and strong attractive forces.
