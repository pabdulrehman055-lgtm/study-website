1. 🧮 Fundamental Concepts of Chemistry

What is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the composition, structure, properties and transformations of matter.

🔹 Mole

A mole is the amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ particles. This number is called Avogadro's number.
Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

Number of particles = moles × 6.022 × 10²³

🔹 Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance and is expressed in g/mol.

Number of moles = Mass ÷ Molar Mass

n = m / M
Example:
Calculate the number of moles in 36 g of H₂O.

Molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol
n = 36 ÷ 18
n = 2 mol

🔹 Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry deals with the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction.

🔹 Limiting Reactant

The reactant that is completely consumed first and determines the maximum amount of product formed is called the limiting reactant.

🔹 Excess Reactant

The reactant that remains after the limiting reactant has been completely consumed is called the excess reactant.

🔹 Percentage Yield

Percentage Yield = (Actual Yield ÷ Theoretical Yield) × 100

2. ⚛️ Atomic Structure

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains the chemical properties of that element.

Particle Charge Relative Mass Location
Proton +1 1 Nucleus
Neutron 0 1 Nucleus
Electron -1 Very small Outside nucleus
Atomic Number = Number of Protons
For a neutral atom, number of protons = number of electrons.
Mass Number = Protons + Neutrons

🔹 Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different numbers of neutrons.

🔹 Electronic Configuration

Electrons are arranged in shells and subshells around the nucleus. The electronic configuration helps determine the chemical behavior of an element.

3. 📊 Periodic Table & Periodicity

The periodic table arranges elements according to increasing atomic number and their recurring chemical properties.

Important Periodic Trends

Property Across a Period Down a Group
Atomic Radius Generally decreases Generally increases
Ionization Energy Generally increases Generally decreases
Electronegativity Generally increases Generally decreases
Metallic Character Generally decreases Generally increases
MDCAT Tip: Do not only memorize the arrows. Understand why the trends occur.

4. 🔗 Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonds are attractive forces that hold atoms or ions together.

🔹 Ionic Bond

An ionic bond is formed through the transfer of electrons, usually from a metal to a non-metal.

Example: NaCl

🔹 Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share electrons.

Example: H₂, Cl₂ and CH₄.

🔹 Hydrogen Bond

Hydrogen bonding is a relatively strong intermolecular attraction involving hydrogen bonded to highly electronegative atoms such as oxygen or nitrogen.

5. 💨 States of Matter

Property Solid Liquid Gas
Shape Fixed Not fixed Not fixed
Volume Fixed Fixed Not fixed
Particle Movement Very limited Moderate High

🔹 Gas Laws

Boyle's Law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Charles's Law: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Ideal Gas Equation: PV = nRT
Temperature in gas-law calculations should normally be expressed in Kelvin.

6. 💧 Solutions

A solution is a homogeneous mixture containing a solute and a solvent.

Solute: Substance being dissolved.
Solvent: Substance that dissolves the solute.

🔹 Molarity

Molarity = Moles of Solute ÷ Volume of Solution in Litres

🔹 Solubility

Solubility is the maximum amount of a substance that can dissolve in a specified amount of solvent under given conditions.

7. ⚡ Chemical Kinetics

Chemical kinetics is the study of the rate of chemical reactions and the factors affecting reaction rate.

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

  • Temperature
  • Concentration
  • Surface area
  • Pressure for gases
  • Catalyst
  • Nature of reactants
A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy and is not permanently consumed in the overall reaction.

8. ⚖️ Chemical Equilibrium

Dynamic equilibrium occurs in a reversible reaction when the forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates.

At equilibrium, concentrations of reactants and products remain constant, although the reactions continue.

Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it tends to shift in a direction that opposes the disturbance.

9. 🧪 Acids, Bases & Salts

Acid: A substance that donates H⁺ according to the Brønsted-Lowry concept.
Base: A substance that accepts H⁺ according to the Brønsted-Lowry concept.

pH Scale

pH = -log[H⁺]
pH Nature
Less than 7 Acidic
7 Neutral
Greater than 7 Basic

10. 🔋 Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry deals with chemical reactions involving the transfer of electrons and their relationship with electrical energy.

Oxidation: Loss of electrons.
Reduction: Gain of electrons.
OIL = Oxidation Is Loss
RIG = Reduction Is Gain

11. 🔥 Thermochemistry

Thermochemistry deals with heat changes associated with chemical reactions and physical changes.

Exothermic: Releases heat to surroundings.
Endothermic: Absorbs heat from surroundings.

12. 🧬 Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon compounds and their reactions.

Important Concepts

  • Carbon bonding
  • Structural formulas
  • Isomerism
  • Homologous series
  • Functional groups
  • Organic reactions

13. ⛓️ Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

Alkenes CₙH₂ₙ Double
Alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ Triple

15. 🧬 Biochemistry

Biochemistry studies the chemical substances and reactions occurring in living organisms.

  • Carbohydrates
  • Proteins
  • Lipids
  • Nucleic acids
  • Enzymes
Biochemistry is especially important for understanding the chemical basis of biological processes.

16. 🌍 Environmental Chemistry

Environmental chemistry studies chemical processes occurring in air, water, soil and living systems.

Important Topics

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Greenhouse effect
  • Global warming
  • Acid rain
  • Ozone depletion

📝 MDCAT Chemistry Practice MCQs

1. One mole contains:

A) 6.022 × 10²³ particles

B) 3.011 × 10²³ particles

C) 9.8 × 10²³ particles

D) 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ particles

✅ Answer: A

2. Atomic number is equal to the number of:

A) Neutrons

B) Protons

C) Nucleons

D) Protons + neutrons

✅ Answer: B

3. Which particle has a negative charge?

A) Proton

B) Neutron

C) Electron

D) Nucleus

✅ Answer: C

4. Which bond involves sharing of electrons?

A) Ionic

B) Covalent

C) Metallic

D) Coordinate only

✅ Answer: B

5. The general formula of alkanes is:

A) CₙH₂ₙ

B) CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

C) CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

D) CₙHₙ

✅ Answer: C

6. A solution with pH 2 is:

A) Basic

B) Neutral

C) Acidic

D) Amphoteric

✅ Answer: C

7. Oxidation is:

A) Gain of electrons

B) Loss of electrons

C) Gain of protons

D) Loss of neutrons

✅ Answer: B

8. Which factor can increase reaction rate?

A) Lowering temperature

B) Increasing suitable concentration

C) Removing all reactants

D) Always decreasing surface area

✅ Answer: B

9. The formula PV = nRT represents:

A) Boyle's law only

B) Charles's law only

C) Ideal gas equation

D) Avogadro's number

✅ Answer: C

10. The limiting reactant is:

A) Present in unlimited amount

B) Consumed first

C) Always a catalyst

D) Never involved in the reaction

✅ Answer: B

11. An exothermic reaction:

A) Absorbs heat

B) Releases heat

C) Has no energy change

D) Stops at equilibrium

✅ Answer: B

12. Which functional group belongs to alcohols?

A) -COOH

B) -CHO

C) -OH

D) -NH₂

✅ Answer: C

13. Which is a hydrocarbon?

A) CH₄

B) H₂O

C) NH₃

D) NaCl

✅ Answer: A

14. At 25°C, a neutral solution has pH:

A) 1

B) 5

C) 7

D) 14

✅ Answer: C

15. A catalyst generally:

A) Increases activation energy

B) Lowers activation energy

C) Is completely consumed

D) Stops equilibrium

✅ Answer: B
