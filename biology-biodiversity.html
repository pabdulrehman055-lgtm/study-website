Chapter 9 — Biodiversity & Evolution

MDCAT Biology Notes, Important Concepts & MCQs

1. What is Biodiversity?

Biodiversity means the variety of living organisms present in a particular area or on Earth.

Three Main Levels of Biodiversity

  • Genetic diversity: Variation in genes within a species.
  • Species diversity: Variety of different species in an area.
  • Ecosystem diversity: Variety of ecosystems and habitats.
MDCAT Point: Biodiversity can be studied at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

2. Importance of Biodiversity

Biodiversity is important for the stability and functioning of ecosystems.

  • Provides food and medicinal resources.
  • Maintains ecological balance.
  • Provides raw materials.
  • Supports pollination and nutrient cycling.
  • Helps ecosystems respond to environmental changes.
Remember: Greater biodiversity generally increases the variety of biological resources available to an ecosystem.

3. Classification of Organisms

Biological classification is the organization of organisms into groups based on similarities and evolutionary relationships.

Taxonomic Hierarchy

  1. Domain
  2. Kingdom
  3. Phylum
  4. Class
  5. Order
  6. Family
  7. Genus
  8. Species
Important: Species is the basic unit of biological classification.

4. Species

A species is generally considered a group of organisms that can interbreed naturally and produce fertile offspring.

Binomial Nomenclature

Scientific names commonly consist of two parts:

  • Genus
  • Specific epithet

Example: Homo sapiens

MDCAT Tip: The genus name starts with a capital letter, while the specific epithet starts with a small letter.

5. Evolution

Evolution is the change in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Evolution occurs over generations and can lead to adaptation and diversification of organisms.

Key Idea: Evolution acts on populations across generations, not on an individual organism during its lifetime.

6. Natural Selection

Natural selection is a mechanism of evolution in which individuals with advantageous heritable variations are more likely to survive and reproduce.

Basic Steps

  1. Variation exists among individuals.
  2. Some variations are heritable.
  3. Organisms face competition and environmental pressures.
  4. Individuals with advantageous traits may leave more offspring.
  5. Useful traits can become more common in later generations.

7. Adaptation

An adaptation is an inherited characteristic that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment.

Examples

  • Thick fur in animals living in cold environments.
  • Streamlined body of aquatic animals.
  • Specialized beaks in birds.
  • Water-conserving features in desert plants.
Adaptations are inherited characteristics that become useful under particular environmental conditions.

8. Variation

Variation means differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

Sources of Genetic Variation

  • Mutation
  • Crossing over
  • Independent assortment
  • Random fertilization
MDCAT Point: Mutation introduces new genetic changes, while processes such as crossing over and independent assortment create new combinations of existing genetic material.

9. Evidence of Evolution

Several types of evidence support evolutionary relationships.

Fossils

Fossils provide evidence of organisms that lived in the past and can reveal changes through geological time.

Comparative Anatomy

  • Homologous structures: Similar basic structure but may perform different functions.
  • Analogous structures: Similar function but different structural/evolutionary origins.

Embryology

Similarities in early developmental stages can provide evidence of evolutionary relationships.

Molecular Evidence

Similarities in DNA and protein sequences can indicate evolutionary relationships between organisms.

10. Homologous vs Analogous Structures

  • Homologous: Similar underlying structure, possibly different functions.
  • Analogous: Similar function but different underlying structure and origin.
Exam Trick: Homologous structures are associated with common ancestry and divergent evolution.

11. Speciation

Speciation is the formation of new species.

When populations become reproductively isolated and accumulate genetic differences over generations, they may eventually form separate species.

Reproductive Isolation

Reproductive isolation prevents successful gene flow between populations.

Geographic separation can contribute to reproductive isolation and eventually lead to speciation.

12. Extinction

Extinction occurs when no living members of a species remain.

Major Causes

  • Habitat destruction
  • Overhunting
  • Pollution
  • Climate and environmental changes
  • Introduction of invasive species

13. Conservation of Biodiversity

Conservation aims to protect organisms, habitats and genetic diversity.

In-situ Conservation

Protection of organisms in their natural habitats.

Examples include:

  • National parks
  • Wildlife sanctuaries
  • Protected forests

Ex-situ Conservation

Conservation outside the natural habitat.

Examples include:

  • Zoos
  • Botanical gardens
  • Seed banks

14. Human Impact on Biodiversity

  • Deforestation
  • Urbanization
  • Industrial pollution
  • Overexploitation of resources
  • Habitat fragmentation
  • Climate change
  • Illegal hunting
Remember: Habitat loss and overexploitation are major threats to biodiversity.

15. Quick Revision

  • Biodiversity = variety of life.
  • Three levels: genetic, species and ecosystem.
  • Species is a basic unit of classification.
  • Evolution is change in inherited characteristics over generations.
  • Natural selection can change populations over generations.
  • Variation is important for evolution.
  • Mutation can introduce new genetic variation.
  • Fossils provide evidence of past life.
  • Homologous structures suggest common ancestry.
  • Speciation is formation of new species.
  • Extinction means disappearance of a species.
  • In-situ conservation protects organisms in natural habitats.
  • Ex-situ conservation occurs outside natural habitats.

16. MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Biodiversity refers to:

A) Only animal diversity

B) Only plant diversity

C) Variety of life

D) Only genetic mutations

Answer: C) Variety of life

2. Which is a level of biodiversity?

A) Genetic diversity

B) Atomic diversity

C) Chemical diversity

D) Cellular pressure

Answer: A) Genetic diversity

3. The basic unit of biological classification is:

A) Kingdom

B) Family

C) Species

D) Class

Answer: C) Species

4. Evolution occurs across:

A) One cell cycle

B) Generations

C) One minute

D) One individual's lifetime only

Answer: B) Generations

5. Natural selection acts on:

A) Heritable variation

B) Water molecules

C) Non-living matter

D) Rocks

Answer: A) Heritable variation

6. Which can introduce new genetic variation?

A) Mutation

B) Digestion

C) Respiration

D) Transpiration

Answer: A) Mutation

7. Homologous structures generally indicate:

A) Common ancestry

B) No relationship

C) Identical habitats only

D) Identical functions only

Answer: A) Common ancestry

8. Similar function but different evolutionary origin describes:

A) Homologous structures

B) Analogous structures

C) Vestigial genes

D) Chromosomes

Answer: B) Analogous structures

9. Formation of a new species is called:

A) Extinction

B) Speciation

C) Digestion

D) Respiration

Answer: B) Speciation

10. Which is an example of in-situ conservation?

A) Seed bank

B) Zoo

C) National park

D) Laboratory culture

Answer: C) National park

11. Which is an example of ex-situ conservation?

A) Wildlife sanctuary

B) National park

C) Protected forest

D) Botanical garden

Answer: D) Botanical garden

12. Fossils are important because they provide evidence of:

A) Future organisms

B) Past life

C) Only modern humans

D) Only bacteria

Answer: B) Past life

13. Which is a major threat to biodiversity?

A) Habitat destruction

B) Photosynthesis

C) Respiration

D) Pollination

Answer: A) Habitat destruction

14. Reproductive isolation can contribute to:

A) Speciation

B) Digestion

C) Photosynthesis

D) Transpiration

Answer: A) Speciation

15. Which taxonomic rank comes immediately below genus?

A) Family

B) Species

C) Order

D) Class

Answer: B) Species

17. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Learn the three levels of biodiversity.
  • Remember the complete taxonomic hierarchy.
  • Understand natural selection rather than memorizing only its definition.
  • Differentiate homologous and analogous structures.
  • Know the meaning of adaptation, variation and speciation.
  • Remember the difference between in-situ and ex-situ conservation.
  • Practice MCQs involving examples and comparisons.
