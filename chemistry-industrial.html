Unit 20: Industrial Chemistry

Definition: Industrial chemistry deals with the production and application of useful chemical substances on a large scale.

Important areas in this unit include adhesives, dyes and polymers.

MDCAT Focus: Learn the types, uses and basic characteristics of adhesives, dyes, addition polymers and condensation polymers.

1. Adhesives

Definition: An adhesive is a substance used to join two surfaces together by forming a bond between them.

Adhesives are widely used in construction, packaging, furniture, automobiles, electronics and many other industries.

Properties of Good Adhesives

  • They should form a strong bond.
  • They should adhere well to the surfaces.
  • They should have suitable setting or curing properties.
  • They should be reasonably durable.
  • They should be suitable for their intended application.

2. Types of Adhesives

Adhesives can be classified according to their composition and method of setting.

Type General Description Applications
Natural adhesives Obtained from natural sources Paper, wood and traditional applications
Synthetic adhesives Produced chemically Construction, furniture, packaging and industry
Examples of synthetic adhesives: epoxy adhesives and some polymer-based adhesives.

3. Applications of Adhesives

Adhesives are used in many areas of daily life and industry.

  • Joining wood in furniture.
  • Packaging materials.
  • Construction materials.
  • Automobile manufacturing.
  • Electronic components.
  • Paper and cardboard products.
  • Manufacturing and repair processes.
Remember: The main purpose of an adhesive is to join surfaces together.

4. Dyes

Definition: Dyes are colored substances that can impart color to materials, especially textiles and other useful products.

Dyes are important in the textile and chemical industries. Different dyes are selected according to the material being colored and the required properties.

5. Types of Dyes

Dyes can be classified in different ways according to their chemical nature and method of application.

Natural Dyes

Natural dyes are obtained from natural sources such as plants and other naturally occurring materials.

Synthetic Dyes

Synthetic dyes are produced through chemical processes and are widely used in modern industries.

Type Source General Use
Natural dyes Natural sources Textiles and traditional coloring
Synthetic dyes Chemical synthesis Textiles and industrial products

6. Uses of Dyes

  • Coloring textile fibers.
  • Printing and textile manufacturing.
  • Coloring leather and other materials.
  • Industrial and commercial products.
  • Specialized applications requiring colored materials.
MDCAT Point: Dyes are mainly studied for their types and applications in industrial chemistry.

7. Polymers

Definition: A polymer is a very large molecule formed by joining many smaller molecules called monomers.
Many monomers → Polymer

The small repeating units that form a polymer are called monomers.

Example:
Ethene is a monomer that can form polyethene.

8. Addition Polymers

Definition: Addition polymers are formed by the addition of unsaturated monomers, usually without the elimination of a small molecule.

Alkenes are common monomers for addition polymerization.

n(CH₂=CH₂) → (–CH₂–CH₂–)ₙ

Ethene forms polyethene.

Examples

Monomer Polymer
Ethene Polyethene
Propene Polypropene
Vinyl chloride PVC
Exam Tip: Addition polymerization commonly involves monomers containing carbon-carbon double bonds.

9. Condensation Polymers

Definition: Condensation polymers are formed when monomers join together with the elimination of small molecules such as water.

Condensation polymerization generally involves monomers containing two or more suitable functional groups.

Monomers → Polymer + Small molecule
Common examples: polyamides and polyesters.

10. Addition vs Condensation Polymerization

Feature Addition Polymerization Condensation Polymerization
Monomers Often unsaturated monomers Usually monomers with suitable functional groups
Small molecule eliminated Generally no Usually yes
Example Polyethene Polyester / Polyamide
Easy way to remember:
Addition → monomers add together.
Condensation → polymer forms with elimination of a small molecule.

11. Important Polymer Examples

Polymer Monomer / Type Common Application
Polyethene Ethene / Addition Packaging and containers
Polypropene Propene / Addition Containers and household products
PVC Vinyl chloride / Addition Pipes and insulation
Polyester Condensation polymer Fibers and fabrics
Polyamide Condensation polymer Fibers and engineering materials

12. Why Polymers Are Important

Polymers are widely used because they can provide useful combinations of properties such as strength, flexibility, durability and chemical resistance.

  • Packaging materials.
  • Textile fibers.
  • Pipes and insulation.
  • Household products.
  • Construction materials.
  • Automotive components.
  • Medical and industrial products.

Quick Revision

  • Adhesives are used to join surfaces.
  • Adhesives can be natural or synthetic.
  • Dyes are colored substances used to color materials.
  • Dyes may be natural or synthetic.
  • A polymer is a large molecule made from repeating monomer units.
  • A monomer is a smaller molecule used to form a polymer.
  • Addition polymerization generally uses unsaturated monomers.
  • Ethene forms polyethene.
  • Propene forms polypropene.
  • Vinyl chloride forms PVC.
  • Condensation polymerization usually eliminates a small molecule.
  • Polyesters and polyamides are important condensation polymers.

15 MDCAT MCQs

1. A substance used to join two surfaces is called:

A) Dye

B) Adhesive

C) Monomer

D) Solvent

Answer: B) Adhesive
2. Which is a type of adhesive?

A) Synthetic adhesive

B) Metal adhesive only

C) Gas adhesive

D) Ionic adhesive only

Answer: A) Synthetic adhesive
3. Dyes are mainly used to:

A) Produce electricity

B) Color materials

C) Produce oxygen

D) Increase pressure

Answer: B) Color materials
4. A polymer is formed from smaller units called:

A) Ions

B) Monomers

C) Atoms only

D) Catalysts

Answer: B) Monomers
5. Ethene forms which polymer?

A) Polyethene

B) PVC

C) Polyamide

D) Polyester

Answer: A) Polyethene
6. Addition polymerization commonly involves:

A) Saturated alkanes only

B) Unsaturated monomers

C) Noble gases

D) Metals only

Answer: B) Unsaturated monomers
7. Propene forms:

A) Polypropene

B) Polyethene

C) PVC

D) Polyamide

Answer: A) Polypropene
8. PVC is produced from:

A) Ethene

B) Propene

C) Vinyl chloride

D) Benzene

Answer: C) Vinyl chloride
9. In addition polymerization, a small molecule is generally:

A) Eliminated

B) Not eliminated

C) Always produced as water

D) Always produced as CO₂

Answer: B) Not eliminated
10. Condensation polymerization usually involves:

A) Elimination of a small molecule

B) Formation of only gases

C) No chemical reaction

D) Only metals

Answer: A) Elimination of a small molecule
11. Which is an example of a condensation polymer?

A) Polyethene

B) Polypropene

C) Polyester

D) PVC

Answer: C) Polyester
12. Which of the following is a condensation polymer?

A) Polyamide

B) Polyethene

C) Polypropene

D) PVC

Answer: A) Polyamide
13. Which polymer is commonly used for pipes?

A) PVC

B) Protein

C) Starch

D) Cellulose only

Answer: A) PVC
14. Which statement is correct?

A) Monomers are always larger than polymers

B) Polymers consist of repeating monomer units

C) Polymers contain no carbon

D) All polymers are metals

Answer: B) Polymers consist of repeating monomer units
15. Which of the following is an application of dyes?

A) Textile coloring

B) Making oxygen

C) Producing hydrogen gas only

D) Increasing atomic number

Answer: A) Textile coloring

Exam Tips 🎯

  • Remember the definition of an adhesive.
  • Know natural and synthetic adhesives.
  • Remember the main uses of dyes.
  • Learn the difference between monomer and polymer.
  • Remember: ethene → polyethene.
  • Remember: propene → polypropene.
  • Remember: vinyl chloride → PVC.
  • Understand addition vs condensation polymerization.
  • Remember that condensation polymerization generally eliminates a small molecule.
  • Revise polyester and polyamide as important condensation polymers.
