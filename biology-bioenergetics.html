Biology Chapter 4: Bioenergetics

Bioenergetics is the study of energy transformations in living organisms. Cells need energy for growth, movement, transport, synthesis and many other activities.

MDCAT Important: ATP is the major immediate energy currency of the cell.

1. What is Energy?

Energy is the capacity to do work. Living cells continuously transform energy from one form to another.

  • Plants capture light energy during photosynthesis.
  • Cells convert chemical energy into usable forms.
  • ATP provides readily usable energy for cellular work.

2. ATP - The Energy Currency

ATP stands for adenosine triphosphate. It is a nucleotide that stores and transfers energy within cells.

ATP consists of:

  • Adenine
  • Ribose sugar
  • Three phosphate groups

Energy can be released when ATP is hydrolyzed to ADP and inorganic phosphate.

ATP → ADP + Pi + energy

ATP is continuously produced and used by cells rather than being stored in very large quantities.

3. Anabolism and Catabolism

Anabolism

Anabolism consists of metabolic reactions that build larger molecules from smaller molecules.

Anabolic reactions generally require energy.

Catabolism

Catabolism consists of metabolic reactions that break larger molecules into smaller molecules.

Catabolic reactions often release energy that can be captured in ATP or other energy carriers.

Feature Anabolism Catabolism
Function Builds molecules Breaks molecules down
Energy Usually requires energy Often releases energy

4. Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is a series of metabolic reactions through which cells extract energy from nutrients such as glucose.

In aerobic respiration, oxygen is used and glucose is ultimately converted into carbon dioxide and water.

Overall simplified equation:

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

Cellular respiration does not occur in only one step. It consists of several stages.

5. Major Stages of Aerobic Respiration

Stage Main Location
Glycolysis Cytoplasm
Pyruvate oxidation Mitochondrial matrix
Krebs cycle Mitochondrial matrix
Electron transport chain Inner mitochondrial membrane

6. Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the first major stage of cellular respiration. It occurs in the cytoplasm and does not directly require oxygen.

  • One glucose molecule contains six carbon atoms.
  • Glucose is converted into two molecules of pyruvate.
  • ATP is produced.
  • NADH is also produced.
MDCAT Point: Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm, not inside the mitochondrion.

7. Pyruvate Oxidation

Under aerobic conditions, pyruvate enters the mitochondrion and is converted into acetyl-CoA.

During this process:

  • Carbon dioxide is released.
  • NADH is produced.
  • Acetyl-CoA is formed.

8. Krebs Cycle

The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle, occurs in the mitochondrial matrix in eukaryotic cells.

Acetyl-CoA enters the cycle and is gradually oxidized.

  • Carbon dioxide is released.
  • NADH is produced.
  • FADH₂ is produced.
  • A small amount of ATP or an equivalent energy-rich molecule is produced.
Remember: The Krebs cycle produces reduced electron carriers, especially NADH and FADH₂, which later donate electrons to the electron transport chain.

9. Electron Transport Chain

The electron transport chain is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane of eukaryotic cells.

Electrons from NADH and FADH₂ pass through a series of electron carriers.

The energy released is used to pump protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating a proton gradient.

10. Chemiosmosis and ATP Synthase

The proton gradient stores potential energy. Protons flow back across the membrane through an enzyme called ATP synthase.

ATP synthase uses this proton flow to help produce ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate.

Key Concept: Electron transport creates the proton gradient, while ATP synthase uses the gradient to produce ATP.

11. Role of Oxygen

In aerobic respiration, oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain.

Oxygen combines with electrons and protons to form water.

Without sufficient oxygen, the normal aerobic electron transport process cannot continue normally.

12. NADH and FADH₂

NADH and FADH₂ are electron carriers. They transport high-energy electrons to the electron transport chain.

Carrier Role
NADH Transfers high-energy electrons to the ETC
FADH₂ Transfers high-energy electrons to the ETC

13. Fermentation

Fermentation is an anaerobic process that allows glycolysis to continue when oxygen is unavailable or insufficient.

Lactic Acid Fermentation

In some cells and microorganisms, pyruvate can be converted into lactate.

Alcoholic Fermentation

In some microorganisms such as yeast, pyruvate can be converted into ethanol and carbon dioxide.

Type Main Products
Lactic acid fermentation Lactate
Alcoholic fermentation Ethanol + CO₂

14. Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration

Feature Aerobic Anaerobic / Fermentation
Oxygen Required for aerobic respiration Not required
Glucose breakdown More complete Incomplete
Energy yield Much greater Much lower
End products CO₂ and H₂O Depends on pathway

15. Photosynthesis and Respiration

Photosynthesis and cellular respiration are closely related processes in the flow of energy through living systems.

Photosynthesis Cellular Respiration
Stores energy in organic molecules Releases energy from organic molecules
Occurs in chloroplasts of plant cells Glycolysis occurs in cytoplasm; aerobic stages occur in mitochondria
Uses CO₂ and H₂O Produces CO₂ and H₂O in aerobic respiration

17. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. The major immediate energy currency of the cell is:

A) DNA

B) ATP

C) RNA

D) Glucose

Answer: B) ATP

2. Glycolysis occurs in the:

A) Nucleus

B) Cytoplasm

C) Mitochondrial matrix

D) Chloroplast

Answer: B) Cytoplasm

3. Glycolysis converts glucose into:

A) Acetyl-CoA directly

B) Two pyruvate molecules

C) Carbon dioxide only

D) Oxygen

Answer: B) Two pyruvate molecules

4. The Krebs cycle occurs mainly in the:

A) Cytoplasm

B) Mitochondrial matrix

C) Nucleus

D) Ribosome

Answer: B) Mitochondrial matrix

5. The electron transport chain is located in the:

A) Inner mitochondrial membrane

B) Nucleus

C) Cytoplasm

D) Cell wall

Answer: A) Inner mitochondrial membrane

6. The final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration is:

A) Carbon dioxide

B) Oxygen

C) Glucose

D) ATP

Answer: B) Oxygen

7. Which enzyme uses the proton gradient to produce ATP?

A) Amylase

B) ATP synthase

C) Pepsin

D) Lipase

Answer: B) ATP synthase

8. Which molecules carry high-energy electrons to the ETC?

A) DNA and RNA

B) NADH and FADH₂

C) ATP and ADP

D) Glucose and oxygen

Answer: B) NADH and FADH₂

9. Alcoholic fermentation produces:

A) Lactate only

B) Ethanol and carbon dioxide

C) Oxygen and glucose

D) Water only

Answer: B) Ethanol and carbon dioxide

10. Catabolism generally involves:

A) Building larger molecules

B) Breaking down molecules

C) DNA replication only

D) Protein synthesis only

Answer: B) Breaking down molecules
