1. Atomic Spectra

When atoms emit or absorb electromagnetic radiation, the radiation can form a characteristic spectrum called an atomic spectrum.

Each element has its own characteristic spectral lines.

2. Emission Spectrum

An emission spectrum is produced when excited atoms release energy in the form of electromagnetic radiation.

The emitted radiation may appear as separate bright lines.

3. Absorption Spectrum

An absorption spectrum is produced when atoms absorb specific wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation.

It appears as dark lines against a continuous spectrum.

4. Energy Levels in an Atom

Electrons in an atom can exist only in certain allowed energy levels.

These energy levels are often represented by the principal quantum number n.

When an electron moves from a higher energy level to a lower energy level, a photon is emitted.

When an electron absorbs suitable energy, it can move from a lower energy level to a higher energy level.

5. Energy of an Emitted Photon

The energy of the emitted or absorbed photon is equal to the difference between the two energy levels.

ΔE = E₂ - E₁

The photon energy is also given by:

E = hf

6. Hydrogen Spectrum

The hydrogen atom produces a line spectrum because its electron can occupy specific energy levels.

When the electron changes from one allowed energy level to another, radiation of a particular wavelength is emitted or absorbed.

7. Spectral Series

The hydrogen spectrum contains several spectral series.

  • Lyman series: transitions ending at n = 1
  • Balmer series: transitions ending at n = 2
  • Paschen series: transitions ending at n = 3

The Balmer series contains spectral lines mainly in the visible region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

8. Bohr Model of the Atom

According to Bohr's model, electrons move around the nucleus in certain allowed orbits with definite energies.

An electron does not continuously lose energy while it remains in an allowed stationary state.

Radiation is emitted or absorbed when an electron changes from one energy level to another.

9. Rydberg Equation

The wavelengths of spectral lines of hydrogen can be described using the Rydberg equation.

1/λ = R(1/n₁² - 1/n₂²)

Here, R is the Rydberg constant and n₂ is greater than n₁.

📝 Practice MCQs

1. The spectrum produced by excited atoms releasing radiation is called:

A) Absorption spectrum

B) Emission spectrum

C) Continuous spectrum only

D) Sound spectrum

Answer: B) Emission spectrum

2. When an electron moves from a higher energy level to a lower energy level:

A) A photon is emitted

B) A photon is destroyed

C) No energy change occurs

D) The nucleus disappears

Answer: A) A photon is emitted

3. The Balmer series of hydrogen mainly lies in the:

A) Visible region

B) Microwave region

C) Radio region

D) X-ray region only

Answer: A) Visible region

4. The energy of a photon is given by:

A) E = ma

B) E = hf

C) E = IR

D) E = Pt

Answer: B) E = hf

5. The Lyman series consists of transitions ending at:

A) n = 1

B) n = 2

C) n = 3

D) n = 4

Answer: A) n = 1

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Atomic spectra are characteristic of elements.
  • Emission spectrum contains bright spectral lines.
  • Absorption spectrum contains dark lines.
  • Electrons occupy specific energy levels.
  • Electron transition between levels involves photons.
  • Photon energy: E = hf.
  • Balmer series mainly lies in the visible region.
  • Lyman series ends at n = 1.
  • Bohr proposed allowed stationary energy states.
  • Hydrogen spectral lines can be described by the Rydberg equation.
