Official Chemistry Unit 5

Solid

This StudyHub topic covers the important concepts of solid state chemistry for MDCAT preparation, including crystalline solids, ionic and molecular crystals, crystal lattice and lattice energy.

MDCAT Focus: Understand the difference between crystalline and molecular solids, crystal lattice, ionic crystal structure and lattice energy.

1. What is a Solid?

A solid is a state of matter in which particles are closely packed and have relatively fixed positions.

Solids have both a definite shape and a definite volume.

Important Properties of Solids

  • Definite shape
  • Definite volume
  • Particles are closely packed
  • Strong attractive forces between particles
  • Very low compressibility
  • Particles mainly vibrate around fixed positions
Solid: A state of matter having a definite shape and definite volume because its particles are strongly held together.

2. Crystalline Solids

A crystalline solid has particles arranged in a regular, repeating three-dimensional pattern.

Crystalline solid: A solid in which particles have an ordered and repeating arrangement.

Examples

  • Sodium chloride (NaCl)
  • Diamond
  • Quartz
  • Many metals

Characteristics

  • Regular arrangement of particles
  • Definite geometric structure
  • Usually have a sharp melting point
  • Long-range order
Crystalline solids have an organized arrangement of particles, unlike amorphous solids which do not have long-range order.

3. Crystal Lattice

A crystal lattice is the regular three-dimensional arrangement of particles in a crystalline solid.

Crystal lattice: A regular repeating arrangement of particles throughout a crystal.

Depending on the type of crystal, the particles in the lattice may be atoms, ions or molecules.

Examples

Crystal Type Particles in Lattice Example
Ionic crystal Ions NaCl
Molecular crystal Molecules Ice
Atomic/network crystal Atoms Diamond

4. Ionic Crystals

Ionic crystals consist of positively charged ions (cations) and negatively charged ions (anions) arranged in a regular lattice.

The ions are held together by strong electrostatic attractions.

Ionic crystal: A crystalline solid made up of oppositely charged ions arranged in a regular pattern.

Example: Sodium Chloride

Sodium chloride contains Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. Each ion is surrounded by oppositely charged ions in the crystal.

NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻

Properties of Ionic Crystals

  • High melting points
  • Hard and brittle
  • Strong electrostatic attractions
  • Conduct electricity when molten
  • Conduct electricity when dissolved in water
  • Generally do not conduct electricity in solid form

5. Factors Affecting the Shape of Ionic Crystals

The arrangement and shape of an ionic crystal depend on several factors, especially the sizes and charges of the ions.

Important Factors

  • Relative sizes of cations and anions
  • Charges on the ions
  • Ratio of cations to anions
  • Electrostatic attractions
  • Coordination of ions in the lattice
Important: Ionic crystal structures depend strongly on the relative sizes and charges of the ions.

Ion Size

The size relationship between positive and negative ions affects how efficiently they can fit together in the crystal lattice.

Larger differences in ionic size can lead to different coordination arrangements and crystal structures.

6. Molecular Crystals

Molecular crystals are solids in which molecules occupy the positions of the crystal lattice.

Molecular crystal: A crystalline solid whose lattice points are occupied by molecules.

Forces in Molecular Crystals

Molecules in molecular crystals are held together by intermolecular forces such as:

  • London dispersion forces
  • Dipole-dipole forces
  • Hydrogen bonding

Examples

  • Ice (solid H₂O)
  • Solid CO₂
  • Iodine (I₂)

General Properties

  • Usually lower melting points than ionic crystals
  • Generally poor electrical conductors
  • Held together by intermolecular forces

7. Ionic Crystals vs Molecular Crystals

Property Ionic Crystals Molecular Crystals
Particles Ions Molecules
Main attraction Electrostatic attraction Intermolecular forces
Melting point Generally high Generally lower
Electrical conductivity Conduct when molten or dissolved Generally poor
Example NaCl Ice

8. Lattice Energy

Lattice energy is related to the strength of attraction between oppositely charged ions in an ionic crystal.

Lattice energy: The energy associated with the formation or separation of an ionic crystal lattice from its gaseous ions.

Different textbooks may define the sign of lattice energy differently depending on whether lattice formation or separation is being considered. For MDCAT, focus on its relationship with the strength of ionic attraction.

Factors Affecting Lattice Energy

  • Charge of the ions
  • Distance between the ions
  • Size of the ions
Higher ionic charges → Stronger attraction → Greater lattice energy
Smaller ionic size → Smaller ion-to-ion distance → Stronger attraction

9. Effect of Ionic Charge

Greater ionic charges produce stronger electrostatic attractions.

For example, attractions involving ions with charges +2 and −2 are generally stronger than those involving +1 and −1, when other factors are comparable.

Higher charges on ions generally lead to stronger ionic attraction and higher lattice energy.

10. Effect of Ionic Size

When ions are smaller, their centers can be closer together. This generally increases electrostatic attraction.

When ions are larger, the distance between their centers is generally greater, reducing the attraction when charge is constant.

Smaller ions → stronger attraction → generally higher lattice energy

11. Quick Revision

  • Solids have definite shape and definite volume.
  • Crystalline solids have an ordered repeating arrangement.
  • A crystal lattice is a regular three-dimensional arrangement.
  • Ionic crystals contain cations and anions.
  • NaCl is an example of an ionic crystal.
  • Molecular crystals contain molecules in their lattice.
  • Ice is a molecular crystal.
  • Ionic crystals are held together by electrostatic attractions.
  • Molecular crystals are held together by intermolecular forces.
  • Ionic crystal structure depends on ion size and charge.
  • Lattice energy is related to ionic attraction.
  • Higher ionic charge generally increases lattice energy.
  • Smaller ions generally have stronger ionic attraction.

12. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. A solid has:
  1. No definite shape or volume
  2. Definite shape and definite volume
  3. Definite shape but no volume
  4. Definite volume but no shape
Answer: B
2. A crystalline solid has particles arranged in:
  1. A completely random pattern
  2. A regular repeating pattern
  3. A gaseous pattern
  4. No pattern
Answer: B
3. The regular arrangement of particles in a crystal is called:
  1. Gas pressure
  2. Crystal lattice
  3. Evaporation
  4. Vapor pressure
Answer: B
4. NaCl is an example of a:
  1. Molecular crystal
  2. Ionic crystal
  3. Liquid crystal only
  4. Gas crystal
Answer: B
5. An ionic crystal contains:
  1. Only neutral molecules
  2. Cations and anions
  3. Only electrons
  4. Only neutrons
Answer: B
6. The main attraction in an ionic crystal is:
  1. Electrostatic attraction
  2. Gravitational force
  3. Nuclear force
  4. Magnetic force only
Answer: A
7. Which is a molecular crystal?
  1. NaCl
  2. Ice
  3. MgO
  4. KCl
Answer: B
8. Molecular crystals are mainly held together by:
  1. Intermolecular forces
  2. Nuclear forces
  3. Metallic bonds only
  4. Gravitational forces
Answer: A
9. Increasing ionic charge generally causes lattice energy to:
  1. Decrease
  2. Increase
  3. Become zero
  4. Remain unrelated
Answer: B
10. Smaller ions generally produce:
  1. Weaker ionic attraction
  2. Stronger ionic attraction
  3. No attraction
  4. Only molecular attraction
Answer: B
11. Which factor affects the shape and arrangement of ionic crystals?
  1. Ionic size
  2. Ion charge
  3. Ratio of ions
  4. All of these
Answer: D
12. Ionic solids generally have:
  1. Very low melting points
  2. High melting points
  3. No melting point
  4. Only gaseous properties
Answer: B
13. Solid ionic compounds generally conduct electricity:
  1. Very well in the solid state
  2. When molten or dissolved
  3. Never
  4. Only at 0°C
Answer: B
14. Which particles occupy the lattice positions in an ionic crystal?
  1. Positive and negative ions
  2. Only molecules
  3. Only electrons
  4. Only atoms of hydrogen
Answer: A
15. Lattice energy is strongly affected by:
  1. Ionic charge and ionic size
  2. Color only
  3. Shape of container
  4. Atmospheric humidity only
Answer: A

13. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember the definition of a crystal lattice.
  • Know the difference between ionic and molecular crystals.
  • NaCl is an important example of an ionic crystal.
  • Ice is an important example of a molecular crystal.
  • Higher ionic charge generally means stronger attraction.
  • Smaller ions generally give stronger ionic attraction.
  • Know the factors affecting ionic crystal structure.
  • Remember that ionic compounds conduct electricity when molten or dissolved.
Golden Point: Lattice energy mainly depends on the charges and sizes of the ions.
