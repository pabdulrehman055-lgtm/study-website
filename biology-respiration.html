🫁 Respiration

Definition: Respiration is the process by which the body obtains oxygen and removes carbon dioxide. At the cellular level, respiration also refers to the processes through which cells obtain usable energy from nutrients.
MDCAT Focus: Learn the human respiratory system, mechanism of breathing, gas exchange and the effects of smoking.

1. Human Respiratory System

The human respiratory system is responsible for taking oxygen into the body and removing carbon dioxide.

Main Parts

  1. Nose and nasal cavity
  2. Pharynx
  3. Larynx
  4. Trachea
  5. Bronchi
  6. Bronchioles
  7. Alveoli
  8. Lungs
  9. Diaphragm

2. Nose and Nasal Cavity

Air usually enters the respiratory system through the nose.

  • Nasal hairs help trap dust particles.
  • Mucus traps particles and microorganisms.
  • The nasal cavity helps warm incoming air.
  • Moisture is added to the inhaled air.

3. Pharynx and Larynx

Pharynx

The pharynx is a passage shared by the respiratory and digestive systems.

Larynx

The larynx is commonly called the voice box. It contains the vocal cords and forms part of the airway.

Remember: Larynx = voice box.

4. Trachea

The trachea, or windpipe, carries air from the larynx toward the bronchi.

  • It is supported by rings of cartilage.
  • The cartilage helps keep the airway open.
  • The inner lining contains mucus and cilia.
  • Mucus traps particles while cilia help move them upward.

5. Bronchi and Bronchioles

The trachea divides into two main bronchi. One bronchus enters each lung.

Inside the lungs, the bronchi divide repeatedly into smaller tubes called bronchioles.

Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli

6. Alveoli 🫁

Alveoli are tiny air sacs located at the ends of the respiratory passages in the lungs.

Adaptations of Alveoli

  • They are very numerous, providing a large surface area.
  • Their walls are very thin.
  • They have a rich supply of blood capillaries.
  • They provide a short diffusion distance for gases.
  • They have a moist surface that supports gas exchange.
Exam Point: Gas exchange occurs mainly across the alveolar-capillary surface.

7. Gas Exchange

Gas exchange occurs by diffusion because gases move from areas of higher partial pressure to areas of lower partial pressure.

In the Lungs

  • Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into the blood.
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into the alveoli.

In Body Tissues

  • Oxygen moves from blood into tissues.
  • Carbon dioxide moves from tissues into blood.

8. Mechanism of Breathing

Breathing consists mainly of inhalation and exhalation.

Inhalation

  • The diaphragm contracts and moves downward.
  • The external intercostal muscles contract.
  • Thoracic volume increases.
  • Pressure inside the lungs decreases.
  • Air moves into the lungs.

Exhalation

  • The diaphragm relaxes and moves upward.
  • Thoracic volume decreases.
  • Pressure inside the lungs increases.
  • Air moves out of the lungs.
Easy Rule:
Volume ↑ → Pressure ↓ → Air enters
Volume ↓ → Pressure ↑ → Air leaves

9. Diaphragm

The diaphragm is a dome-shaped sheet of skeletal muscle located below the lungs.

Condition Diaphragm Effect
Inhalation Contracts and flattens Thoracic volume increases
Exhalation Relaxes and becomes dome-shaped Thoracic volume decreases

10. Transport of Oxygen

Oxygen entering the lungs diffuses into the blood and is transported mainly by hemoglobin inside red blood cells.

Hemoglobin combines reversibly with oxygen to form oxyhemoglobin.

Remember: Most oxygen is transported in blood bound to hemoglobin.

11. Transport of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide produced by cellular metabolism is transported from tissues to the lungs.

Most carbon dioxide is transported in the blood in the form of bicarbonate ions. Some is carried dissolved in plasma or bound to proteins.

12. Respiration and Cellular Energy

Cells use cellular respiration to obtain usable energy from nutrients such as glucose.

Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases a large amount of usable energy from glucose.

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

The main stages of aerobic cellular respiration are associated with the cytoplasm and mitochondria.

Do not confuse breathing with cellular respiration. Breathing is the physical movement of air, while cellular respiration involves chemical reactions that release usable energy.

13. Effect of Smoking 🚭

Smoking damages the respiratory system and can seriously affect lung function.

Major Effects

  • Damages cilia in the airways.
  • Increases mucus production.
  • Can cause chronic coughing.
  • Damages lung tissue.
  • Reduces efficient gas exchange.
  • Increases the risk of chronic respiratory diseases.
  • Greatly increases the risk of lung cancer.

Carbon Monoxide

Tobacco smoke contains carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide binds strongly to hemoglobin and reduces the blood's ability to transport oxygen effectively.

MDCAT Tip: Smoking → cilia damage + increased mucus + lung damage + reduced oxygen transport + increased disease risk.

14. Breathing vs Cellular Respiration

Breathing Cellular Respiration
Physical movement of air Chemical process in cells
Involves inhalation and exhalation Releases usable energy from nutrients
Occurs through respiratory system Occurs through metabolic pathways in cells

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Nose → filters, warms and moistens air.
  • Larynx → voice box.
  • Trachea → windpipe.
  • Trachea divides into two bronchi.
  • Bronchi divide into bronchioles.
  • Alveoli are the main sites of gas exchange.
  • Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into blood.
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into alveoli.
  • Diaphragm contracts during normal inhalation.
  • Diaphragm relaxes during normal exhalation.
  • Most oxygen is carried by hemoglobin.
  • Most carbon dioxide is transported as bicarbonate.
  • Smoking damages cilia and lung tissue.
  • Carbon monoxide reduces effective oxygen transport.
  • Breathing and cellular respiration are different processes.

📝 MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. The main site of gas exchange in human lungs is:

A) Trachea
B) Bronchi
C) Alveoli
D) Larynx

Answer: C) Alveoli

2. During normal inhalation, the diaphragm:

A) Relaxes and moves upward
B) Contracts and moves downward
C) Does not move
D) Contracts and moves upward

Answer: B) Contracts and moves downward

3. Which structure is commonly called the voice box?

A) Pharynx
B) Larynx
C) Trachea
D) Bronchus

Answer: B) Larynx

4. Oxygen moves from alveoli into blood mainly by:

A) Active transport
B) Osmosis
C) Diffusion
D) Endocytosis

Answer: C) Diffusion

5. Which substance carries most oxygen in human blood?

A) Plasma proteins
B) Hemoglobin
C) Platelets
D) White blood cells

Answer: B) Hemoglobin

6. The trachea divides into:

A) Alveoli
B) Two bronchi
C) Two bronchioles
D) Two alveolar sacs

Answer: B) Two bronchi

7. Which feature of alveoli helps rapid gas exchange?

A) Thick walls
B) Small surface area
C) Thin walls and large surface area
D) Lack of blood supply

Answer: C) Thin walls and large surface area

8. During inhalation, the volume of the thoracic cavity:

A) Decreases
B) Increases
C) Remains constant
D) Becomes zero

Answer: B) Increases

9. Carbon dioxide is transported in blood mainly as:

A) Oxygen
B) Bicarbonate ions
C) Hemoglobin only
D) Glucose

Answer: B) Bicarbonate ions

10. Carbon monoxide in cigarette smoke affects oxygen transport because it:

A) Destroys all RBCs immediately
B) Binds strongly to hemoglobin
C) Increases oxygen concentration in blood
D) Converts oxygen into glucose

Answer: B) Binds strongly to hemoglobin

11. Which structure prevents food from entering the airway during swallowing?

A) Epiglottis
B) Alveolus
C) Diaphragm
D) Bronchiole

Answer: A) Epiglottis

12. Smoking can damage which structures that normally help clear mucus?

A) Cilia
B) RBCs
C) Nephrons
D) Villi

Answer: A) Cilia

13. During normal exhalation, the diaphragm:

A) Contracts and flattens
B) Relaxes and moves upward
C) Contracts and moves downward
D) Stops functioning

Answer: B) Relaxes and moves upward

14. Which statement correctly describes breathing?

A) It is only a chemical reaction in mitochondria
B) It involves movement of air into and out of the lungs
C) It produces antibodies
D) It occurs only in red blood cells

Answer: B) It involves movement of air into and out of the lungs

15. Aerobic cellular respiration requires:

A) Oxygen
B) Nitrogen gas only
C) Carbon monoxide
D) No nutrients

Answer: A) Oxygen

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the pathway of air from nose to alveoli.
  • Know the structure and function of alveoli.
  • Understand why gas exchange occurs by diffusion.
  • Remember diaphragm movement during inhalation and exhalation.
  • Know the role of hemoglobin in oxygen transport.
  • Remember that most CO₂ is transported as bicarbonate.
  • Know the harmful effects of smoking.
  • Do not confuse breathing with cellular respiration.
