Acellular Life

Acellular life refers to biological entities that do not have a typical cellular structure. Viruses are the most important acellular entities studied in MDCAT Biology.

Important: Viruses are generally considered acellular and cannot reproduce independently. They depend on a host cell for replication.

1. What Are Viruses?

A virus is a microscopic infectious agent made mainly of genetic material surrounded by a protein coat. Viruses can infect humans, animals, plants and microorganisms.

Important characteristics

  • Viruses are acellular.
  • They do not have cytoplasm or cellular organelles.
  • They contain DNA or RNA as genetic material.
  • They have a protein coat called a capsid.
  • They reproduce only inside suitable host cells.
  • They are much smaller than most bacteria.
MDCAT Point: A virus is not considered a typical living cell because it lacks cellular organization and cannot reproduce independently.

2. Structure of a Virus

The basic structure of a virus consists of genetic material and a protective protein covering.

Genetic Material

Viral genetic material may be either DNA or RNA. A particular virus normally contains one type of nucleic acid.

Capsid

The capsid is the protein coat surrounding viral genetic material. It protects the nucleic acid and can help the virus attach to host cells.

Envelope

Some viruses have an outer lipid membrane called an envelope. Viral envelope proteins can help the virus recognize and enter host cells.

3. Viral Replication

Viruses cannot reproduce on their own. They use the machinery of a host cell to produce new viral components.

Main stages

  1. Attachment: The virus attaches to specific receptors on the host cell.
  2. Entry: Viral material enters the host cell.
  3. Uncoating: The viral genetic material is released.
  4. Synthesis: Viral nucleic acid and proteins are produced using host-cell machinery.
  5. Assembly: New virus particles are assembled.
  6. Release: New viruses leave the host cell.
Remember: Attachment → Entry → Uncoating → Synthesis → Assembly → Release

4. Lytic Cycle

In the lytic cycle, a virus rapidly produces new virus particles and eventually causes destruction of the host cell.

Basic sequence

  • Virus attaches to the host cell.
  • Viral genetic material enters the cell.
  • Viral components are produced.
  • New viruses are assembled.
  • The host cell may lyse and release the new viruses.

5. Lysogenic Cycle

In the lysogenic cycle, viral genetic material can become associated with the host cell's genetic material and remain relatively inactive for a period.

In bacteriophages, integrated viral DNA is called a prophage.

Difference: Lytic cycle → active replication and host-cell destruction.
Lysogenic cycle → viral genetic material can remain integrated with host DNA before entering an active replication phase.

6. Bacteriophages

Bacteriophages, or phages, are viruses that infect bacteria.

General structure

  • Head containing genetic material
  • Protein capsid
  • Tail structure in many phages
  • Structures that help attach to bacterial cells
MDCAT Point: Bacteriophage = virus that infects bacteria.

7. HIV

HIV stands for Human Immunodeficiency Virus. It attacks important cells of the human immune system, especially CD4 T lymphocytes.

Important facts

  • HIV is an RNA virus.
  • It is a retrovirus.
  • It uses the enzyme reverse transcriptase.
  • It primarily targets CD4 T cells.
  • Progressive HIV infection can severely weaken the immune system.

8. AIDS

AIDS means Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome. It is the advanced stage of HIV infection in which the immune system becomes severely damaged.

Transmission of HIV

  • Unprotected sexual contact
  • Contaminated blood or blood products
  • Sharing contaminated needles
  • Mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding
HIV is not spread by ordinary casual contact such as shaking hands, hugging or sharing normal household space.

9. Reverse Transcriptase

Reverse transcriptase is an enzyme used by retroviruses such as HIV to make DNA from an RNA template.

This DNA can then become associated with the host cell's genetic material.

Exam Tip: Reverse transcriptase → RNA → DNA

10. Why Viruses Are Different From Cells

  • No cellular membrane system like a typical cell
  • No cytoplasm
  • No independent metabolism
  • No cellular organelles such as mitochondria or ribosomes
  • Cannot independently reproduce
  • Depend on host cells for replication

Quick Revision

  • Virus = acellular infectious entity.
  • Capsid = protein coat of a virus.
  • Viral genome = DNA or RNA.
  • Some viruses have a lipid envelope.
  • Viruses require host cells for replication.
  • Bacteriophage infects bacteria.
  • HIV attacks mainly CD4 T cells.
  • HIV is a retrovirus.
  • Reverse transcriptase makes DNA from an RNA template.
  • AIDS is the advanced stage of HIV infection.

MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Which of the following is acellular?

A) Bacterium
B) Virus
C) Protozoan
D) Fungus

Answer: B) Virus

2. The protein coat of a virus is called:

A) Capsule
B) Capsid
C) Cell wall
D) Envelope

Answer: B) Capsid

3. Viral genetic material can be:

A) Only DNA
B) Only RNA
C) DNA or RNA
D) Protein only

Answer: C) DNA or RNA

4. Viruses reproduce:

A) Independently
B) Inside host cells
C) Only in water
D) In soil

Answer: B) Inside host cells

5. A virus that infects bacteria is called:

A) Retrovirus
B) Bacteriophage
C) Viroid
D) Prion

Answer: B) Bacteriophage

6. HIV primarily attacks:

A) Red blood cells
B) Platelets
C) CD4 T cells
D) Bone cells

Answer: C) CD4 T cells

7. HIV belongs to which type of virus?

A) Retrovirus
B) Bacteriophage
C) Adenovirus only
D) Plant virus

Answer: A) Retrovirus

8. Reverse transcriptase converts:

A) DNA into protein
B) RNA into DNA
C) Protein into RNA
D) DNA into lipid

Answer: B) RNA into DNA

9. Which cycle commonly results in destruction of the host cell?

A) Lytic cycle
B) Lysogenic cycle
C) Krebs cycle
D) Calvin cycle

Answer: A) Lytic cycle

10. Viral DNA integrated into bacterial DNA is called:

A) Plasmid
B) Prophage
C) Ribosome
D) Capsule

Answer: B) Prophage

11. Which structure protects viral genetic material?

A) Capsid
B) Ribosome
C) Mitochondrion
D) Nucleus

Answer: A) Capsid

12. AIDS is associated with infection by:

A) HIV
B) Influenza virus
C) Bacteriophage
D) Plasmodium

Answer: A) HIV

13. Which is absent in viruses?

A) Genetic material
B) Protein coat
C) Cellular organelles
D) Nucleic acid

Answer: C) Cellular organelles

14. Which statement is correct?

A) All viruses contain both DNA and RNA

B) Viruses can reproduce independently

C) Viruses depend on host cells for replication

D) Viruses are made only of carbohydrates

Answer: C) Viruses depend on host cells for replication

15. Which enzyme is especially important in HIV replication?

A) Amylase
B) Reverse transcriptase
C) Pepsin
D) Lipase

Answer: B) Reverse transcriptase

Exam Tips ⭐

  • Remember that viruses are acellular.
  • Capsid is the viral protein coat.
  • Viral genetic material can be DNA or RNA.
  • Bacteriophages infect bacteria.
  • HIV is a retrovirus.
  • HIV mainly targets CD4 T cells.
  • Reverse transcriptase makes DNA from RNA.
  • Know the difference between lytic and lysogenic cycles.
