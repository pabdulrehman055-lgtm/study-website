Unit 13: Fundamental Principles of Organic Chemistry

Definition: Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry that mainly deals with carbon-containing compounds, especially hydrocarbons and their derivatives.
MDCAT Focus: Learn the definition and classification of organic compounds, important functional groups, homologous series and the basic idea of stereoisomerism.

1. Why Carbon is Special

Carbon is the central element of organic chemistry because it can form strong covalent bonds with other carbon atoms as well as hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur and halogens.

Carbon has four valence electrons and commonly forms four covalent bonds.

Carbon can form chains, branches and rings. This property is called catenation.

2. Catenation

Catenation is the ability of an element, especially carbon, to form covalent bonds with atoms of the same element.
Example:

Carbon can form chains such as:

C — C — C — C

Extensive catenation is one major reason why millions of organic compounds exist.

3. Classification of Organic Compounds

Organic compounds can be classified in several ways depending on their carbon skeleton and functional groups.

Class General Description
Open-chain compounds Carbon atoms form straight or branched chains
Cyclic compounds Carbon atoms form rings
Aliphatic compounds Generally non-aromatic carbon compounds
Aromatic compounds Contain aromatic ring systems such as benzene
Heterocyclic compounds Rings containing carbon and at least one other element

4. Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.
Type Main Feature Example
Alkanes Single C–C bonds CH₄
Alkenes At least one C=C bond C₂H₄
Alkynes At least one C≡C bond C₂H₂
Aromatic hydrocarbons Aromatic ring system C₆H₆

5. Functional Groups

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms in an organic molecule that gives the molecule characteristic chemical properties and reactions.
Functional Group Class Example
–OH Alcohol CH₃OH
–CHO Aldehyde CH₃CHO
>C=O Ketone CH₃COCH₃
–COOH Carboxylic acid CH₃COOH
–X Haloalkane CH₃Cl
–NH₂ Amine CH₃NH₂
–COOR Ester CH₃COOCH₃
–O– Ether CH₃OCH₃
Functional groups are extremely important because they determine many characteristic reactions of organic compounds.

6. Homologous Series

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds having the same functional group and general formula, with successive members differing by a –CH₂– unit.
Example: Alkanes

CH₄ → methane
C₂H₆ → ethane
C₃H₈ → propane
C₄H₁₀ → butane
Successive members of a homologous series differ by CH₂.

7. Characteristics of a Homologous Series

  • Members have the same general formula.
  • Members contain the same functional group.
  • Successive members differ by CH₂.
  • They show similar chemical properties.
  • Physical properties generally change gradually with molecular size.

8. Isomerism

Isomers are compounds that have the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms.

Isomerism explains why compounds with the same molecular formula can have different structures and properties.

9. Structural Isomerism

Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different connectivity or arrangement of atoms.
Example: C₄H₁₀

n-Butane:
CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃

Isobutane:
(CH₃)₃CH

Both have the molecular formula C₄H₁₀ but different structures.

10. Types of Structural Isomerism

Type Main Difference
Chain isomerism Different carbon skeletons
Position isomerism Different position of functional group or multiple bond
Functional group isomerism Different functional groups with the same molecular formula

11. Chain Isomerism

Chain isomerism occurs when compounds have the same molecular formula but different arrangements of the carbon skeleton.
Example:

C₄H₁₀ has:
straight-chain butane
branched-chain isobutane

12. Position Isomerism

Position isomerism occurs when the same functional group or multiple bond occurs at different positions in the carbon chain.
Example:

1-propanol: CH₃–CH₂–CH₂OH

2-propanol: CH₃–CHOH–CH₃

Both have the molecular formula C₃H₈O but the hydroxyl group is at a different position.

13. Functional Group Isomerism

Functional group isomers have the same molecular formula but different functional groups.
Example:

C₂H₆O can represent:

Ethanol → CH₃CH₂OH
Dimethyl ether → CH₃OCH₃

14. Stereoisomerism

Stereoisomers have the same molecular formula and the same connectivity of atoms but differ in the three- dimensional arrangement of atoms in space.

Stereoisomerism is particularly important when studying compounds that have restricted rotation or chiral centers.

15. Geometrical Isomerism

Geometrical isomerism can occur when rotation around a bond is restricted, commonly around a carbon-carbon double bond.

Example:

Some alkenes can exist as cis and trans forms.
A C=C double bond restricts free rotation, which can allow different spatial arrangements.

16. Optical Isomerism

Optical isomerism is associated with molecules that can exist as non-superimposable mirror-image forms called enantiomers.

A carbon atom attached to four different groups is commonly called a chiral center or stereogenic center.
Enantiomers have the same connectivity but differ in their three-dimensional arrangement.

18. Saturated and Unsaturated Compounds

Saturated compounds contain only single carbon- carbon bonds in their carbon skeleton.
Unsaturated compounds contain one or more carbon- carbon double or triple bonds.
Type Bond Example
Saturated C–C Ethane
Unsaturated C=C Ethene
Unsaturated C≡C Ethyne

20. Quick Revision

  • Organic chemistry mainly deals with carbon-containing compounds.
  • Carbon commonly forms four covalent bonds.
  • Catenation is the ability of carbon to bond with itself.
  • Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen.
  • Alkanes contain C–C single bonds.
  • Alkenes contain at least one C=C bond.
  • Alkynes contain at least one C≡C bond.
  • A functional group determines characteristic chemical behavior.
  • Successive members of a homologous series differ by CH₂.
  • Isomers have the same molecular formula but different arrangements.
  • Structural isomers differ in connectivity.
  • Chain isomers have different carbon skeletons.
  • Position isomers have a functional group or multiple bond at different positions.
  • Functional group isomers have different functional groups.
  • Stereoisomers have the same connectivity but different spatial arrangements.
  • Geometrical isomerism can occur around restricted double bonds.
  • Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images.
  • A carbon attached to four different groups can be a chiral center.

21. MDCAT Practice MCQs

1. Organic chemistry mainly deals with:

A. Only metals
B. Carbon-containing compounds
C. Noble gases only
D. Nuclear reactions

Answer: B. Carbon-containing compounds

2. The ability of carbon to form bonds with other carbon atoms is called:

A. Ionization
B. Catenation
C. Reduction
D. Sublimation

Answer: B. Catenation

3. Hydrocarbons contain:

A. Carbon and oxygen
B. Carbon and hydrogen
C. Hydrogen and oxygen
D. Carbon and nitrogen

Answer: B. Carbon and hydrogen

4. Which functional group is present in alcohols?

A. –COOH
B. –CHO
C. –OH
D. –NH₂

Answer: C. –OH

5. The functional group of carboxylic acids is:

A. –OH
B. –COOH
C. –CHO
D. –O–

Answer: B. –COOH

6. Successive members of a homologous series differ by:

A. CH₃
B. CH₂
C. H₂O
D. CO₂

Answer: B. CH₂

7. Compounds having the same molecular formula but different structures are called:

A. Isotopes
B. Isomers
C. Ions
D. Radicals

Answer: B. Isomers

8. C₄H₁₀ can show:

A. Chain isomerism
B. Nuclear isomerism only
C. Ionic bonding
D. Optical activity in all forms

Answer: A. Chain isomerism

9. Which pair represents position isomers?

A. 1-propanol and 2-propanol
B. Methane and ethane
C. Ethane and ethene
D. Benzene and cyclohexane

Answer: A. 1-propanol and 2-propanol

10. Which compound is an alkene?

A. Ethane
B. Ethene
C. Ethyne
D. Methane

Answer: B. Ethene

11. Which compound contains a triple bond?

A. Ethane
B. Ethene
C. Ethyne
D. Methanol

Answer: C. Ethyne

12. The functional group –CHO represents:

A. Alcohol
B. Aldehyde
C. Ketone
D. Ether

Answer: B. Aldehyde

13. Stereoisomers have:

A. Different molecular formulas
B. Different atomic elements
C. Same connectivity but different spatial arrangement
D. No carbon atoms

Answer: C. Same connectivity but different spatial arrangement

14. Geometrical isomerism is commonly associated with:

A. Free rotation around single bonds
B. Restricted rotation around double bonds
C. Ionic compounds only
D. Noble gases

Answer: B. Restricted rotation around double bonds

15. A carbon attached to four different groups can be called a:

A. Chiral center
B. Nucleophile only
C. Catalyst
D. Polymer

Answer: A. Chiral center

22. MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Remember that carbon commonly forms four covalent bonds.
  • Know the meaning of catenation.
  • Memorize the major functional groups and their formulas.
  • Remember that homologous-series members differ by CH₂.
  • Learn the difference between structural and stereoisomerism.
  • Practice chain, position and functional-group isomerism.
  • Know the difference between alkane, alkene and alkyne.
  • Remember that C=C restricts rotation and can produce geometrical isomerism.
  • Understand the basic idea of chiral centers and enantiomers.
  • Practice identifying functional groups from structures.
