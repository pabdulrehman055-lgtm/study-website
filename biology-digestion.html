🍎 Digestion

Definition: Digestion is the process of breaking large, complex food molecules into smaller molecules that can be absorbed and used by the body.
MDCAT Focus: Learn the human digestive system, associated glands and the digestion and absorption of carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

1. Human Digestive System

The digestive system consists of the alimentary canal and associated digestive organs.

Alimentary Canal

  1. Mouth
  2. Pharynx
  3. Esophagus
  4. Stomach
  5. Small intestine
  6. Large intestine
  7. Rectum
  8. Anus
Food pathway:
Mouth → Pharynx → Esophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine → Large Intestine → Rectum → Anus

2. Mouth, Teeth and Tongue

Digestion begins in the mouth.

Teeth

Teeth mechanically break food into smaller pieces. This process is called mechanical digestion.

Tongue

The tongue helps mix food with saliva and forms it into a bolus that can be swallowed.

Saliva

Saliva moistens food and contains the enzyme salivary amylase, which begins the digestion of starch.

3. Pharynx and Esophagus

Pharynx

The pharynx is a passage through which swallowed food moves toward the esophagus.

Esophagus

The esophagus is a muscular tube that carries food from the pharynx to the stomach.

Peristalsis: Rhythmic muscular contractions that push food along the alimentary canal.

4. Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ that stores food and mixes it with gastric juice.

Functions of Stomach

  • Stores food temporarily.
  • Mixes food mechanically.
  • Secretes hydrochloric acid (HCl).
  • Begins significant protein digestion.
  • Helps kill many microorganisms entering with food.

Pepsin

Pepsin is a protease enzyme that begins the digestion of proteins in the stomach.

Remember: Pepsin → Protein digestion in the stomach.

5. Liver and Gallbladder

Liver

The liver is a large digestive-associated organ with many important functions.

One important digestive function is the production of bile.

Bile

  • Bile is produced by the liver.
  • It is stored and concentrated in the gallbladder.
  • It is released into the small intestine.
  • Bile salts help emulsify fats.
Important: Bile is not a digestive enzyme. It helps fat digestion by emulsifying fats into smaller droplets.

6. Pancreas

The pancreas releases pancreatic juice into the small intestine.

Important Pancreatic Enzymes

  • Pancreatic amylase → carbohydrate digestion.
  • Trypsin and other proteases → protein digestion.
  • Pancreatic lipase → fat digestion.

Pancreatic secretions also contain bicarbonate, which helps neutralize acidic chyme entering the small intestine.

7. Small Intestine

The small intestine is the major site of digestion and absorption of nutrients.

Main Regions

  • Duodenum
  • Jejunum
  • Ileum

The duodenum receives bile and pancreatic secretions.

8. Villi and Absorption

The inner surface of the small intestine contains numerous finger-like projections called villi.

Importance of Villi

  • Increase the surface area for absorption.
  • Have a thin epithelial layer.
  • Contain blood capillaries.
  • Contain a lymphatic vessel called a lacteal.
Exam Point: Villi increase the surface area available for absorption.

9. Digestion of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are eventually broken down into simple sugars, especially monosaccharides such as glucose.

Site Important Process
Mouth Salivary amylase begins starch digestion.
Small intestine Pancreatic and intestinal enzymes complete carbohydrate digestion.
Absorption Monosaccharides are absorbed into the blood.

10. Digestion of Proteins

Proteins are digested into smaller peptides and ultimately amino acids.

Site Important Process
Stomach Pepsin begins protein digestion.
Small intestine Pancreatic and intestinal proteases continue digestion.
Absorption Amino acids are absorbed mainly into blood capillaries.

11. Digestion of Fats

Fats are mainly broken down into fatty acids and monoglycerides during digestion.

Role of Bile

Bile salts emulsify large fat droplets into smaller droplets. This increases the surface area available to lipase.

Role of Lipase

Lipase breaks down fats into smaller molecules that can be absorbed.

Remember:
Bile → emulsification of fats
Lipase → enzymatic digestion of fats

12. Large Intestine

The large intestine receives material that remains after most nutrients have been absorbed in the small intestine.

Main Functions

  • Absorbs water.
  • Absorbs some electrolytes.
  • Helps form and store feces.
  • Contains a large population of bacteria.

13. Rectum and Anus

The rectum stores feces temporarily. The anus is the opening through which feces are eliminated.

Defecation: The elimination of feces from the digestive tract.

14. Mechanical vs Chemical Digestion

Mechanical Digestion Chemical Digestion
Physically breaks food into smaller pieces. Uses enzymes and other chemicals to break molecules down.
Chewing is an example. Pepsin breaking down proteins is an example.

15. Important Digestive Enzymes

Enzyme Acts On Produces
Salivary amylase Starch Smaller carbohydrates
Pepsin Proteins Smaller peptides
Pancreatic amylase Starch Smaller carbohydrates
Proteases Proteins/peptides Smaller peptides/amino acids
Lipase Fats Fatty acids and monoglycerides

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Digestion breaks complex food into smaller molecules.
  • Digestion begins in the mouth.
  • Salivary amylase begins starch digestion.
  • Esophagus moves food by peristalsis.
  • Stomach produces HCl and begins protein digestion.
  • Pepsin is important for protein digestion in the stomach.
  • Liver produces bile.
  • Gallbladder stores bile.
  • Bile emulsifies fats but is not an enzyme.
  • Pancreas releases digestive enzymes and bicarbonate.
  • Small intestine is the major site of digestion and absorption.
  • Villi increase the surface area for absorption.
  • Large intestine absorbs water and helps form feces.
  • Rectum stores feces.
  • Anus is the opening for elimination of feces.

📝 MDCAT-Style MCQs

1. Digestion of starch begins in the:

A) Stomach
B) Mouth
C) Large intestine
D) Esophagus

Answer: B) Mouth

2. Which enzyme begins protein digestion in the stomach?

A) Amylase
B) Lipase
C) Pepsin
D) Maltase

Answer: C) Pepsin

3. Bile is produced by the:

A) Pancreas
B) Stomach
C) Liver
D) Small intestine

Answer: C) Liver

4. Bile is stored in the:

A) Pancreas
B) Gallbladder
C) Stomach
D) Rectum

Answer: B) Gallbladder

5. The main function of bile salts is:

A) Protein synthesis
B) Fat emulsification
C) Starch production
D) Blood clotting

Answer: B) Fat emulsification

6. Most nutrient absorption occurs in the:

A) Stomach
B) Small intestine
C) Large intestine
D) Esophagus

Answer: B) Small intestine

7. Villi are important because they:

A) Produce teeth
B) Increase surface area for absorption
C) Produce bile
D) Store feces

Answer: B) Increase surface area for absorption

8. Which organ produces pancreatic juice?

A) Liver
B) Gallbladder
C) Pancreas
D) Stomach

Answer: C) Pancreas

9. Peristalsis is:

A) Chemical digestion of proteins
B) Rhythmic muscular movement of the alimentary canal
C) Absorption of glucose
D) Production of bile

Answer: B) Rhythmic muscular movement of the alimentary canal

10. Which enzyme digests fats?

A) Lipase
B) Pepsin
C) Amylase
D) Trypsin only

Answer: A) Lipase

11. Which organ mainly absorbs water from undigested material?

A) Large intestine
B) Stomach
C) Esophagus
D) Mouth

Answer: A) Large intestine

12. Which substance helps neutralize acidic chyme in the small intestine?

A) Bicarbonate
B) Hemoglobin
C) Bile pigment only
D) Saliva only

Answer: A) Bicarbonate

13. Proteins are ultimately digested into:

A) Fatty acids
B) Amino acids
C) Glucose only
D) Glycerol only

Answer: B) Amino acids

14. Which organ temporarily stores feces?

A) Rectum
B) Liver
C) Stomach
D) Pancreas

Answer: A) Rectum

15. Which statement about bile is correct?

A) It is a protein-digesting enzyme
B) It is produced by the pancreas
C) It helps emulsify fats
D) It digests starch directly

Answer: C) It helps emulsify fats

🎯 MDCAT Exam Tips

  • Memorize the complete pathway of food.
  • Know the functions of liver, gallbladder and pancreas.
  • Remember: bile is not a digestive enzyme.
  • Know the difference between mechanical and chemical digestion.
  • Learn the major digestive enzymes and their substrates.
  • Understand why villi are important for absorption.
  • Remember that the small intestine is the major site of nutrient absorption.
  • Practice food-pathway and enzyme MCQs repeatedly.
